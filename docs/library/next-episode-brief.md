## The angle (familiar but unexpected)
**Familiar:**
- Everyone has heard of the Panama Canal, the shortcut between two oceans.

**Unexpected:**
- The oceans never meet. The canal is a lake about 85 ft (26 m) above the sea, filled only by rain.
- Every ship that crosses climbs a staircase of fresh water and then pours about 50 million gallons
  of it into the sea.
- In 2023 the rain stopped and world trade queued up. In August 2026 a ship paid a reported
  $5.3 million to skip the line.
- NOAA now gives 83% odds of a historic El Niño, and the canal authority says El Niño hits hardest
  the following year. The 2027 dry season is coming, and the reservoir meant to save the canal
  won't be finished in time.

**The promise the video must keep:** show how a 112-year-old machine runs on rain, why it was
built that way, and what happens when the rain doesn't come.

**Core / casual / new:**
- **Core** (money, trade, power): the $5.3M slot, rerouted LPG and the cost of a closed shortcut.
- **Casual** (megaprojects, history, geography): the French disaster, the lake in the sky, the 1913
  blast.
- **New** (everyone): "wait, the canal is fresh water?"

## Wow factors
- **3:00, a number we computed.** One transit ≈ 50 million gallons ≈ 189 million litres ≈ **76
  Olympic pools**, poured into the sea. At about 36 transits a day, that's roughly 2,700 pools a
  day. Build it as a pool-counter tally over the locks.
  - Caveat: the 50M figure is for the original Panamax locks. The 2016 Neopanamax locks reuse
    about 60% via water-saving basins (verify on pancanal.com). State both.
- **6:00, a reconstruction only we show.** A 3D drowning of the Chagres valley: the Gatun Dam
  closes, the valley floods into what was then the world's largest artificial lake, and treetops
  vanish.
  - Then cut to **10 Oct 1913, 2:02 p.m.** Wilson presses a key in Washington, and the signal
    runs by telegraph via Galveston and a submarine cable. The Gamboa dike blows and the lake
    pours into the Culebra Cut.
  - Real 1913 photos of the blast are public domain (Bucknell, Nelson F. Davis collection).

## Fact-risk note
- **No accusation of a named living person or company is central**, so there's no status-ladder
  problem and no legal read is needed for the main line.
- SK Gas is named only as the reported bidder (neutral). Attribute it: "according to Bloomberg".
- **Live numbers (⏱)** move weekly: slots, draft, the Gatun level and the ENSO odds. Lock them on
  VO day, re-check them on publish day, and put "AS OF <date>" on screen.
- **The El Niño claim is a forecast.** Always say "forecasters give 83% odds", never "the strongest
  El Niño ever". The ACP's "the year after" is a pattern, not a promise. Never say "the canal will
  close" or "shut down"; the accurate verbs are "restrict", "cut" and "queue".
- **French death toll.** Sources range from about 5,600 documented to 22,000 estimated. Say "at
  least five thousand, and by some estimates over twenty thousand", and attribute it.
- **Water per transit.** 50–52M gallons applies to the original locks. The Neopanamax basins reuse
  part of it. Don't put a single number in red unless it's tier 1 (ACP) or tier 2.
- **Río Indio** cost and timeline are 2025 projections; check the current ACP status before VO.
  Displacement is contested, so give both the ACP figure and the critics' figure.
- **Politics.** US statements about "taking back" the canal (2025) are optional context. Keep
  them neutral and attributed, or leave them out; they aren't the story.
- **Lane overlap.** Hormuz gets one line only, so the episode doesn't read as "Hormuz 2". The
  story is water and engineering, not a chokepoint war.
