# Veritasium: video autopsies

Veritasium (Derek Muller and team) has the largest documentary outliers in our set and the best
public evidence on packaging (`channel-breakdowns.md` §15, "Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective").
Five of the biggest. Labels as in `fern.md`.

## 1. Why Are 96,000,000 Black Balls on This Reservoir?

`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uxPdPpi5W4o` · **112.7M views** · 12:07 · published 2019-05-10 · 1028K likes (0.91%)

**Measured** [observed, from captions and YouTube's most-replayed data]: 2,331 caption words, **192 wpm** of runtime (manual captions); turn words ('but', 'however', 'until'…) every **22.6 s** median, first at 1:40; most-replayed peaks 5:48 (1.0), 11:23 (0.18), 10:10 (0.11), 8:14 (0.08).

- **Title and thumbnail:** `Why Are [Huge Exact Number] [Objects] on This [Place]?` Thumbnail: Derek on a boat, gesturing at a reservoir carpeted to the horizon with black balls; no text [observed]. The image and the question are the same thing: the most-viewed documentary in our whole set (112.7M).
- **Hook (0:00–0:30):** "These are shade balls. They're being dumped into this water reservoir in Los Angeles. And contrary to what you may have heard, their main purpose is not to reduce evaporation. So, what are they really for?" (0:17) [observed]. Image, misconception, question in 17 s.
- **Structure:** On-location explainer in 9 chapters: the balls → tour → the real reason (bromate, a carcinogen formed when sunlight hits bromide plus chlorine) → choosing balls → engineering → driving through them → water quality → cost → alternatives [observed].
- **Chapters (from the description):** 0:00 The massive shade balls · 1:18 Touring the reservoir · 3:01 Preventing bromate · 4:41 Choosing shade balls · 6:06 Engineering and materials · 7:33 Driving through the balls · 8:18 Water quality and savings · 9:28 Cost and efficiency · 10:40 Alternative designs
- **Re-hooks and timing:** A **question barrage** at 0:57–1:05: "why are they black? Are they safe…? How much do they cost? Do they actually reduce evaporation? And what is their real purpose?" Each question is later a chapter [observed]. The fastest speech in the set (192 wpm), host plus interviewee.
- **Wow moments:** Top peak 5:48 (1.0 against 0.18 for the next): the origin of shade balls, invented to keep birds off ponds near airports. Almost all replay energy sits on one origin-story reveal [observed]. The first quarter's heat is the lowest in the set (0.025): people watched straight through.
- **Ending:** Banter about the balls looking like a pool of boba [observed]. No summary.
- **What we steal:** **One absurd real image + an exact huge number + 'why'** (the formula of the biggest video we have); **the question barrage** that maps to the chapter list; misconception first (the evaporation myth).

## Channel patterns

- **Title tests are live**: two of five videos returned different titles to two clients on the
  same day [observed]. Veritasium repackages its biggest videos years after release.
- **The question barrage** (four or five questions at about 1:00 that become the chapter list) [observed].
- **Misconception first** in the opening line [observed], which matches Muller's own research
  (`channel-breakdowns.md` §15).
- **One reveal can carry a whole video**: the shade-ball origin story is the single replay spike [observed].
- **Personal stakes by self-test** (Derek's blood) [observed].
