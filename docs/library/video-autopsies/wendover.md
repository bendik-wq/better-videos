# Wendover Productions: video autopsies

Wendover (Sam Denby) is the model for dense, systems-level explainers about logistics, aviation
and geography (`channel-breakdowns.md` §8). Five of the biggest, from manual captions where
available, chapters and most-replayed data pulled 2026-10-12. Labels as in `fern.md`.

## 1. Cities at Sea: How Aircraft Carriers Work

`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c0pS3Zx7Fc8` · **14.1M views** · 11:54 · published 2018-12-26 · 159K likes (1.12%)

**Measured** [observed, from captions and YouTube's most-replayed data]: 2,274 caption words, **191 wpm** of runtime (manual captions); turn words ('but', 'however', 'until'…) every **57.5 s** median, first at 1:42; most-replayed peaks 11:46 (1.0), 0:49 (0.55), 11:18 (0.47), 11:04 (0.35).

- **Title and thumbnail:** Metaphor title: `Cities at Sea:` plus the plain `How [System] Works`. Thumbnail: a carrier on a dark sea under a warm sky with HOW AIRCRAFT CARRIERS WORK in large white type and the channel's stripe [observed]. Old-school title-on-thumbnail; the metaphor is in the title, not the image.
- **Hook (0:00–0:30):** Sponsor line first ("This video was made possible by Brilliant…"), then: "A single aircraft carrier is enough to markedly change the level of a nation's military might" [observed]. The cold open is a thesis, not a scene.
- **Structure:** Systems explainer in 10 chapters: why carriers matter → who has them (19 worldwide; the US has the 11 largest) → strike-group defence → refuelling → logistics and mail → the flight deck → crew roles by shirt colour → life aboard → power projection → vulnerability [observed].
- **Chapters (from the description):** 0:00 Strategic role of carriers · 0:48 Carrier operations and scale · 1:24 Carrier strike group defense · 2:21 Refueling and replenishment · 3:43 Logistics and mail delivery · 5:13 Flight deck technology · 6:22 Crew and deck operations · 7:11 Life aboard a carrier · 8:22 Power projection and missions · 9:53 Vulnerability and future
- **Re-hooks and timing:** Measured density is the highest of any long video here (191 wpm). Re-hooks are surprising specifics rather than questions: each carrier has a US mailing address (3:43); deck crews identified by shirt colour (6:22) [observed].
- **Wow moments:** 0:49: the count, 19 carriers in the world and the US has the 11 largest (a ranking reveal). The other peaks (11:04–11:46) are the sponsor's logic puzzle at the end, which people pause to solve [observed].
- **Ending:** Ends in the sponsor's logic quiz, an interactive outro [observed].
- **What we steal:** **The metaphor title** ("Cities at Sea") for a mechanism video; one surprising, concrete logistics fact per chapter (the mailing address); an end-sponsor that is itself a puzzle keeps the outro watched.

- **Structure:** A guided tour down the length of the motorcade, vehicle by vehicle (route security → the secure package → the control car → support units and press → variants) → Marine One → Air Force One → the cost [observed]. **The convoy is the chapter list.**
- **Chapters (from the description):** 0:00 The logistical nightmare · 0:38 Motorcade route security · 2:03 The secure package · 3:48 Presidential protection · 4:45 Motorcade support units · 6:02 Efficiency and Marine One · 6:58 Helicopter transport · 7:49 Air Force One logistics · 8:56 The cost of transport · 10:33 Sponsor and outro

The cost chapter (8:56) pays off the title with public figures: Air Force One at an estimated $206,337 per flight hour [observed].
- **Wow moments:** The body peaks are modest and spread (2:53 the sealed car's equipment, 5:10 the hazmat unit) [observed]. The top peak, 11:26, is inside the sponsor read, which re-uses the video's unit: a $20 domain is "the cost of 1/3 of a second on Air Force One" [observed].
- **Ending:** Squarespace read that ends on the video's own cost unit [observed].
- **What we steal:** **The tour-down-the-object structure** (front to back of a convoy, a ship, a chip); **an oddly exact per-unit number** in the title, paid off with a public figure; **convert the sponsor's price into the video's unit** so the read stays on topic.

## Channel patterns

- **Sponsor named in the first five seconds, read at the end** [observed in 4 of 4 with captions].
  The 2017–2021 Nebula-era model; the open still lands its thesis by 0:20.
- **The densest narration in the set** (158–191 wpm of runtime) and the shortest turn gaps (17–26 s)
  [observed]. Density works when every sentence is a new fact.
- **Computed comparisons are the replayed moments** (14 first-class passengers vs 122 economy) [observed].
- **Thumbnail text complements the title** (title: $2,614 per minute; thumbnail: How the President
  Travels) [observed].
