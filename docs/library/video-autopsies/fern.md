# Fern: video autopsies

Fern (`@Fern-TV`, made by the team behind the German channel Simplicissimus) is the reference
channel for our look (`docs/fern-breakdown.md`). Seven of its biggest and newest videos, read
through their caption transcripts, descriptions and YouTube's most-replayed heatmap, all pulled
with yt-dlp on 2026-10-12. Quotes are kept short. Data labels: **[observed]** = what the captions,
chapters, heatmap or thumbnail show; **[inferred]** = our reading of why.

How to read the measured line: "wpm of runtime" is caption words ÷ video length, so it includes
music-only stretches; "turn words" counts sentence-level *but / however / yet / except / until /
instead*; heatmap peaks are YouTube's "most replayed" points (0–1, relative to the video's own
maximum), not retention. The first 4% of each video is excluded because every video peaks at 0:00.

## 1. The Kids Who Hacked The CIA

`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmtFtWVrxFE` · **20.1M views** · 23:05 · published 2024-03-17 · 377K likes (1.87%)

**Measured** [observed, from captions and YouTube's most-replayed data]: 3,789 caption words, **164 wpm** of runtime (auto captions); turn words ('but', 'however', 'until'…) every **38.1 s** median, first at 0:26; most-replayed peaks 15:00 (1.0), 7:23 (0.99), 9:00 (0.69), 13:09 (0.56).

- **Title and thumbnail:** Four-word title, underdog vs institution (`The Kids Who [Verb] [Giant]`). Thumbnail is a cork evidence board: two teenage faces with black bars over the eyes, red string to a CIA seal, WikiLeaks and Anonymous logos, and a red **WANTED** label [observed]. Not the object-in-the-void look: Fern's biggest thumbnails are high-contrast collages with one red element.
- **Hook (0:00–0:30):** Present-tense dated scene: "It's October 2015, a chilly day in autumn. John Brennan receives a weird phone call." The prank call is replayed as a dialogue, then the turn at 0:26 ("But John Brennan happens to be one of the most powerful men on Earth. The head of the CIA."). By 0:45 the caller demands "$5 trillion"; by 1:10 the reveal that the caller is a 15-year-old in his bedroom in the West Midlands [observed].
- **Structure:** Investigation told as a **dual protagonist braid**: Default (US) and Kraka (UK) are introduced in separate chapters, converge on the CIA director's email, then are caught one at a time. In medias res → origin of each kid → escalation ladder (trolling → hacktivism → crypto → Snowden → officials) → the hack → crackdown → legacy [observed].
- **Chapters (from the description):** 0:00 The CIA director hack · 2:00 Rise of the young hacker · 3:54 From trolling to activism · 7:26 Hacktivism and crypto · 9:12 The Snowden influence · 12:14 Targeting high officials · 15:23 Chaos and leaked documents · 17:56 The inevitable crackdown · 20:51 Consequences and legacy
- **Re-hooks and timing:** The cold open is a rapid-fire question exchange (seven questions in 40 s). Chapter starts are time-and-place cards ("2008 in Virginia…", "England, 2015."). Sponsor (Incogni) sits at about 6:00–7:26 and is bridged from the story's own subject, data privacy [observed]. Measured turn-word gap 38 s, close to our 30–40 s cadence.
- **Wow moments:** Top peak 15:00: the social-engineering step-by-step, where a support technician hands over Brennan's account number and PIN. Second peak 7:23: the first line after the sponsor (viewers skipping the ad land there). 13:09: James Clapper's address "waiting to be found with just one Google search" [observed]. The wow is a **mechanism made simple**: how a teenager does it with a phone.
- **Ending:** Widens to a present-day stake: the 2023 renewal of a surveillance program and a court finding that the FBI misused a database "278,000 times", ending on "Maybe they're even surveilling us." The heatmap's last quarter is the weakest (0.125) [observed].
- **What we steal:** The dated present-tense cold open; two protagonists who converge; the sponsor bridged from the story's own theme; ending by turning the camera on the viewer's own surveillance; sources linked as a Google Doc in the description [observed].

## 2. The Hunt for the King of the Dark Web

- **Chapters (from the description):** 0:00 Intro · 1:24 The Dark Web · 6:40 The Hunt · 12:20 Alpha Bay
- **Re-hooks and timing:** Short "But…" turns roughly every 20 s (median gap 20 s). Chapter 2 ends on a question ("Who is the powerful mastermind…?", 5:38). Sponsor (NordVPN) ends at 6:40 straight into "The American investigators want to find out where AlphaBay's servers are…" [observed].

- **What we steal:** The single-mistake reveal (a hotmail address) as the wow moment; the flash-forward to the arrest; ending with 'the throne is empty again', an open loop for a sequel.

## Channel patterns

- **The dated present-tense cold open is Fern's signature** [observed in 5 of 6 with captions]:
  "It's October 2015…", "On the morning of July 5th, 2017…", "Jenny is working the night shift…".
  The turn comes by 0:20–0:30.
- **The three-question loop at about 1:00–1:40** [observed in 3 of 6]: three questions in a row that
  become the video's contract, restated once later.
- **The sponsor read sits at 5–9 minutes and the first line after it is a top-two heatmap peak**
  in four of the six videos with heatmaps (7:23, 6:46, 9:01, 7:37) [observed]. Viewers skip the ad
  and land on that line. Make it a re-hook.
- **Thumbnails are collages, not voids**: evidence boards with WANTED labels and eye bars, a face
  plus a red arrow, a red crosshair. The constant is one red element and high contrast [observed].
- **Speaking rate 150–173 wpm of runtime** [observed]. Our 150 wpm budget matches.
- **Sources in a public Google Doc** linked from every description [observed].
