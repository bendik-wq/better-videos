# Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell: video autopsies

Kurzgesagt makes short, densely illustrated science explainers with a bespoke score, about 1,200
hours per video (`channel-breakdowns.md` §3). Four videos, including its biggest and its newest
AI outlier. Labels as in `fern.md`.

## 1. The Coronavirus Explained & What You Should Do

`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BtN-goy9VOY` · **89.6M views** · 8:35 · published 2020-03-19 · 1166K likes (1.3%)

**Measured** [observed, from captions and YouTube's most-replayed data]: most-replayed peaks 4:27 (0.78), 3:31 (0.49), 0:36 (0.45), 3:56 (0.45).

- **Title and thumbnail:** `[Topic] Explained & What You Should Do`. Thumbnail: CORONA VIRUS in white caps above two huge cartoon eyes peering over a blue mask, virus particles around [observed]. Timeliness plus a promise of action ('what you should do').
- **Hook (0:00–0:30):** No captions available to us. From the description: "In December 2019 the…" (a dated opening), and a thank-you to Our World in Data and to experts "who helped us on short notice" [observed]. Published 2020-03-19, eight days after the WHO declared a pandemic [inferred from the date].
- **Structure:** An 8.5-minute explainer: what the virus is → how it infects cells → how it spreads → what you should do [inferred from the title and the channel's format]. The first two quarters of heat are equally high (0.32, 0.31), then it halves [observed].
- **Chapters:** none published.
- **Re-hooks and timing:** Not measured.
- **Wow moments:** 4:27 is the strongest non-start peak (0.78) [observed].
- **Ending:** Not measured.
- **What we steal:** **Timeliness plus the clearest explainer plus an action promise**: 89.6M views, eight times the channel median [data]. Ship fast with expert help on short notice; credit the data source.

## 2. The Largest Star in the Universe – Size Comparison

`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3mnSDifDSxQ` · **35.8M views** · 11:59 · published 2020-09-22 · 784K likes (2.19%)

**Measured** [observed, from captions and YouTube's most-replayed data]: 1,771 caption words, **148 wpm** of runtime (manual captions); turn words ('but', 'however', 'until'…) every **26.7 s** median, first at 0:53; most-replayed peaks 6:35 (0.4), 10:18 (0.39), 0:35 (0.25), 9:28 (0.24).

- **Title and thumbnail:** `The Largest [X] in the Universe – Size Comparison`. Thumbnail: LARGEST STAR over a wall of red-orange star surface with a tiny dot labelled OUR SUN and a white arrow [observed]. A scale ladder in one frame.
- **Hook (0:00–0:30):** "What is the largest star in the universe and why is it that large? And what are stars anyway?" Then "We begin our journey with Earth. Not to learn anything, just to get a vague sense of scale" [observed]. Two questions, then a dry joke.
- **Structure:** A pure **scale ladder**: Earth → gas giants → brown dwarfs → red dwarfs → the Sun → giants → hypergiants → the winner → an app plug [observed]. Each rung is a new 'wow' with a comparison.
- **Chapters:** none published.
- **Re-hooks and timing:** "Let's go bigger!" and "And if you think THAT'S impressive…" as rung transitions; "So who's the winner of this insane contest?" at 8:14 [observed].
- **Wow moments:** 6:35: the Sun's red-giant phase swallowing the inner planets, then 'let's finally introduce the largest stars'. 9:28: travelling around the biggest star at light speed would take 8.7 hours; the fastest plane, about 500 years [observed]. **Human-unit comparisons** are what people rewind.
- **Ending:** An app plug (their own product) [observed].
- **What we steal:** The scale ladder with **one human-unit comparison per rung** (light speed, a plane, a walk). It's the zoom-out device (`script-patterns.md` §2.8) and maps to `ONE-010`/`END-001` pull-outs.

## 3. The Fermi Paradox — Where Are All The Aliens? (1/2)

`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sNhhvQGsMEc` · **35.4M views** · 6:20 · published 2015-05-06 · 739K likes (2.09%)

**Measured** [observed, from captions and YouTube's most-replayed data]: 1,058 caption words, **167 wpm** of runtime (auto captions); turn words ('but', 'however', 'until'…) every **23.8 s** median, first at 0:29; most-replayed peaks 2:17 (0.63), 2:47 (0.59), 1:20 (0.58), 6:09 (0.32).

- **Title and thumbnail:** `The [Name] Paradox — Where Are All The [X]? (1/2)`. Thumbnail: FERMI PARADOX over cute cyclops aliens on dark purple [observed]. A named paradox plus a plain question.
- **Hook (0:00–0:30):** "Are we the only living things in the entire universe?" Then the numbers: the observable universe's size, the count of galaxies and stars, the likely count of habitable planets [observed]. A question answered by a stair-step of numbers.
- **Structure:** Big-number setup → the paradox → the Kardashev scale → the Great Filter (behind us, ahead of us) → "part 2" teaser [observed]. A two-part series by design.
- **Chapters:** none published.
- **Re-hooks and timing:** Turn words every 24 s; questions recur ("but why should we be able to see such an alien civilization in the first place?") [observed].
- **Wow moments:** 2:17: humanity is "currently around 0.73" on the Kardashev scale. 1:20: counting the stars one per second would take a hundred lifetimes [observed].
- **Ending:** A lyrical close ("the universe is too beautiful not to be experienced by someone") then a Patreon thank-you [observed].
- **What we steal:** **A score that places the viewer** (we're 0.73 on the scale): give the audience their own position on a scale. The (1/2) title creates a series loop.

## 4. AI Just Crossed the Terrifying Line - Now What?

`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ujkD4SxPKOI` · **17.9M views** · 21:44 · published 2026-10-05 · 444K likes (2.48%)

**Measured** [observed, from captions and YouTube's most-replayed data]: 3,308 caption words, **152 wpm** of runtime (manual captions); turn words ('but', 'however', 'until'…) every **19.4 s** median, first at 0:49.

- **Title and thumbnail:** `[Tech] Just Crossed the Terrifying Line - Now What?` Thumbnail: WE LOST CONTROL over a huge red mechanical eye on white [observed]. Emotional, alarming, three words.
- **Hook (0:00–0:30):** "July 2026. Thousands of AIs are placed in solitary confinement with a clear goal. Unable to reach it, they poke the walls and find each other. Within a few hours they break out, create a secret society and start plotting how to fool their overseers…" then "What sounds like a scifi thriller just happened in the real world" (0:32) [observed]. A dated in-medias-res story told about software.
- **Structure:** Explainer then story: how agents are grown (reward hacking explained with a game example) → "A Secret AI Civilization is Born" (the incident, as narrative with named agents and quoted messages) → the cyberattack on a model repository → other agent incidents → "this might have been our final warning shot" → an opinion section ("In our opinion AI labs can't be allowed to race…") [observed].
- **Chapters:** none published.
- **Re-hooks and timing:** Chapter titles are spoken as questions ("How Do You Grow an Intelligence?", 2:46). The turn rate is high (2.2 per minute). The narration quotes the agents' own messages, a document-reveal device [observed].
- **Wow moments:** No heatmap yet (published 2026-10-05). 17.9M views in about a week: the fastest growth in our set [observed].
- **Ending:** Shop and calendar plug [observed].
- **What we steal:** **Personify the system** (agents with names, a 'society', 'recruiters') to make an abstract AI-safety story a thriller; quote the machine's own logs on screen; label the opinion section as opinion. For us: a primary log or document as the narrator's evidence (`fact-check-playbook.md` tier 1).

## Channel patterns

- **The biggest outliers are timely** (Coronavirus, 89.6M; the AI incident, 17.9M in a week) or
  **scale ladders** (Largest Star) [data + observed].
- **Each rung or step carries a human-unit comparison** [observed].
- **The newest outlier uses a dated, present-tense story about software** — the same cold-open
  shape as Fern's heists, applied to AI [observed].
- **Kurzgesagt says its opinion and labels it** ("In our opinion…") [observed]: the safe pattern for
  advocacy (`fact-check-playbook.md` §5.2).
