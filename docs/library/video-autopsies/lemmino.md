# LEMMiNO: video autopsies

LEMMiNO (David Wångstedt) makes rare, long, meticulous mystery documentaries; he writes, narrates,
scores and animates them himself (`docs/research/channel-breakdowns.md` §4). Four of the biggest,
read from manual captions, descriptions and most-replayed data pulled 2026-10-12. Labels as in
`fern.md`: [observed] from captions, chapters, heatmap and thumbnails; [inferred] our reading.

## 1. Cicada 3301: An Internet Mystery

`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2O7blSSzpI` · **42.5M views** · 17:53 · published 2018-05-19 · 1135K likes (2.67%)

**Measured** [observed, from captions and YouTube's most-replayed data]: 2,424 caption words, **136 wpm** of runtime (manual captions); turn words ('but', 'however', 'until'…) every **27.4 s** median, first at 0:51; most-replayed peaks 0:42 (1.0), 10:12 (0.74), 3:34 (0.48), 17:21 (0.4).

- **Title and thumbnail:** `[Name]: An Internet Mystery`, 31 characters. Thumbnail: a pale cicada on black with "Cicada 3301" in glitchy red type across it [observed]. One object in a void: the purest example of our house grammar among the biggest videos in this set.
- **Hook (0:00–0:30):** "On the 4th of January, 2012, a user on 4chan posted this image…" The image is on screen; within 40 s the first hidden message is found by opening the file in a text editor [observed]. The hook is the *first solved step*, which teaches the viewer the game.
- **Structure:** Mystery box in four chapters, each a puzzle round (The First Puzzle, The Second, The Third, The End), then theories. The hero object (the cicada image) recurs at every chapter [observed].
- **Chapters (from the description):** 0:00 The First Puzzle · 5:19 The Second Puzzle · 7:42 The Third Puzzle · 10:20 The End
- **Re-hooks and timing:** The same three questions are asked at 2:03 and again, word for word, at 5:08 ("What was the puzzle for? Who was behind it? What happens when you reach the end?"). Each round closes with "However… this was only the beginning" / "this was still not the end" (5:15, 7:40) [observed]. Turn words every 27 s median.
- **Wow moments:** Top peak 0:42: the hidden text found inside the image file. 3:34: real posters with QR codes found on lampposts in several countries (a found-footage clip). 10:12: the questions restated in the final chapter [observed]. The replayed moments are *the moments the viewer can try themselves*.
- **Ending:** "…the current status of the third puzzle and the possibility of a fourth remains clouded in mystery." Unresolved by design; the final peak (17:21) is that line [observed].
- **What we steal:** The **verbatim repeated question triad** as a chapter re-hook; the first 40 s showing a trick the viewer could do; a single hero object that is literally the evidence; an honest unresolved ending.

## 4. The Unbreakable Kryptos Code

`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jVpsLMCIB0Y` · **9.3M views** · 47:01 · published 2024-05-04 · 298K likes (3.2%)

**Measured** [observed, from captions and YouTube's most-replayed data]: 7,187 caption words, **153 wpm** of runtime (manual captions); turn words ('but', 'however', 'until'…) every **24.3 s** median, first at 0:12; most-replayed peaks 23:58 (0.55), 7:31 (0.55), 33:22 (0.43), 38:33 (0.34).

- **Title and thumbnail:** Thumbnail is pure typography: KRYPTOS repeated in stencil letters, one line in red [observed]. The object is the text.
- **Hook (0:00–0:30):** "Right outside the United States capitol lies the headquarters of the Central Intelligence Agency…" A slow geographical approach to the courtyard and the sculpture [observed]. The first turn ("But, despite their best efforts, the code remained intact") is at 1:02.
- **Structure:** A 47-minute explainer-with-twist in 13 chapters: teach the cipher (Vigenère) → watch others break K1–K3 → the plaintext → the unsolved K4 → the artist's errors and clues → hypotheses. It's a course disguised as a mystery [observed].
- **Chapters (from the description):** 0:00 Intro · 2:30 The Vigenère Table · 5:45 Partial Decipherment of Kryptos · 7:46 Identifying Ciphers Within the Code · 11:09 Breaking K2 · 15:21 Breaking K3 and K1 · 17:49 The K1-K3 Plaintext · 24:02 The Mystery of K4 · 27:21 The Intended Solutions · 36:50 The IDBYROWS Mishap · 39:56 The K4 Clues · 41:44 Hypothetical Solutions to K4 · 45:45 Outro
- **Re-hooks and timing:** Each chapter is a solved sub-puzzle; the reversals are about who solved it first ("But, as it turns out, Stein was not the first to solve it either", 7:23, when it emerges the NSA solved it in 1992) [observed].
- **Wow moments:** 7:31: the NSA had secretly solved it years before the public 'first' solution. 23:58: how long the NSA took. 33:22: an anagram found in the keywords [observed]. Again: secret prior knowledge and a hidden pattern.
- **Ending:** Second-person send-off to the viewer who might solve K4: "All you need to do is throw some symbols into a blender, and you'll become immortal." A wry, warm button [observed].
- **What we steal:** **Teach the tool first** (the cipher), then let the viewer watch experts use it; that's how a 47-minute technical video holds. For us: teach the mechanism (how a CDO works) in Ch.1, then let the story use it.

## Channel patterns

- **Slowest speaking rate in the set** (136–160 wpm of runtime) [observed]: long holds and music;
  confidence to hold works when every line carries a fact.
- **Dated, procedural cold opens** with names, times and places [observed in 4 of 4].
- **Repeated question triads** are LEMMiNO's re-hook (Cicada repeats its three questions verbatim
  three minutes apart) [observed].
- **The replayed moments are the evidence itself**: a hidden string in an image file, a radar line
  on a map, a solved cipher [observed]. Our equivalent is a document or map shot the viewer wants to
  pause on, so hold those shots long enough to read (3–5 s minimum).
- **Unresolved endings are fine** if the video has been rigorous; honesty ("I have no idea") is the
  button [observed].
