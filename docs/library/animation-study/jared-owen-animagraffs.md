# Jared Owen and Animagraffs: the 3D cutaway channels (and why they narrow our claim)

Added in the second pass because the first README claimed that no studied channel treats its
subject as "a physical mechanism you can open, wind and watch run". These two channels do exactly
that, at scale, and **Jared Owen also does famous historical objects** (*What's inside the
Titanic?* 22M, *How did the Enigma Machine work?* 14M, *What's inside the Statue of Liberty?* 14M).
MAINSPRING's look has to be defined against them, not just against Kurzgesagt.

## 2. Title shapes [data]

- **`How does a [famous machine] work? ([model name])`** (Jared Owen) and **`How a [machine] Works`**
  (Animagraffs). The parenthesis names a specific model (M1A2 Abrams, Pave Hawk, Union Pacific
  "Big Boy"). Specificity is the whole hook: not "a tank", *that* tank.
- **`What's inside the [famous object]?`** (Titanic, Statue of Liberty, Big Ben, White House). A
  **famous name plus a door into it**: the same recipe as README §2.1, done literally.
- No verdicts, no "you", no panic. Pure curiosity with a guaranteed payoff.

## 3. Thumbnail grammar [observed: 8 per channel]

| | Jared Owen | Animagraffs |
|---|---|---|
| Image | One clean CGI cutaway of the object, side-on or 3/4, with a section removed | Semi-transparent "ghosted" body showing the mechanism inside; fire, fuel and moving parts colour-coded |
| Text | The object's name in heavy white condensed sans, top-left; often **"Inside"** in cyan under it | **None** |
| Colour | Saturated part colours (yellow gears, blue motors, red magnet) on dark grey or sky | Pale cream, grey or sky background; the mechanism carries the colour |
| Feel | A product-catalogue diagram | A museum cross-section |

## 4. Animation grammar [inferred from thumbnails and titles; no frames measured]

- **The camera orbits a single 3D object and the object comes apart**: shells fade or slide off,
  parts are isolated, labelled, and put back. The cutaway *is* the story.
- **Colour-coding by function** (fuel, air, power, crew) stays consistent across the video.
- **Scale cues are people**: tiny crew figures inside the tank or the Titanic.
- Narration (Jared Owen) is plain and procedural; Animagraffs' long videos are closer to a
  guided tour.

## 5. Why they get shared [inferred]

Homework, hobbyist and "I always wondered" sharing; the tank and Titanic videos are evergreen
search results. No comment or share data could be fetched (YouTube comments and Reddit search are
blocked from this container).

## 6. What this means for MAINSPRING

**What they own (stay out):**
1. **Engineering cutaways of real machines and buildings**: tanks, ships, aircraft, engines, dams,
   bridges, famous buildings with the roof taken off.
2. **The "How does X work? (Model)" and "What's inside X?" title formulas.**
3. **Ghosted or sectioned photoreal-CGI hero objects** with colour-coded internals, and the
   object-name-plus-"Inside" thumbnail.

**What's left for us (see README §5):** clockwork, paper and counter *metaphors* applied to
**history and news**: the event is shown as a mechanism that we build out of desk objects (counter
wheels, punched cards, ledgers, stamps, a wound spring), **not** a cutaway of the real machine
involved. If an ep10 scene starts to look like "the real object, sectioned, orbiting on grey", it is
a Jared Owen shot and must be redesigned.

**What we can still learn from them (mechanism, not look):**
- The **model-name parenthesis** as a specificity device: name the exact thing.
- A **6–20 min sweet spot** for a single-object explainer (Jared Owen's 8–20 min videos do 2–3× his
  median [data]).
- **Function colour-coding held all the way through** a video.
