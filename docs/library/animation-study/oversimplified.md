# OverSimplified: animation study

The most efficient channel in this study: **33 videos, a median of 40M views each**. It makes long,
comic, cartoon retellings of famous wars and revolutions, released rarely (a few a year at most),
almost always as multi-part series.

## 2. Title and topic selection

- **The topic is a name everyone already knows:** WW2, the Cold War, the Civil War, Napoleon,
  Henry VIII, Prohibition. Search demand is huge and permanent. The joke is in the brand name: a
  famous, complicated thing, *OverSimplified*.
- **The exception that proves the rule:** *The Pig War* (40M) is an obscure event, but its *name*
  is a joke by itself, "a war over a pig". The absurd premise does the work fame usually does
  [inferred].
- No clickbait words, no questions, no numbers except Part N [data].

## 3. Thumbnail grammar [observed: 6 looked at]

- **The event's name in big white rounded capitals** (THE COLD WAR, WW2, THE AMERICAN REVOLUTION),
  plus a **large Part numeral** in the bottom-left corner.
- **The "OverSimplified" wordmark** in the lower centre on every thumbnail.
- **Two or three caricature leaders**, head and shoulders, facing each other or the camera:
  Churchill and Hitler, Washington and a redcoat, Lincoln and Davis, Napoleon on a rearing horse.
  The heads are large and round, with small simple features and a few defining props (cigar,
  moustache, tricorne).
- **A political map as the background**, in flag colours (red vs blue for the Civil War), with
  small icons on it (planes, cannons, mushroom clouds).
- **No arrows, no circles, no shocked faces.** The feel is a board game about to start.

## 5. Script structure [recall + data]

- **Chronological, character-driven:** each nation or leader is a character with one exaggerated
  personality trait, and history plays out as their dialogue.
- **Escalation through decisions:** each section turns on a choice (invade or don't, sign or
  don't), so cause and effect is always clear.
- **Running gags** recur across an episode and across the channel (catchphrases, recurring
  background characters, an anachronistic sponsor gag) [recall; source: shapes.inc fandom
  summaries describe interruptions acting as a "Greek chorus" and sponsors built into the
  narrative].
- **The sponsor read is written into the story:** historical characters do the read [source:
  shapes.inc summary; recall; **verify before relying on it**. The chaindesk summary of *The Pig War*
  says the narrator introduces the video and mentions the sponsor (NordVPN) near the start, which
  points to a narrator read near the top, not necessarily a character read].
- **Parts end on a cliffhanger or a turning point** at a natural historical break, and Part 2
  starts with a quick recap. *RECAP: First Punic War* is even a standalone 2-minute bridge before
  the sequel series [data].
- **The serious turn:** in the darker episodes, the comedy stops for the atrocities. The tonal drop
  is part of why it's trusted [recall].
- **The script is cleaned for classrooms:** the original WW1 script had much more profanity, cut
  to keep it classroom-friendly [source: oversimplified.fandom.com, via search snippet].
- **Team:** writers, animators and asset designers; After Effects for animation, Photoshop for
  assets [source: oversimplified.tv/create].

## 6. Animation grammar [observed in thumbnails · recall]

- **The map is the stage.** Armies are icons or little figures moving across a coloured political
  map, and borders change colour as territory changes hands. This is the channel's main way of
  showing strategy.
- **Cut-out puppet characters:** big round heads on small bodies, limbs with few joints, moved in
  After Effects. Few drawings per character; acting comes from head tilts, eye changes and
  props.
- **The props are the joke:** a top hat, a sword, a bottle. A character holding a modern object
  is an instant gag.
- **Fast cuts on punchlines** and longer holds for exposition. Comedy timing sets the edit
  [recall].
- **Text on screen:** dates and place names as captions, unit counts on maps, and the occasional
  caption gag.

## 7. Sound [recall]

- **One narrator voicing every character**, pitched or sped up so leaders sound distinct.
- Stock and orchestral cues with comic stingers: a record scratch, a dramatic sting, silence for
  the punchline.
- Battle sound effects are kept cartoonish, and the music drops out at sombre moments.

## 8. Why it gets shared

- **The homework-saver:** students and teachers send it as "watch this before the test". Every
  topic is on a syllabus. Evidence [source]: a teacher on the World History Encyclopedia community
  forum calls OverSimplified and Crash Course World History "staples in my classroom"; a student in
  a school newspaper prefers it to a lecture because of "an element of fun"; Plugged In calls it an
  animated 21st-century CliffsNotes and flags that it isn't for elementary-age children. Sources:
  [forum thread](https://community.worldhistory.org/t/what-s-your-go-to-youtube-channel-for-teaching-history/70),
  [The Central Trend](https://thecentraltrend.com/126092/opinion/oversimplified-really-knows-how-to-make-history-fun/),
  [Plugged In](https://www.pluggedin.com/youtube-reviews/oversimplified/).
- **Quoting the jokes:** the running gags become memes in the comments and on social media
  [inferred].
- **The series loop:** Part 1 is the 105M entry point, and the end of each part sells the next.

## 9. What we take, and what we must not copy

**Take:**
1. **Pick an event the whole world already knows by name**, or one whose name is absurd by itself.
2. Animate nations or institutions as **characters with a single trait**.
3. Use **the map as the stage** for strategy.
4. Write the **sponsor read into the story** (unverified mechanic, see §5: check it by watching first).
5. **End Part 1 on a turning point** and build the sequel with a 2-minute recap.
6. **Stop the comedy for the tragedy.**

**Don't copy:** round-headed cut-out leaders, the name pattern `[Event] - [Channel] (Part N)`,
narrator-voiced pitched characters, or their running gags or catchphrases. MAINSPRING's tone is
dry documentary, so if we use comedy it should come from **objects and numbers**, not
caricatured people.

## 10. The live comparison: Yarnhub (animated famous-event history, 2024–26) [data]

OverSimplified hasn't posted in 12+ months, so the active animated-history benchmark is **Yarnhub**
(found in the discovery pass, README §1.4): 3.4M subscribers, 241 videos, median 2.2M,
**35 uploads in the last 12 months at a 3.4M median**, top-30 median length 14 min. Its biggest
recent titles are **one engagement told as a story**: *When F-16 Falcons Ate Hawks for Breakfast*
(32M), *When a B-17 Tail Fell With a Gunner Inside* (22M), *When MiG-29s Ambushed F-15 Eagles*
(20M), *Wake Island: The Last Stand That Stunned Japan* (16M, ~2026-09), *Midway From The Japanese
Perspective* (18M). Thumbnails [observed, 4]: photoreal-ish 3D aircraft in sky or desert, **no
text**. Title grammar: `When [specific unit] [vivid verb] [specific unit]` and the other side's
view. It confirms, live, that **one narrow, concrete door into a famous war** (one aircraft, one
tail gunner, one island) is what the 2025–26 feed rewards in animated history (`data/discovery_Yarnhub.videos.csv`).
