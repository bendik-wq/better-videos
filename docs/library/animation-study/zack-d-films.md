# Zack D. Films: animation study (Shorts and long-form)

The biggest animated-explainer channel by raw views: **3D photoreal shorts made in Blender**, one
fact or story in about 30–60 s. Its **long-form animated documentaries about famous disasters and
brand stories** were hits in **2022–23** (dated below); its last-12-month long-form is
compilations and "unreleased videos". It is still the most directly relevant channel for ep10's
thumbnail and topic lane, which is why §9 sets hard limits on what we borrow.

## 2. Title shapes

- **Shorts:** a mechanism or curiosity question, 33 characters median, and **4,028 of 4,035 titles
  carry an emoji** that names the feeling (😯 🤔 😱 🤮). Tags: *(explained)* on 151, *(fiction)* on
  26, *(urban legend)* on 3. Fiction is labelled [data].
- **Long-form:**
  - `[Place]'s [Year] [Unexpected noun] Disaster`: *Ohio's 1986 Balloon Disaster*. "Balloon" and
    "disaster" shouldn't go together.
  - `The [Famous brand] [Harmless thing] That Killed [N] People`: *The Pepsi Contest That Killed 5
    People*. A brand, something harmless, a body count.
  - `[Brand]'s $[full number, all digits] Mistake`: *Toyota's $2,300,000,000 Mistake*. **The
    number is written out in full** so it looks bigger.
  - `The Disturbing Story Behind The [Brand] Logo` and `The [Brand] Lie (explained)`: a hidden
    story inside something everyone has seen.

## 3. Thumbnail grammar [observed]

**Shorts** (we looked at 9 frames): the shelf image is a mid-action frame with **word-by-word
burned-in captions**, small white bold sans, low centre ("rained down", "at deflecting",
"start to swell"). The frames are photoreal 3D: a stitched wound, a full-body vein map, a man in
armour taking a hit, an inflamed blood vessel. **Inside-the-body cutaways** dominate the biggest
hits.

**Long-form** (6 looked at), **with no text on most:**
- *Ohio's 1986 Balloon Disaster*: Cleveland's Terminal Tower with a **red balloon cloud rising
  behind it in the shape of a mushroom cloud**. A visual rhyme that says "disaster" without a
  word.
- *The Pepsi Contest…*: **a Pepsi bottle cap held in fingers, dripping blood.** One familiar
  object, altered.
- *Toyota's $2.3B Mistake*: a red sedan with a panicked driver, with the label STUCK GAS PEDAL and
  a curved arrow.
- *Unreleased Videos*: the creator's 3D avatar holding tiny people like puppets.

**Rule:** one real-world icon that everyone recognises, altered by **one** wrong detail (blood,
the cloud's shape), photoreal and in daylight.

## 4. Hook mechanics

- **Shorts:** the goal is to "capture attention within the first 1 to 2 seconds of a swipe" and
  hold for 30–60 s, "substantive as well as hooky" [source: Chris Reilly, a 3D contractor for the
  channel]. In practice [recall; no Shorts transcript or frame was fetchable, so **verify by
  watching 5 Shorts before ep10's cutdowns are designed**]:
  - Frame 1 is the payoff image already in motion (the splinter going in, the stitch going
    through).
  - Line 1 is a direct question or a "this is why…".
  - Then a step-by-step mechanism with an inside-the-body or X-ray cut.
  - The last line often loops back to the first.
- **Long-form:** we have the Balloonfest summary (youtubesummary.com) but no verbatim transcript
  (the timed mirrors and rosetta.to don't have it; YouTube captions are blocked here). Structure
  only, no timings:
  1. The setup is a **feel-good civic stunt** (a charity fundraiser to rebrand the city and beat
     Disneyland's Guinness record with nearly 1.5M balloons).
  2. Scale: a giant net and thousands of volunteers.
  3. **The warning sign:** a storm on Lake Erie; two fishermen reported missing.
  4. The launch: 100,000+ people, the storm seems to clear.
  5. **The reversal:** the weather turns, and the balloons are pushed *down* instead of rising.
  6. Fallout: runways closed, streets blocked.
  7. **The tragedy:** the balloons hamper the Coast Guard search; both men are found drowned.
  8. **An ironic epilogue:** the record went into the 1988 Guinness book, and the category was
     later discontinued [observed: summary].

## 5. Script structure

- **Shorts:** about 100–150 words; one mechanism; no intro, no outro, no CTA [recall + inferred].
- **Long-form (Balloonfest):** innocent ambition → scale → an ignored warning → a reversal of
  physics → collateral damage → a human cost → irony. **That is a classic tragedy arc in 5½
  minutes**, told about a silly-sounding event, which is why it travels.

## 6. Animation grammar [observed frames + source]

- **Photoreal 3D in daylight:** Blender as "the main workhorse", Cascadeur for physics-assisted
  character animation, BlenderKit assets, EmberGen for smoke and fluids, After Effects compositing,
  a Flamenco render farm, **templates for compositing, a base 3D file and editing**, and custom
  Python to sync files [source: chrisreillyportfolio.com].
- **X-ray and cutaway transitions:** the skin goes translucent, the camera dives into a vein.
- **Stylised "uncanny-friendly" humans:** slightly plastic, big expressions, consistent across
  shorts (a recurring avatar with a headband).
- **Captions are part of the picture:** word-by-word, centred. Built for sound-off viewing.
- **Cinematic camera:** low angles, slow push-ins, slow-motion impacts.

## 7. Sound [recall]

- A brisk, warm single narrator; a short music loop; punchy impact and whoosh effects synced to
  the captions.

## 8. Why it gets shared

- **"I always wondered that" plus mild gross-out** (splinters, toenails, blown veins). People
  send it to friends with "ew" [inferred].
- **Brand disasters are social currency:** "did you know a Pepsi contest killed 5 people?"
  [inferred].

## 9. What we take, and what we must not copy

**Take, for ep10 (mechanisms only):**
1. **Specificity in the title**: a named subject plus an exact number (*Toyota's $2,300,000,000
   Mistake*). We take the specificity, not his formula (see the ban below).
2. **The tragedy arc for a silly-sounding event**: innocent ambition → warning → physics reversal
   → human cost → irony.
3. **Shorts cutdowns:** frame 1 is the payoff, captions are part of the picture, the last line loops.
   Captions in **our** typeface and layout.
4. **Thumbnail economy**: one object, no clutter. In **our** look (paper, counters, printed card),
   not photoreal daylight objects.

**Don't copy:**
- Their recurring avatar, the headband character, the emoji-suffix title system as a signature, or
  any Zack D. Films story already told (Balloonfest, the Pepsi Number Fever contest, Toyota's
  recall, the Starbucks logo, the Flamin' Hot story, Norman).
- **The combination that is his long-form signature.** We may not ship a video that has **all three**
  of: (a) a **brand, product or civic-stunt disaster** topic; (b) a **no-text, photoreal thumbnail of
  one famous object altered by one wrong detail** or shaped like a more famous disaster (the
  "visual rhyme"); (c) a title in his formula, **"The [Brand/Place] [Thing] That Killed N People"**
  or **"[Brand]'s $[full number] Mistake"**. **Any two of the three is already too close** if the
  topic is (a). If ep10's topic is a brand or place disaster, its thumbnail must be one of our own
  grammars (README §5) and its title must avoid both formulas.
