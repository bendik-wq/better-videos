# Science cinema: melodysheep, Sciencephile the AI, What If

Three channels that animate **scale, deep time and speculation** with CG and composited imagery
rather than cartoons. melodysheep is the cinematic ceiling; Sciencephile is the low-budget meme
remix; What If is the photoreal "you are there" factory.

### Title and thumbnail [observed: 8 looked at]
- **Title in capitals plus a poetic subtitle after a colon**, plus "(4K)": a film title, not a
  YouTube title.
- The thumbnail is **a film poster**: a widely letter-spaced white geometric-sans title over one
  planet or creature in deep space, with a small subtitle line. Muted teal, rust and black.
  Nothing shouts.

### Hook and structure [source: A Sound Effect interview, 2019 · youtubesummary.com]
- *Timelapse of the Future* is built on **a timeline as "scaffolding"**: Earth's upheavals → the
  Sun's death → the degenerate era → the black-hole era → heat death.
- The **opening is tied to an on-screen counter at 120 BPM** that **doubles in speed every ~5
  seconds**, so the acceleration of time is *felt* rhythmically. "The counter acts as a metronome
  driving the first piece of music."
- **Sampled voices of scientists** (Attenborough, Cox, Tyson, Hawking, Kaku, Rees) are placed to
  fit each section's pacing, chosen "for their fit… rather than volume of information".
- **Helen Keller quotes bookend it.**
- Boswell built in "moments where the music, visuals and sound effects could breathe".

### Sound
- **Music and sound design are one discipline:** black-hole rumbles replace the synth bass;
  CS-80 synths rise over distant explosions; one black-hole merger goes orchestral → eerie → trip-hop
  in about a minute; **a self-composed waltz** plays as the first black holes evaporate.
- He spent days on 10 seconds of the mix. Made in Ableton with Spitfire, Omnisphere and a Moog
  Sub-37 [source].

### Animation grammar [recall]
- Slow orbital and dolly moves through CG space; huge scale held for seconds; **few cuts, long
  dissolves**; a counter or year readout as the only text.

### What we take, and what we must not copy
**Take:**
- **A counter as the spine**, sped up with the music.
- Let it **breathe**: hold a wide shot over music with no narration for 4–8 s after each reveal.
- **Score and sound design as one instrument** (the thing's sound *is* the bass).
- A poster-grade thumbnail for prestige.

**Don't copy:** the "TIMELAPSE OF…" naming, the Symphony of Science sampled-voice music, or the
letter-spaced poster title treatment as a signature.

### Formats that work
1. **The substitution series:** replace the Sun or Moon with X, one object after another, each a
   new disaster. It's a ladder (`kurzgesagt.md` §5).
2. **Compression:** *All of Biology in 9 minutes*, *Physics in 6 minutes*. A big promise and a
   short runtime.
3. **The iceberg:** surface (known) → deep (bizarre), as levels.
4. **The tier list:** rank things in S–F tiers.

### Thumbnail [observed: 9 looked at]
- Stock or CG space imagery, **triptych comparisons** (three Suns over one skyline), **text with
  strike-throughs** (~~Black holes~~ ~~White holes~~ **Gray holes?**), a giant red X over a
  nebula, a meme-font "All Biology in 9 Minutes". Cheap, but the *idea* is always readable in one
  glance.

### Style [recall]
- A synthetic text-to-speech narrator (the "AI" in the name), dense meme inserts and stock
  footage remixes. Dry, deadpan, fast. Proof that a **format idea** can carry a channel with
  minimal craft.

**Take:** the strike-through thumbnail for "you thought X, it's actually Y"; the substitution
ladder; the tier and iceberg as chapter devices.

## Cross-channel lesson for ep10
These three show that **deep time, scale and speculation sell when there is a clock**
(melodysheep's counter, What If's "Hour by Hour", Sciencephile's "in 9 minutes"). Our engine can
render a *physical* counter (a dial or odometer in the 3D set), which keeps us visually distinct
from all three.
