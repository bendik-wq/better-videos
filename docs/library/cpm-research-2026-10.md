# CPM and sentiment research: picking a high-CPM next episode (2026-10-18)

An `idea-engine` run with one extra constraint: the topic has to sit in a **high-CPM ad niche**
(money, housing, insurance, retirement, debt, tax), with **live audience anxiety right now** and a
**hero object that works in the bright white-clay 3D style**. Every number below either links to
its source or is labelled **[computed]** (we derived it from tier-1 data, with the method
given), **[lead]** (secondary reporting we haven't confirmed yet) or **[inferred]** (our own
reading).

Past episodes, so we don't repeat them: AI data rush, Palantir, Strait of Hormuz, grid hacking
(Ukraine), Panama Canal drought. The last two were infrastructure and place stories, and none was
about the viewer's own money. Taking the money lane now rotates the lane.

**The real-Studio-data check:** [AIR Media-Tech, 300 channels, 3,595 channel-months, May 2025–May 2026](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/how-much-does-youtube-really-pay-in-2026-real-rpm-data-from-300-channels)
- Median RPM, all niches: **~$2.30**.
- Education and science: **$10.22**, the highest niche.
- Business and finance: **$2.01**, but from fewer than 10 channels and marked "directional". Its
  interquartile range runs from $0.73 to $5.17.
- Business and finance carries a **Q4 premium of about 60%**.
- The sample is AIR's own clients, many of them outside the US. AIR gives no geographic mix.

**Search-ad demand as a proxy for advertiser appetite:**
- In the LocaliQ/WordStream 2025 benchmarks, the all-industry average Google search CPC is
  $5.26, and legal is the highest category at about $8.58
  ([LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/);
  [WordStream 2025](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2025-google-ads-benchmarks)).
- The insurance and finance rows weren't visible in what we could read **[lead]**.

**Seasonality:**
- vidIQ says October to December is the CPM peak. November and December often run at 2–3× the
  yearly average, and finance shows one of the biggest spikes. CPMs drop 50% or more in early
  January.
- A late-November or early-December upload of a money video lands in the best ad window of the
  year.

**What this means for us [inferred]:**
1. Insurance and investing or mortgage topics sit in the most expensive advertiser categories.
   For a mostly US, UK, Canadian and Australian audience on long-form video, a realistic
   expectation is roughly **$8–20 RPM**, against about $2–5 for general documentary. That's the
   vendor midpoints, discounted by AIR's evidence that the format and audience matter more than
   the label.
2. **Explainer documentaries still qualify.** A video about mortgage rates or home insurance can
   be matched to mortgage, broker, insurer and brokerage advertisers. YouTube matches ads by
   topic and by viewer, not by whether the format is a talking head. We haven't found a tier-1
   source that confirms the topic matching, so this is **[inferred]**. Our own Studio RPM on the
   next episode is the real test; log it in the autopsy.
3. The **viewer** matters more than the niche label. AIR's low finance median and its wide
   spread show that a finance video watched mostly from low-CPM countries earns gaming-level
   RPM. Our titles should be framed for US and UK homeowners and savers.

## 2. Sentiment right now (evidence, 1–18 Oct 2026)

### 2a. Bonds, rates and mortgages: the hottest by far
**What the primary data show (tier 1):**
- **The 10-year Treasury closed at 5.31% on 5 Oct 2026.** That's the highest close since 2002
  (the 2002 peak was 5.44% on 1 Apr). It started the year at 4.19% (2 Jan) and was 5.09% on
  16 Oct. The 30-year reached 5.67% (7 Oct).
  [Treasury daily par yield curve](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=2026)
  **[computed from the CSV]**.
- **The 7 Oct 2026 ten-year auction** (a reopening, so the term reads 9-year 10-month) sold
  **$39B at a high yield of 5.300%**, with a bid-to-cover of 2.77. That's the highest yield at
  any 10-year auction since 8 Nov 2000 (5.865%). The 30-year auction on 8 Oct cleared at 5.618%.
  [Fiscal Data auctions](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/treasury-securities-auctions-data/)
  **[computed]**. NBC independently reports the same 5.3% and "since November 2000".
- **The 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.40% on 15 Oct 2026**, against 6.27% a year earlier.
  [Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms).
- **The Fed hiked on 16 Sep 2026**, by 25 bp to 3.75–4.00%, on a 12–0 vote.
  [FOMC statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm).

  ([statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20240918a.htm))

  New 10-year debt now costs 5.3%, so every rollover reprices upward. Bills, which all mature
  within a year, are $7.12T of $31.83T marketable debt.
  [Average interest rates](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/average-interest-rates-treasury-securities/);
  [MSPD Sep 2026](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/monthly-statement-public-debt/).

  [TIC Table 5](https://ticdata.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/tic/Documents/slt_table5.txt).
  - Japan: $1,068B, down from $1,239B in Feb 2026, the largest holder but falling.

### Winner: **The Auction That Sets Your Mortgage Rate** (21/25 · Money · H)

**Angle, familiar but unexpected:** everyone thinks the Fed sets mortgage rates. It cut by 1.75
points from Sep 2024 to Dec 2025, yet the 30-year mortgage went from 6.09% to 7.40%. Your rate
is really set by strangers bidding for US government debt, and in October 2026 that auction
cleared at its highest rate since 2000.

**Cold-open hook:** "On the seventh of October, at one o'clock in the afternoon, the US government
sold thirty-nine billion dollars of IOUs in a sealed auction that lasted seconds. The price it
got (five point three percent) quietly repriced every mortgage in America."

**The promise:**
- Follow one dollar of your mortgage payment back to whoever actually lent it.
- Show why the Fed couldn't stop the rise.
- Show what happens if the auction goes badly.

**Structure sketch [inferred]:**
1. The house and the rate tag.
2. The auction: how sealed bids work and who the dealers are.
3. The pipe: mortgage → Fannie/Freddie MBS → bond investors → priced against the 10-year.
4. Who's bidding: TIC flows from Japan, the UK and the Caymans, with Japan selling.
5. Why yields rose: inflation, the oil shock, the global rout, $40T of debt and $1T of interest.
6. The rollover wall: the average rate is 3.52% and new debt costs 5.3%.
7. **Climax/countdown:** the next quarterly refunding and the November 10- and 30-year
   auctions. Check the dates on Treasury's tentative auction schedule.
8. What it means for a buyer, an owner and a saver: savers win too, with T-bills paying about 4%.

**Why the CPM is high:**
- It sits in investing and mortgages ($15–50 CPM and $5–17 RPM in vendor estimates), with
  natural contextual matches for mortgage lenders, brokers, robo-advisers and Treasury-bill
  apps.
- Publishing in mid-to-late November lands in the Q4 peak; AIR measured a ~60% Q4 premium for
  finance.

**Sentiment evidence:**
- The highest 10-year yields and auction yield since 2000–2002 this month (tier 1).
- The mortgage rate is 7.40% (tier 1).
- The Fed's first hike since 2023 (tier 1).
- Small finance channels at 1.6–3.9× their subscriber counts on bond-crisis titles in Sep–Oct
  2026 (`yt-dlp`).
- The Infographics Show's "$1 Trillion Interest" video drew 736K in 4 days.
