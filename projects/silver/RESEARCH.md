# The Hunt Brothers' Silver Corner: research and fact-check

MAINSPRING episode: the Hunt brothers' silver play (1973–1980), Silver Thursday (27 March 1980), the bailout, the 1988 bankruptcy, and the January 2026 echo. Compiled 2026-10-19. Write the script from this pack, never from memory. Every number you say on air needs a row here.
Source IDs (S01…) point to `SOURCES.md`.

**Source tiers.**
- **1**: primary records (SEC/CFTC/Fed reports, congressional hearings, court rulings, exchange notices, official price/CPI data).
- **2**: newspapers and magazines of record (NYT, WaPo, WSJ, LA Times, Time, AP, Reuters, Bloomberg, CNBC, CS Monitor).
- **3**: books by named authors.
- **4**: reputable reference (Britannica, federalreservehistory.org).
- **5**: other press, dealers and blogs.
- **L**: lead only (Wikipedia, forum, Substack). Never cite a lead on screen.

**Markers.**
- ⏱ means re-check on VO-lock day and on publish day.
- ⚑ means it must be resolved or cut before script lock. These are usually items I could only see via a search snippet or an unread source.
- ⚠ DISPUTED means the sources disagree. Every position is listed.

**Access notes.**
- The **SEC staff report (S01)** was downloaded and read: Parts 1–4, ~320 pp of OCR.
- The **Rosenthal subcommittee hearing volume (S02)** was downloaded from archive.org and read: OCR of 1,211 pages, including the Hunts' sworn testimony of 2 May 1980.
- These sites blocked this environment, so I couldn't read their pages directly: nytimes.com, cmegroup.com, bloomberg.com, cnbc.com, britannica.com, justia (Minpeco opinion), web.archive.org, the LBMA price server and newspapers.com. Facts that rest only on a search snippet of those pages carry ⚑.
- In the hearing OCR some words are garbled. Where a quote contains an OCR gap I mark it [OCR].

## 10. Mechanisms explained (for graphics)

- **Futures and margin.** A Comex contract = 5,000 oz (S01 p.5 fn1). On 30 Dec 1979, $30,000 margin controlled >$170,000 of silver (S01 p.5). Every $1/oz move on ~96.8M oz of futures = ~$96.8M in or out of the Hunts' accounts every day (S01 p.33).
- **Variation margin is cash.** When prices fell, the brokers (FCMs) had to pay the clearinghouse dollar-for-dollar even when the Hunts didn't pay them. Bache paid ~$22.3M/day (S01 pp.9, 79).
- **Limit-down illusion.** Back-month futures could fall only $1/day while spot fell freely, so the accounts looked solvent at "futures prices" (S01 pp.10–11).
- **Taking delivery.** Standing for delivery turns paper into metal. When the Hunts stood for delivery in 1980 they had to pay full price, and they had already withdrawn the paper gains (S01 p.36 fn15).
- **Contagion path.** Hunts → ~20 FCMs → clearinghouses → banks that lent to brokers (Bache Metals' silver-backed bank loans) → public shareholders (S01 pp.3–5).

## 13. Story-worthy details (verified, with source)

1. **The man who bought the world's silver drove a 1973 Cadillac**, wore cheap brown suits and listed his phone number in the Dallas directory. "Chicken-fried steak and Jell-O … then go out and buy all the silver in the world." (S04)
2. **Dad's rule:** "risk a little with the potential to make a lot, not vice versa." His sons did the opposite. (S04)
3. **Libya took his oil (1973). He bought metal you can touch.** "Silver looked safer than overseas oil concessions." Time 1988 quotes him wanting "to invest in something I could get my hands on". (S04, S06)
4. **Rallies paid them cash daily:** each $1 rise = ~$96.8M wired to the Hunts. By New Year 1980 they had pulled out ~$1.4bn. (S01 p.33)
5. **Bunker walks into the CFTC (8 Jan 1980)** and asks whether the regulator wants silver up or down, and whether it will shut the market. (S02 memo)
6. **Tiffany's full-page anger:** "unconscionable … several billion, yes billion, dollars". (S04)
7. **Burglars started stealing silverware.** Families melted heirlooms. The CFTC chairman told Congress people were "turning in their home silverware". (S04, S02c)
8. **The ticking clock:** $434M due to Engelhard on Monday, 31 March. (S01)
9. **12:15 p.m., Wednesday 26 March:** Bache's chairman calls Volcker. The Comex president calls back furious: "Why the hell did I call Paul Volcker." (S01 pp.91–92)
10. **That afternoon the exchange *cut* margins** to save the broker, freeing ~$80M. (S01)
11. **2:13 p.m., Thursday 27 March:** the NYSE halts Bache stock, two minutes before silver futures close. Silver ends at $10.80. (S01 p.233)
12. **A Sunday-night summit in Boca Raton:** the bankers' convention where Volcker happened to be speaking. The banks say no after midnight, and the Hunts pay Engelhard in Arctic oil rights instead. (S01, S02b)
13. **Contempt of Congress, almost:** they skipped the hearing on 29 April, were threatened with contempt, then showed up on 2 May. Photographers were asked not to use flashbulbs. (S02a)
14. **"We talk too much to each other and not enough to others."** (Bunker, under oath; S02a)
15. **"You don't have a loss until you sell something."** (Bunker, under oath; S02a)
16. **Volcker: "This was gambling."** He still blessed a $1.1bn loan, swearing "There is no more money involved here. This is a restructuring." (S02b)
17. **The cowboy airlift:** a former senior aide swore that ranch hands rode "shotgun" on silver flights to Switzerland. The Hunts swore it never happened, and the subcommittee chairman sent the contradictions to the Attorney General (10 Jun 1980). (S02d) Present as a he-said/they-said.
18. **Bankruptcy inventory (1988):** two Cadillacs, a Mercedes, a Vail ski-lodge half-share and a 13,649-piece Byzantine coin collection. (S07)
19. **The punchline of 1980's market:** in January 1980 their silver was "worth" ~$9.8bn. Valued for the bailout it was $14.05/oz. (S01)
20. **Lamar named the Super Bowl** after a kids' bouncing ball, and was the brother who didn't go bankrupt. (S37, S09)

## 14. DISPUTED / CORRECTED points (read before scripting)

- **D1. The 1980 peak: date and number.**
  - Comex **spot settlement record: $48.70, Thu 17 Jan 1980** (S01 p.37 fn16).
  - **Intraday high $50.36**: the SEC says "Friday, January 17, 1980" (S01 p.65), but 17 Jan 1980 was a *Thursday*. CME Group (via snippet) gives **$50.35 on 18 Jan**. Time 1988 gives $50.35.
  - **London fix $49.45 on Fri 18 Jan 1980** (S28/S31 snippets ⚑).
  - LA Times/WaPo 1988 says "$52 an ounce on Jan. 21, 1980". This is unsupported by the SEC series, so don't use it.
  - **Script:** "about $50 an ounce on 17–18 January 1980" or "$49.45 at the London fix". Don't say "$50.35 on Jan 17" without a caveat.
- **D2. "From $21.62 to $10.80 in one day"** (Wikipedia/Britannica-style claim) is **wrong per the SEC**:
  - 25 Mar close $20.20, 26 Mar $15.80, 27 Mar $10.80.
  - The one-day fall on Silver Thursday was **−31.6%**, not −50%. Over two days it was −46.5%.
  - Time May 1980 (snippet) mentions "$10.20 in late March". That is unexplained ⚑.
- **D3. "The Hunts owned a third / half / two-thirds of the world's silver."**
  - SEC: the position was ~**15% of world stocks** (Handy & Harman 1.3bn oz) and ~76% of exchange-certificated stocks. The ~50% that was futures were claims, not metal.
  - Time Apr 1980: "two-thirds of all the commercial silver that is not in government or industrial users' hands", per the Hunt group's own figures.
  - Volcker (Apr 1980): perhaps two-thirds of some stock measure [OCR].
  - Herbert (sworn): "nowhere near two-thirds".
  - **Script:** "roughly 195 million ounces, perhaps a sixth of all the silver above ground, and most of the silver in exchange warehouses".
- **D4. The Bache margin call.**
  - Popular "$134M/$135M" (dealer blogs, FA Magazine). Time Apr 1980: Bache "announced that it had issued a $100 million margin call". NYSE log, 27 Mar: Bache had "margin calls outstanding for $146 million" (S01 p.233). By 26 Mar Bache had already advanced ~$156M to clearinghouses (S01 p.79).
  - "$134M" is probably a conflation with the 1988 $134M Minpeco judgment.
  - **Script:** "well over $100 million in unmet margin calls".
- **D5. The Switzerland airlift with armed cowboys.**
  - The *only primary record* found is the sworn statement (23 May 1980) of ex-Hunt aide Bill Bledsoe: silver "flown from New York City and Chicago to Switzerland … I didn't actually see the airplanes take off loaded with silver, but it was the talk around the Hunt companies … the Hunts enlisted ranch hands from their cattle ranches in Texas to ride shotgun".
  - The Hunts testified that no silver was shipped. Herbert: "I have transferred no silver out of the country" [OCR partial]. Their lawyers said a records search found "No documentary evidence to reflect any shipment of physical silver from the United States to Switzerland" (S02d).
  - The popular detail (three chartered 707s, Circle K Ranch shooting contest, 40M oz, $200,000) traces to retellings (e.g., a 2008 FOFOA blog citing Larry LaBorde's piece *H.L. Hunt's Boys and the Circle K Cowboys*; Britannica via snippet ⚑; possibly Hurt *Texas Rich* 1981 or Fay *Beyond Greed* 1982, not checked ⚑). FOFOA itself cut 40M to 4M oz after readers objected.
  - Physics: 40M oz = 1,244 t ≈ 30 full 707 freighter loads.
  - The SEC does say the Hunts held **forward positions in London and Zurich** and that Swiss Bank Corp (Zurich) was a major lender (S01 pp.33–34, 40).
  - **Script:** "A former aide swore he heard they flew silver to Switzerland under armed guard. The Hunts swore they never did."
- **D6. "A billion dollars isn't what it used to be."** No primary source. Attribution and date unverified (F14).
- **D7. Minpeco damages.** $66.2M → $198.6M → $133.95M (LA Times/WaPo, with arithmetic by Minpeco's lawyer) vs $63M → $189M → $134M (Time). **Net ~$134M agrees.** Use LA Times figures.
- **D8. CFTC 1989 penalty.** "$10M" (one penalty, AP) vs "$10M each" (APMEX). Ban "lifetime" (AP). ⚑ NYT 21 Dec 1989 unread.
- **D9. 2026 crash size.** Spot −27% (Reuters) vs futures −31.4% (CNBC ⚑) vs −26.42% settle (Bloomberg data in Cryptonews). Intraday −35/36%. Name the series.
- **D10. Cause of the 2026 crash.** Warsh nomination + dollar (primary trigger in CNBC/Reuters/State Street quotes) + margin/leverage unwinding. The CME's big hikes came *after* (2 Feb, ~6 Feb).
- **D11. Ages.** Bunker (born 22 Feb 1926) was **54** during the 1980 crisis, as Time says. The SEC report's ages (Bunker 56, Herbert 54, Lamar 49) reflect when it was drafted (~1982). Don't use them for 1980 scenes.
