# Packaging: Episode 9, Palomares (1966)
Chosen by the ep9-topic-packaging workflow (4 scouts → 3 judges → packaging tournament with adversarial critics → final judge). Full output: packaging-workflow.json.
## Decision
- **Title:** America Lost the Same H-Bomb Twice
- **A/B title:** America Lost an H-Bomb. Then Lost It Again
### Why
EXPECTED VIEWS × FULL-WATCH RETENTION decides it, and Palomares wins on both.

1) STORY ENGINE AND RETENTION. This is the only finalist with a real ticking clock (an 80-day hunt with Soviet trawlers and the world press watching), a payoff (the bomb comes up on 7 Apr) and a sting after the payoff (the plutonium is still in the soil and the fence is still up). Its reversals are physical events, not paperwork: mid-air collision, then plutonium rain, then the fisherman's sighting with nothing to point at but open water, then the largest naval search to date with Bayesian probability maps, then the ambassador's swim, then FOUND, then the line parts and the bomb is LOST AGAIN for 9 days, then it is re-found, CURV gets snagged, and the bomb is lifted, then the contamination twist. That gives 8–9 peaks across 14–20 minutes, which fits the 'so many climaxes' brief.
- The Louvre's back half is procedural (hearings, audits, empty searches). By design it has no resolution and no protagonist, so retention would fall off a cliff after minute 5.
- Bull has a great one-two in the middle (assassination, then the Teesport pipes), but the main whodunit can never be answered, the HARP opening is slow, and the climax is a 1996 report.

2) DEMAND AND CTR CEILING. 'Lost nuke' is a proven multi-million genre:
- Infographics 3.04M, Timeline 2.68M, Thoughty2 2.64M, Herrera 2.63M, SkyFury 8.24M and Kyle Hill 5.97M as recent nuclear breakouts.
- Videos specifically about Palomares top out at 808K. The genre has demand, but there is no definitive telling of this story yet.
- The Louvre has bigger raw demand, but it is badly saturated. Primal Space, SML, Zoufry, Hank Green and about 30 low-effort uploads already have the 3D rebuild, and our own revised forecast for it is only 120–400K.
- Bull's 'underserved' claim turned out to be false: Real Engineering has 2.58M and Qxir 611K already covers Teesport and Britain's role.
- Palomares is the only one where our angle, a bomb lost twice, is both fresh and the strongest beat in the story.

3) PACKAGING. 'America Lost the Same H-Bomb Twice' has a familiar noun, an absurd premise and a proven '…Twice' shape. 'H-Bomb' instead of 'Nuke' separates it from the Goldsboro '…Nuked…Twice' videos.
- 'WHERE'S #4?' (three bombs and a red dashed gap) reads in under 1 s at 168×94. It complements the title rather than repeating it: the image says there were four and one is gone, and the title says it was lost twice.
- It works worldwide. Bull's packaging leans on the UK ('Sheffield', 'Teesport'), and the Louvre's leads depend on fact gates for the expert recutting quotes.

4) FACT SAFETY. Palomares is the lowest-risk option: a 1967 Navy report, Moran's book, the 1975 DNA report, the NYT 2016 piece, and no living people accused.
- The Louvre is the highest-risk: an active case with suspects charged but not convicted, and the jewels could be recovered at any moment, which would break the title.
- Bull's killing can only ever be 'suspected'.

5) VISUAL TOOLKIT. Palomares fits it best: a painted blue-sky Spanish coast, a vertical sea cutaway, a yellow Alvin lighting a white B28 in its parachute, a Bayesian probability grid lighting up cell by cell, a split-flap counter for DAY 1 → DAY 80, Navy report pages, and the fenced field. Every peak is a diorama we can build with no real likenesses needed.

6) LANE RULE. Cold War disaster / deep-sea search thriller is a new lane after two money and markets episodes. It is not a repeat of the Hormuz geopolitics episode either.

What we lose:
- The 60th anniversary (Jan 2026) has passed, so the video has to be evergreen and win on Browse from the lost-nukes cluster.
- The genre is crowded, which is why the lead package must push the 'twice' angle hard and show a better telling inside 30 s (probability grid plus a primary Navy record on screen, never stock B-52 footage).
- Realistic base: CTR at or above the channel median +1 pt and 2–4× the channel median views at 28 days. The 2.6–3M+ genre ceiling (and the 10M stretch) is reachable only if the 24 Mar line-snap sequence holds retention past the midpoint.

## Hook (locked)
[Card, first frame: 17 JAN 1966 · 31,000 FT] Thirty-one thousand feet over Spain, an American B-52 slams into its own fuel tanker. Seven men die, and four hydrogen bombs fall out of the fireball. Three come down near a farming village called Palomares. Two of them crack open and dust the fields with plutonium. The fourth is gone. A fisherman saw a parachute go into the sea, but all he can point to is open water. [3D Bayesian probability grid lights up cell by cell] So the US Navy bets dozens of ships and one tiny submarine on his memory. They find it. They get a line on it. [1 s silence] And they lose it again. [1 s flash-forward: thick hawser gap, bomb tumbling down the slope. Hard cut to black.] Sixty years later, the bomb is long gone. The fence in Palomares is still up. [Fallback if the fence is gone: 'The plutonium in Palomares never left.'] Final line of the video, paying off the bookend over the same fenced field: 'Bomb number four came home. The rest of it never left.'

## Must fix before script
- Lock every depth and date to the US Navy's 'Aircraft Salvage Operation Mediterranean' (1967): found by Alvin 15 Mar at about 2,550 ft, line parted 24 Mar, re-found 2 Apr about 300 ft deeper, lifted 7 Apr (day 80). No depth numbers in the title or thumbnail.
- Confirm that 7 died and the split between aircraft: all 4 aboard the KC-135 and 3 of the 7 aboard the B-52.
- Pin down the 24 Mar mechanics from the Navy report and Moran: what the lift line ran from, how far off the bottom the bomb was, and whether it fell, slid or both. This decides how Thumb B and the 0:27 flash-forward are staged.
- Write the 9 days between 24 Mar and 2 Apr as a genuine loss with real doubt over whether it would ever be found, so the title 'Twice' stays honest.
- Simó Orts: say he saw the parachute and could point only to open water. No 'exactly where' and no 'nobody listened'. His salvage claim is optional and must be labelled 'reportedly' (Moran).
- Bayesian search: say 'one of the first big uses', never 'invented'. Credit John Craven's probability maps.
- Ambassador Duke and minister Fraga's swim on 8 Mar was off the beach, miles from the bomb site. Present it as a PR stunt.
- Before VO, confirm the current fence and cleanup status against CIEMAT and Spanish reporting. Word the 2015 Kerry/García-Margallo statement of intent as 'signed, then never acted on, per Spanish reporting'. Use the fallback hook line if the fence is gone.
- Present the airmen's illness as 'veterans say' / 'NYT reported (Dave Philipps, 2016)'. No medical causation claims.
- Use the 1,400 tons of soil shipped to Savannah River only with a source. Drop '1.3 million cubic feet' unless it is sourced.
- Check Alvin's 1966 livery against WHOI photos and the parachute colour against the recovery photos before any 3D build.
- The first 30–60 s must show the 3D probability grid and one primary record (a Navy 1967 report page or the 15 Mar Alvin dive log). No stock B-52 footage anywhere, so viewers can tell this apart from the low-effort uploads.
- Compress the collision and bomb mechanics into the first ~3 minutes, then escalate. Build 24 Mar as a full cliffhanger set-piece at about the midpoint.

## Pitch
LANE: Cold War disaster / deep-sea search thriller. ANGLE: in 1966 the US dropped four H-bombs on a Spanish fishing village and found three. The fourth was seen only by a fisherman nobody believed, and it took an 80-day hunt with a tiny submarine. The bomb slipped away a second time, and the plutonium is still in the soil. HOOK: 'On January 17th, 1966, four hydrogen bombs fell out of the sky over a Spanish village called Palomares. Three of them landed. The fourth disappeared, and the only man who saw where it went was a fisherman nobody would listen to.' SPINE: (1) A Chrome Dome B-52 collides with its KC-135 during refuelling at 31,000 ft; 7 die. (2) Four bombs fall. One lands softly in a riverbed, and in two the conventional explosives detonate, spraying plutonium on tomato fields. (3) Bomb No. 4 is missing. Francisco Simó Orts ('Paco de la Bomba') saw a parachute go into the sea, but the search stays on land. (4) Escalation: the largest naval search to date, probability maps (an early landmark use of Bayesian search), dozens of ships, Alvin. (5) PR stunt: the US ambassador swims on camera. (6) FOUND (15 Mar): Alvin sees the parachute ~2,550 ft down on a steep slope. (7) REVERSAL (24 Mar): the lift line fails and the bomb slides deeper. (8) Found again, the CURV robot gets tangled, and the bomb is lifted on 7 Apr, day 80. (9) TWIST: 1,400 tons of soil shipped to South Carolina, but land is still fenced, the 2015 US–Spain cleanup pledge was never carried out, and cleanup airmen later reported illness (NYT 2016). CLIMAXES: mid-air collision; plutonium rain; fisherman ignored; ambassador's swim; Alvin finds it; line snaps; robot tangled then recovery; contamination still there. HERO OBJECT: white B28 casing on a grey seabed slope wrapped in its parachute, lit by Alvin's lamps. THUMBNAIL: bright vertical cutaway with a sunny Spanish coast and a tiny fishing boat on top, and deep below the bomb in its parachute with yellow Alvin approaching. Red loop on the bomb. Text 'BOMB #4' / 'STILL LOST?'. DEMAND: Infographics 'Live Nuke Still Missing' 3.04M, Timeline 2.68M, Thoughty2 2.64M, Fascinating Horror Goldsboro 1.14M, Wendigang 969K, LazerPig 919K. Palomares-specific videos top out at 808K (History Guy) / 173K, so it is underserved. Nuclear near-miss breakouts this year: SkyFury 8.24M, Kyle Hill SL-1 5.97M. 60th-anniversary interest in 2026. SOURCES: Barbara Moran 'The Day We Lost the H-Bomb' (2009); US Navy 'Aircraft Salvage Operation Mediterranean' (1967); DNA Palomares Summary Report (1975); NYT Dave Philipps 19 Jun 2016; WHOI Alvin records; 2015 US–Spain statement of intent; CIEMAT monitoring; Beyond Nuclear (Apr 2026). FACT RISK: LOW-MEDIUM. Lock depths and dates to the Navy report and confirm the crew toll split. Present the airmen's health as 'veterans say / NYT reported'. Confirm the cleanup status against Spanish reporting. Say 'one of the first big uses' of Bayesian search, not 'invented'.
