## Description

> On 17 January 1966, an American B-52 carrying four hydrogen bombs collided with its tanker 31,000 feet above the Spanish coast. Seven men died. Three bombs fell near the farming village of Palomares, and two of them cracked open and dusted the fields with plutonium. The fourth vanished into the Mediterranean. A fisherman had seen its parachute go into the sea, but all he could point to was open water. What followed was one of the biggest searches the US Navy had ever mounted: dozens of ships, a probability map, and a tiny submarine called Alvin. They found the bomb, got a line on it, and lost it again. Sixty years later, the plutonium is still behind a fence.
