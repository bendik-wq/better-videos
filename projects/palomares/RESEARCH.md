# Palomares: research and fact pack

This pack is for the episode on the 1966 B-52/KC-135 collision over Spain. It covers the four hydrogen bombs, the plutonium left on land, the 80-day hunt at sea, the bomb that was found and then lost again on 24 March, and the fence that still stands in Palomares.

It was compiled **2026-10-21** from six researcher passes: crash, land, search, politics, people and visual. **The script is written from this pack, never from memory.** Every spoken or on-screen number needs a row here. Source IDs (S01…) point to `SOURCES.md`, which gives full citations, URLs, tiers and read status.

**Tiers (playbook)**
- **T1** primary record
- **T2** official agency publication
- **T3** on-record words: speeches, transcripts, participant memoirs and interviews
- **T4** institutional, peer-reviewed or archival
- **T5** quality press and books
- **T6** aggregators

Moran (S40) is T5 but rests on T1 FOIA records and T3 interviews. Rows say which, e.g. "S40 (T5, from T1 S26)" or "S40 (T5, T3 interview)".

**Markers**
- ✓ verified by a researcher in a primary or official document they opened.
- ⚑ secondary only, single-book, snippet, or a primary we know only through someone else's citation. Resolve it or hedge it before lock.
- ⚠ disputed: sources conflict, and both versions are given here and in §12.
- ⏱ the situation is still moving (court case, fence, talks). Re-check on VO-lock day and publish day.

**Single-book rule.** Most dialogue (airmen, Alvin crew, Guest, Mooney, Brady) exists only in Moran's interviews (S40). Her 2004 article (S41) is by the same author, so it is not independent. On screen, attribute these lines: "as told to Barbara Moran".

**Access note.** These were blocked: DTIC (the full DNA report), USNI, archive.org, WHOI, Google Books and most of El País. The Navy's own 1967 report (S24) is not online. Navy-report facts are ✓ only where the 1975 DNA report (S01, partial copy on OSTI) or the 1985 State Dept history (S02) repeat them. Otherwise they are ⚑ via Moran's page citations.

## READ FIRST: corrections to `packaging.md`

1. **The 7 dead are CONFIRMED ✓.** All 4 aboard the KC-135 and 3 of the 7 aboard the B-52 died. Four of 11 survived (S01 Table 1-1, T1; S40).
2. **Alvin was WHITE in 1966, not yellow** (S40 ch.9: "white, bulbous body"; 1964 commissioning photo). It got its red-orange sail later; the "1982" repaint year is unverified (S99 ⚑), so keep the year off screen. **Cubmarine** (banana yellow, rated to only 600 ft, could never reach the bomb) was at Palomares, but it was not the only yellow sub: **Deep Jeep** was also bright yellow and was there early before being sent home (S40). Aluminaut was painted "bright orangey red" (S40). **Fix the thumbnail and the pitch.**
3. **The bomb's parachute was off-white or dirty yellow, not orange-and-white.** Orange-and-white was the survival chutes. The colour rests on Wendorf's recollection as told to Moran (S40 ⚑, no T1 source). It was a **64-ft** canopy (S06 ✓).
4. **On 24 March there was no Alvin on scene.** Alvin was **ordered to surface** before the lift (S40). The winch began at about 7:30 pm, around sunset (~19:20 local by our own calculation, not a source), and the bomb left the bottom and was lost at roughly 9–9:20 pm, in full darkness. At about 780 m there is no daylight anyway. **But there was a light:** POODL carried a **strobe** as well as pingers and transponders (S40). Thumb B and the 0:27 flash-forward must drop any Alvin lamp cone; a small blinking strobe on the POODL frame is accurate.
5. **The line that parted was NOT a hawser at a ship's hull.** It was the **300-ft line between an anchor and a grapnel twisted into the parachute risers**, below the anchor of a 3½-in nylon line winched up through *Mizar*'s centre well (§6).
6. **It ended "250 ft deeper", not "300 ft deeper".** DNA gives 2,550 → 2,800 ft (S01 ✓). You only get 300 ft by using the press figure of 2,500 ft. It was lifted from about 2,850 ft.
7. **"Day 80" holds if it means 80 days after the crash** (17 Jan → 7 Apr). The Navy's own cost maths, Simó and Moran 2004 all count **81** (both ends). Caption it: "80 days after the crash".
8. **Simó pointed to the area, not the exact spot.** DNA says Alvin found it "**in the vicinity of** the place where Simo' Orts reported seeing a weapon descend" (S01 ✓). One Spanish source puts it about 1 km away (S44 ⚑).
9. **"The search stays on land" (pitch spine, step 3) is wrong.** Guest's very first sea search area (24 Jan) took in the fishermen's sighting. A priority area was centred near Simó's point. Simó was taken out on 22 Jan and again on 7 Feb. Fair wording: "**believed by some, doubted by others, and all he had to point at was water.**"
10. **The swim was at Palomares's own beach, as well as Mojácar.** Duke swam at Mojácar in the morning, then with Fraga at **Quitapellejos beach, Palomares** in the afternoon (S02 ✓). That beach was "miles from" bomb 4's offshore site, about 5 mi out, but next to the contaminated land. The irony is that the plutonium was in the soil, not the sea (a fair simplification: bomb 4 was intact, and the official recovery statement said "no release of radioactivity into the coastal waters has occurred", S40 quoting it).
11. **Fence: STILL UP ⏱⚑.** CSN documents its installation (S11 ✓). Its standing in 2026 rests only on T5 regional reporting of works inside the fenced or expropriated zones (S71, S73, S74), which the fact-check could not re-open. **Use the main hook line, but re-confirm on VO-lock day** (open item 13). Thumbnail C ("STILL HERE") is valid on the same condition.
12. **2015 statement wording.** It was a "**statement of intent**, signed 19 Oct 2015" (Kerry called it "a memorandum"). "Non-binding" comes from the text as reported (S68, S76 ⚑: "no supone… obligación jurídicamente vinculante") and from Wikipedia; S16/S17 don't use the word. No final agreement has followed (GAO Jan 2024, S16 ✓; still none as of May 2025, S70). Talks did happen, so drop "never acted on". The twist: Spain's own state lawyers argued in court in **2025** *against* a cleanup (S70). The Apr 2026 brief quote is unverified (§13).
13. **"1,400 tons" is OK, but it was soil AND vegetation** (S16, S10 ✓). The barrels went to Savannah River, in Aiken, **South Carolina**; Georgia is a known error. **Drop "1.3 million cubic feet"**: no source has it.
14. **Weapon designation.** Do not print "RI" or "FI". Say "Mark 28" / "B28 hydrogen bomb". The researchers disagree on the variant (§12 D1).
15. **Bayesian search did not point to the bomb.** Simó's sighting, Alvin's 1 Mar furrow photo and pilot persistence found it. Say "**one of the first big uses**", credit Craven's maps and Richardson's daily maths, and give it the 1968 USS *Scorpion* payoff.
16. **The 9 days are a real loss ✓.** State history: "the loss of contact with the bomb for 9 days" (S02 T1). DNA: "much anxiety for a period of 8 days" (S01 T1). The title "Twice" is honest.

### 2b. The collision: two versions ⚠

- **Official (USAF accident board) ✓/⚑.** Source: S25 summarised in S40 (T1 via T5). "Investigators concluded that the B-52 overran the KC-135 and then pitched upward and rammed the tanker. The collision ripped the tanker's belly open, spilling jet fuel through the plane, onto the bomber, and into the air. A fireball quickly engulfed both planes."
- **Lewis/Air & Space version ⚑.** Sources: S46 via S100, and S69. "The KC-135's boom struck the B-52's longeron, and the left wing of the bomber snapped off."
- **Messinger, verbatim (T3, S40 interview 2004; same words in S41).** "We came in behind the tanker. We were a little bit fast, and we started to overrun him a little bit… There is a procedure they have in refueling where if the boom operator feels that you're getting too close and it's a dangerous situation, he will call, 'Breakaway, breakaway, breakaway.' … There was no call for breakaway, so we didn't see anything dangerous about the situation. But all of a sudden, all hell seemed to break loose." He recalled overrunning "a 'wee bit' but nothing serious".
- **Survivors' dissent (Wendorf and Rooney, S40, T3).** "They insist that the explosion came first and that it came from the back of the bomber." They suspect fatigue failure in the tail. Wendorf "still had his eye on the tanker when he heard an explosion coming from the back of the B-52. The plane pitched down and to the left. Fire and debris shot into the cockpit."
- **Why it can't be settled (S40).** "Because this accident occurred on foreign soil, SAC dumped the debris into the ocean." The only surviving board member, Ross C. Cox, refused interviews. Moran: "We may never know conclusively."
- **Wreckage.** 91 t spread over "cientos de hectáreas" (hundreds of hectares) on land and at sea (S10, T1 ✓). The DNA report says "some 250 tons of debris" (S01 p.75 ✓; S16). ⚠ D10.
- **Safe script line:** "The official investigation blamed a bomber that came in too fast and rammed the tanker. Two of the men in the cockpit always insisted something exploded in the tail first."

### 3b. Parachute system (as described in S06's FI section, T1 ✓)

- At release, detonators fire mild detonating fuse, which "removed the rear cover plate from the bomb. As the cover plate blew off, the 4-foot pilot parachute deployed a 16-1/2-foot-diameter parachute."
- About 2.4 s later, a second set of detonators "initiated deployment of the second-stage chute", the **64-ft** canopy.
- In free-fall mode, "both the 16-1/2- and 64-foot chutes were then jettisoned, and a 30-inch drag chute deployed".
- Retarded fall rate: "about 30 feet per second".
- S06 also says: "A fourth parachute was added to the Mk 28 Retarded Internal Bomb design to provide free-fall stabilization… The Mk 28 FI Bomb consisted of a Mk 28 Mod 2 Warhead, Mk 28 Mod 3 Fuze, and Mk 28 Mod 0 Full-Fuzing Internal Shape Components."
- Moran's plain-English version (S40) agrees. A 16-ft ribbon chute runs "for two to three seconds, then cut[s] itself loose". The 64-ft "monstrous canopy" slows the bomb to ~28 ft/s.
- ⚠ The visual researcher read S103 (GlobalSecurity) as giving the 64-ft set to the **RI**, with the FI as 4 + 16 + 24 ft. That conflicts with the crash researcher's reading of S06. See D1.

### 3d. Why there was no nuclear explosion

- Moran (S40): "If the high explosive didn't detonate simultaneously, the plutonium would not be evenly compressed; there would be no critical mass and no nuclear explosion. Such a precise detonation could happen only when a bomb was armed—not the case with the bombs of Palomares." The explosive sphere is made of 20 hexagonal and 12 pentagonal lenses ⚑.
- One-point safety, from a DNA footnote, verbatim: "a probability no greater than one in one million that if a nuclear weapon undergoes detonation on any one point… the weapon will not produce a nuclear yield… in excess of 4-pounds TNT equivalent." (S01 ✓)
- Sandia's definition (S07 ✓ T4): "the weapon must have a probability of less than one in one million of producing a nuclear detonation if a detonation of the high explosives originates from a single point (as would likely happen in a crash or fire)." The formal Walske criteria date from **1968, after** Palomares. Sandia set up an independent nuclear-safety department in 1968 "following the Palomares and Thule accidents" (S08 ✓).
- Situation Room to LBJ, 18 Jan: two weapons had "a low order high explosive detonation"… "it is not believed that there is any basis for undue concern over the low order detonation of the two weapons." (S03 d189 ✓) The first sitrep to the SecDef added "Impact on populace practically nil." (S40 ⚑)
- **Suggested VO:** "To set off the bomb, every charge around the plutonium core has to fire at the same instant, squeezing it inward. Hitting the ground sets the explosive off from one side. It blows the core apart instead. No nuclear blast, just plutonium dust in the wind."
- Moran's framing: "It was, in effect, a dirty bomb." (S40 ⚑)

### 4c. Urine tests, the "sleeping dog", doses

Present all of this as "**NYT reported**" or "**veterans say**". Make no causation claims.

- More than **1,500 urine samples** were taken and about 10 men were officially over the limit. Dr **Lawrence T. Odland**, "the Air Force's chief of radiation testing" (use that title, not "radiation chief"), **threw out about 1,000 (67%)**, including all of those from the first days (S61 ⚑ T5; figures checked against the TRANSCEND full-text repost, nytimes.com itself not fetched).
- The **Plutonium Deposition Registry Board** (1966) was set up to monitor the men for life. Its minutes proposed not telling the troops, to avoid setting "a stage for legal action". An AEC note of 5 Apr 1967 says Odland could not get DoD support "because of the sleeping-dog policy". The board was dismantled in 1968 and "had met just once" (S61; S21 ✓ T1 filing).
- A **2001 Labat-Anderson** report found the old data "not useful" and recommended retesting. The men were not retested (S36 via S21, S61).
- In **2013** the Air Force stopped relying on the old urine results and assigned all cleanup troops a "worst-case scenario" dose of **0.31 rem**, too low for VA benefits. Thule veterans were assigned zero. Col. Kirk Phillips: "We think retesting could be a real mistake… It could harm our veterans because we think it would find even lower levels of radiation." (S61 ⚑)
- According to FOE, citing AF memos, the "High 26" were assigned 10–170 rem, everyone else 0.31–10.5 rem, and 1,430 men had no follow-up (S21 ✓ filing).
- AF Medical Service: "adverse acute health effects were neither expected nor observed, and long-term risks for increased incidence of cancer to the bone, liver and lungs were low." (S61)
- NYT's own count: "Of 40 veterans… The New York Times identified, 21 had cancer. Nine had died from it… no formal mortality study has ever been done." (S61) **Attribute it to NYT.**
- Only Messinger of the four aircrew tested positive in mid-February, and not at a dangerous level (S40 ⚑, single-book).

### 5c. The find, 15 March 1966: contact #261

- **Navy summary (S01 p.94, T1 ✓):** "Forty-two days after the accident… on the tenth dive by ALVIN, a target was discovered in the vicinity of the place where Simo' Orts reported seeing a weapon descend."
  - The furrow was "approximately **400 feet long** rather than the expected 10 feet".
  - The weapon "descended to a depth of **355 fathoms** [2,130 ft]… touched down on the rim of an underwater ridge and was apparently dragged over the edge by the current forces on the chute… slid into the deep submarine canyon… continued its descent to a depth of **425 fathoms**, leaving a smooth furrow."
  - "**on 15 March ALVIN successfully backed down the furrow to discover a parachute enshrouded object… at a depth of 2550 feet lying on a 70 degree slope. The object was designated contact #261.**" A photo was taken at 2,550 ft (DNA Fig. 3-15).
  - ⚠ "Forty-two days" does not match 17 Jan → 15 Mar (57 days). It may count from TF-65's start (23 Jan → 15 Mar = 51) or be a different baseline. Don't quote "forty-two days" on screen. ⚑
- **That morning:** Guest has just ruled C-4 "searched to 98 percent" and wants to move Alvin to Bravo. Mooney pushes back. Guest: "**All right, goddamn it. One more day, and that's all.**" (S40, Mooney interview, single-book ⚑)
- **Simó that day:** aboard the minesweeper USS *Salute* with Richardson and John Bruce (WHOI). Over coffee he said he could rig hooks on his trawl and snag the chute himself if the Navy gave up. Lunch was interrupted by news of Alvin's sighting (S40, from Richardson's diary ⚑).
- **Dive #128.** Pilots **McCamis** and **Val Wilson**, observer **Art Bartlett**, with Rainnie on surface control. It was McCamis's son's birthday: he asked to be put "right on the elusive track, because today was his son's birthday". He **backed** down a ~70° slope to keep the track in his front window. At ~2,500 ft both men shouted "That's it! That's it!" (S40, from S51 p.25 ⚑)
- **Code word ⚠:** "instrument panel" (Navy) vs "bent nail" (McCamis). Wilson forgot the code and broke it over the phone (D19). Note to Guest at the *Albany* flag-transfer lunch: "**ALVIN reports INSTRUMENT PANEL.**" He left without a word (S40 ⚑).
- **In the dark:** Alvin wedged itself into a crevice just below the chute and switched its lights off. The three men sat **8 h** in the dark and shared **one illicit cigarette**, with Bartlett juggling the oxygen and the CO₂ scrubber (S40 from S50 p.76, single-book ⚑).
- **Relief:** Aluminaut came down to relieve them, "the first time two submersibles had ever accomplished such a feat" (S40 ⚑). McCamis: "**It was beautiful, the most beautiful thing I ever saw. A great silvery-pink monster, it looked like, with great green phosphorescent eyes coming up silent through the water.**" (S46 p.208 and S40, two books ⚑) Aluminaut parked ~25 yd away (S24 p.66 via S40).
- **Surfacing:** Alvin surfaced after **10 h 23 min**, its longest dive of the operation, and entered the *Fort Snelling* well deck at **8:12 pm** (S40, from S27 ⚑).
- **The doubt:** Guest: "How do you know it's not a parachute full of mud?" McCamis: "What else is going to be down there with a parachute and a bomb rack hanging on to it?" and "In all my life I'd never had my intelligence so insulted." (S40, S41 ⚠ D20)
- **Sitrep 79,** 02:50, 16 Mar: promising but not conclusive. If the object starts to slide he may have to act at once (S40, from S28 ⚑). Aluminaut guarded the contact for **22 h** on 16 Mar.
- **Comic beat, 16 Mar:** someone in Aluminaut walked aft to the urinal, its stern dipped toward Alvin, and McCamis jinked away (S40 ⚑).
- **Leak and press:**
  - State history (S02 p.9 ✓): Guest's "reluctance… to release information without definite confirmation… forced the Embassy to cancel precipitously an early morning press conference on March 16". A statement followed on 17 Mar.
  - Moran: UPI's Harry Stathos heard of the find from a Pan Am pilot who had been drinking with an Air Force colonel. A 1 a.m. press conference was cancelled at 12:45 a.m., and Rusk's line was read instead: "There have been hopeful developments but I cannot give you further information at this time." (S40 ⚑ ⚠ D30)
  - The final release said "located on March 15 in 2,500 feet of water, approximately five miles off shore by units of Task Force 65." The Navy had the embassy's wording "by the submersible Alvin" changed (S40 ⚑).

### 5d. 16–23 March: failed hook-ups

- Alvin carried a light polypropylene messenger line down and dug its fluke into the sediment. The fluke pulled out when they tried to slide a heavier line down it (S40, from S24 p.73 ⚑).
- **19 Mar:** a line-laying attempt using Mizar's instrument sled failed. The bomb had **slid ~20 ft** downslope (S40 ⚑).
- **POODL** (Pitts–Moody–Lindbergh; consultants Ray Pitts and **Jon Lindbergh**, son of Charles) (S40, from S24 vol.4 app.B pp.42–44 ⚑):
  - A **7-ft steel frame "like a giant shuttlecock"** carrying pingers, transponders and a strobe.
  - Buckets of coiled nylon: **190 ft** with a grapnel, and **150 ft** with a hook.
  - Lt Cdr Malcolm MacKinnon: "Oh, my God… It was really a kludge." Moody called it "gypsy-engineered."
- **23 Mar:** Mizar dropped the anchor and POODL **~80 ft from the bomb**. POODL fell over and tangled. Alvin hooked the anchor's line into the chute before its battery ran low. The bomb had moved ~6 ft into a small ravine (S40 ⚑). The same day, diver **Carl Brashear** was nearly killed aboard USS *Hoist*; his left lower leg was nearly severed. This is the *Men of Honor* story; optional cameo (S40 note ⚑).

### 5f. Probability maps: what they did and didn't do

- **Craven** (TAG) sketched a rough probability contour map that "basically outlined what everyone already thought": off the beach, or near the fishermen's sighting (S40 ⚑).
- On Frank Andrews's advice he brought in **Wagner Associates**. Richardson computed **Search Effectiveness Probability (SEP)** for each grid square, a value from 0 to 1 answering "if it's there, would we have found it by now?" (S40 ⚑)
  - He worked nightly from ~11 pm to 3 am on a **Friden mechanical calculator** in *Boston*'s accounting office and briefed Guest at 8 am.
  - Andrews: "Tony, don't talk about Gaussian distributions to an admiral!" Navy graphs "need to point up", so "search failure probability" was renamed.
- Richardson: "It's important psychologically to have something that shows that you're actually achieving something. And SEP served that purpose… It was kind of like the thermometer on the United Fund chart. It keeps going up and up." (S40 ⚑)
- Moran's framing: the TAG wanted "**some fancy numbers to wave in front of the politicians**" if the search failed. Deep-water squares stayed "stubbornly close to zero", while Moody's inshore squares ran over 90% (S40 ⚑).
- **Craven's own version ⚑** (S49 via S109): experts were asked to "place Las Vegas-style bets on the probability of each of the different scenarios". "Without ever having gone to sea, the team now believed they knew where the bomb was." This is self-reported, and **no source shows the map directing Alvin to the find**.
- **Later assessments:**
  - The Navy wanted statistical proof that the bomb was *not* there, and "the prior probabilities of the hypotheses were never updated despite search data" (S96 snippet ⚑).
  - *Nature* (2011) reportedly says Bayes "was not needed" (S96, unverified ⚑).
  - **USS *Scorpion*, 1968:** Richardson's prior map put the wreck "within 200 yards of the highest-probability cell" (S94 ✓ T4). After Palomares "the U.S. Navy started secretly developing Bayesian theory to find objects underwater" (S95).
- **Safe VO:** "One of the first big real-world uses of probability-based search: John Craven's probability maps and Tony Richardson's nightly maths. It didn't point to the bomb. It measured the search, kept the brass on board, and grew into the method that found the USS *Scorpion*."

### 6a. The nine days of real doubt (keeps "Twice" honest)

- **Primary:** "Equipment problems and **the loss of contact with the bomb for 9 days** delayed final recovery." (S02 p.10 ✓) The DNA report says "much anxiety for a period of 8 days" (S01 p.94 ✓). ⚠ D18.
- George Martin (Trieste pilot): "Here we were in the ninth inning, and the score is zero to zero. And the fans—we'll call that the world opinion—was also at zero." (S40 ⚑)
- Moody "felt personally responsible" (S40 ⚑).
- The press had already raised the risk: the Washington Post on 18 Mar wrote that "a wrong nudge could send it rolling into even deeper water". The LA Times on 22 Mar ran "H-Bomb May Slip into Deep Sea Crevice, Balk Recovery" (S87 ⚑).
- Guest's memo of 15 Mar to the Vance board warned the weapon could lie "completely buried in the mud, invisible" (S40 ⚑).
- Robert Sproull, who chaired the Search Evaluation Board, said he was picked because he was "expendable". "It was pretty clear that if the fourth one was not found, there'd be a congressional investigation, and mud all over the face of everyone." The board's job was to judge whether the Navy could abandon the search (S40 ⚑).
- **29 Mar:** *Albany*'s deck log reads "**Commenced handling TALOS missile warheads.**" Moran says, via a gunner's mate, that these were **nuclear** warheads, and hedges "to the best of the author's knowledge" (S40 from S27 ⚑). **Word it as "according to a crew member".**

(S88 via S40 ch.15 only ⚑; no CBS transcript reached) Duke's premature release draft of 18 Mar: "The fourth and final weapon… has been recovered today." (S40 ⚑)

## 7. The re-find and the lift

### 7a. CURV

- **24 Mar:** Adm. Swanson ordered CURV to Spain the same day as the failed lift. The date is inferred from Moran's context ("on March 24, as soon as… the first lift attempt failed… Admiral Swanson ordered CURV to Spain"); the team flew out on 26 Mar and set up on **USS *Petrel*** on **29 Mar** (S40 ch.16 only ⚑; S41 confirms just that the Navy chose CURV and it "attached grapnels to the chute").
- **Name:** Cable-controlled Underwater **Research** Vehicle (S40). ⚠ The museum says "Recovery" (S98).
- **Origin and record:** NOTS Pasadena. It had recovered 52 torpedoes from as deep as 2,000 ft.
- **Size:** about 5 × 6 × 13 ft.
- **Tether:** spliced from ~2,000 to **3,100 ft**. The 55 conductors were spliced one by one and sealed with a duct goo the crew called "monkey shit". Larry Brady: "It looked like a python had swallowed a dog. Or a couple of dogs." (S40 ⚑)
- **Limits:** practical limit ~2,800 ft. Below 2,000 ft the depth gauge was unreliable and the altimeter was dead. Test runs found a dummy bomb at 1,050 ft and a pinger at 2,400 ft (S40 ⚑).

### 7b. Re-find, 2 April

- **Primary:** "on 2 April the object, still chute enshrouded, was located on a bearing of 210° T., a distance of 120 yards from its position on 15 March and now at a depth of 2800 feet." (S01 p.94 ✓) ⚠ Other depths: 2,900 ft (S98, USAF *Nuclear Safety* via S100); 2,990 ft / 910 m (one WHOI page).
- **Crew:** Rainnie, McCamis and observer George Martin. Guest refused the seat: "no way he was diving in a submersible, especially one built by civilians." Searching **downslope**, McCamis saw "a clod of dirt that seemed out of place", then the chute "still tightly wrapped around an object". The search took "just over a half hour" (S40 ⚑).
- **Colour:** Martin had all three sign a **100-peseta note**, ate a peanut-butter-and-jelly sandwich and wrote to his wife (S40 ⚑).
- **Moody:** "**How many bombs do we have down there? Let's just go get her, but do a better job this time.**" (S40 ⚑)
- **3 Apr:** two pingers and a transponder attached. The bomb was code-named "**Robert**", after Guest's stepson (S40, Richardson diary ⚑).

### 7c. Hooking, dragging, fouling (4–7 April) ⚑ S40 throughout

- **4 Apr:** CURV went in just before 9 am and reached the bomb around noon. Brady dug the grapnel through the chute's **apex**.
  - The grapnel was proof-tested to **10,000 lb**, on **3,200 ft of braided nylon** masking-taped to CURV's umbilical and buoyed off at the surface.
  - ⚠ The Navy report says three tines; Brady says four.
  - Brady: the earlier crew were like "two guys flipped their car into a ditch, tried to tow it out on their own, and burned up the clutch… CURV was the tow truck."
- **Three lines:** Guest demanded **three** lines before he would lift. Bob Pace: "Admiral, you got that parachute ballooning in the water now. It's going to be difficult enough to get another grapnel into it without tangling it." Guest: "I can't help that."
- **5 Apr:**
  - The buoyed line had dragged the bomb "several hundred feet west" to **~2,850 ft**.
  - McCamis: "This sixty-four-foot cargo chute was billowing up like you was under a Barnum and Bailey circus tent." The chute also "danced in the water like a giant jellyfish".
  - Wilson panicked that they were inside the chute: "Scared him dead… I couldn't shut him up quick enough." Rainnie ordered Alvin up.
  - **Just before midnight**, CURV attached a **second line**, snaring at least six shroud lines. The bomb "shimmied farther downslope".
- **6 Apr:** 22-kn winds and 5-ft seas. Most men had been awake for 30+ hours, and Guest stood the crews down until evening.
- **~01:00, 7 Apr:** "Around 1 a.m., CURV dove to attach the third and final line." The fouling happened during that dive; Moran gives no exact time for "We're fouled." Single source (Moran interviews); attribute "as told to Barbara Moran". S41 and Wikipedia confirm only that CURV got tangled in the chute.
  - Guest asked Brady to fly in closer so he could see the bomb. Brady: "We might get stuck, but we'll sure give it a try." The starboard thruster breaker kept popping. Brady: "**We're fouled.**"
  - ⚠ Some accounts say CURV was driven into the chute deliberately. Brady and Pace say it was an accident. Some versions put Guest in the wardroom watching on closed-circuit TV.
- **The wardroom row:**
  - Guest wanted to cut CURV's cable, buoy it off and come back later. CURV division head **Howard Tarkington fainted** (also in S60).
  - Chief of staff Cliff Page cleared the room so Lt **Brad Mooney** could argue with Guest alone for hours. Mooney: "**Admiral, this is dumb as hell to cut this thing loose. It's totally enmeshed in there, and they can't help but lift it now.**"
  - Moody and Mooney's argument: CURV itself was the third line.
- **05:02:** Guest messaged Gen. Wilson that the admiral "**had a broken leg**", the code for "lift imminent" (S24 p.95 via S40).

### 7d. The lift, 7 April (Petrel deck log and S24 via S40 ⚑; date ✓ S01, S02)

- **Rig:** Harrell's blocks and pulleys shared the load equally between the **two lines** on **one capstan**, with a dynamometer. A second capstan reeled in CURV's umbilical at the same speed.
- **Station-keeping:** *Petrel* was held over the bomb by **two Mike boats**, bow and stern, directed by walkie-talkie. The ship's own propeller was off and the leeway was **50 yd**. The sea was a flat calm ("little breeze and a calm sea"), which made holding position harder.
- **On deck:** Moody cleared "tourists" off the fantail. An EOD diver on what an explosion would leave: "nothing left but a greasy stain."
- **Times (05:50, 08:46, 10:15) and the deck-log wording come only from Moran's reading of the Petrel log** (S40 notes p.202, citing S27); the log itself is unseen. Depth ~2,850 ft rests on S40 and the NRDC photo caption; DNA gives 2,800 ft for the 2 Apr re-find.
- **05:50:** the lift begins. Guest feared vibration near the bottom and the surface could cut the nylon's strength "by as much as 75 percent". Guest: "**I'd prefer combat any day to this.**" (press conference, 8 Apr)
- **Coming up:** it rose "so smoothly that they hardly noticed it leaving the bottom". After **1 h 45 min** the top of the chute broke the surface. Two of Moody's divers went in. The bomb was tangled about a third of the way up the chute. They rigged straps, **cut CURV free**, and the cargo boom swung it inboard. ⚠ S41 says they paused at **100 ft** for frogmen.
- **08:46:** "**Weapon on deck with parachute.**" (Petrel deck log) Radiation readings negative.
  - ⚠ Lines: "three lifting lines" (S98) vs two lines plus CURV (S40, S41).
- **Render-safe:** the thermal battery was jammed by water pressure. EOD's **Walter Funston** drilled it, drove in a **3-in wood screw**, and twisted it out with a strap around a shackle (a "Spanish windlass"). **Declared safe at 10:15.**
- **Condition:** "The tail section was torn and jagged, the parachute twisted and fouled, the nose dented as if punched by a giant fist… a portion of the bomb rack still attached." Today the casing is at NMNSH, Albuquerque, stencilled "**CAUTION. DO NOT BUMP**" (S40, S41; S97).
- **Afterwards:** Guest: "**Thank God we finally did it.**" (S24 p.96 via S40) There was no cheering. Strips of the parachute became autographed souvenirs. Herman Kunz's suitcase of 12 bottles of Gilbey's gin, and the "prescription" dose for Mooney, make a light coda.

### 7e. 8 April: the bomb shown to the press

- **Good Friday** (Easter was 10 Apr). **Per Moran only**, about **100 newsmen** were ferried from Garrucha to USS *Albany*. The State history is smaller: "a small number of press representatives were brought alongside the recovery ship for a glimpse", with a briefing for the whole press corps (S02 ✓). On screen, say "the press" without a number, or attribute the 100 to Moran. *Petrel* passed about **35 yd** away for about **20 min**, with the bomb on wooden chocks. Alvin, Aluminaut and Cubmarine sailed past in review (S40 from S26 pp.344–345 ⚑).
- **Primary record:** "Permission to photograph the bomb after recovery, **a completely unprecedented action**, was given on 26 March." (S01 p.202 ✓) The DNA phrase describes the permission granted on 26 Mar, not the 8 Apr event itself. "Press representatives photographed it freely." (S01 ✓) The State history: "A small number of press representatives were brought alongside the recovery ship for a glimpse of the bomb." (S02 p.11 ✓)
- **"First time the US displayed a nuclear weapon in public"** (S40; NYT 9 Apr per S87 ⚑).
- **Why it was shown:** the military had wanted to "raise the bomb in secret, pack it into a box, and ship it… under cover of darkness". Duke phoned McNamara, who agreed the find "had to be verified" (S40, Duke oral history ⚑).
- **Serial number:** taped over on McNamara's orders (S69 ⚑).
- **Kalb (CBS):** it lay "under the Mediterranean sun as if it were a bathing beauty posing for photographers… For a multimegaton monster, it looks extremely dull." (S88 ⚑)
- **Guest:** "I'm just about out on my feet." He couldn't remember the Alvin crew's names. Duke: "Admiral, you have the gratitude of grateful countrymen, a grateful host country, and in fact the gratitude of the world." (S40 ⚑)
- **Departure:** "At the request of the Government of Spain… the recovery ship with its atomic cargo immediately departed for the United States without docking at any Spanish port." (S02 ✓) Muñoz Grandes "didn't want the bomb to touch Spanish soil". It went home on USS *Cascade* to Pantex, Amarillo (S40, S100 ⚑).

### 8b. The swim, 8 March 1966 ✓

- **Primary:** "On March 8 Ambassador Duke, Information Minister Fraga, and members of their families went swimming in the sea off Palomares to demonstrate the safety of the area for tourists." Duke "conceived the idea of attending the opening [of a new hotel at Mojácar] and taking a swim. Joined by most of his staff, he took the plunge into the icy waters in the morning. Later that afternoon, Fraga and Duke took a second swim." (S02 p.9 ✓)
- **Date:** the Parador de Mojácar opened "el 8 de marzo de 1966" (S35 ✓). ⚠ Some Spanish outlets say 7 Mar (D25).
- **Swim 1, Mojácar parador beach, morning.** Duke wore "swim trunks, loafers, a blue bathrobe, and a bathing cap" in **54 °F** water. "Okay, let's go!" Afterwards, asked "Did you detect any radioactivity in the water?": "**If this is radioactivity, I love it!**" Asked if he had seen the bomb: "I wish I had!" (S88 "US Envoy Swims in Mediterranean", CBS 9 Mar 1966, via S40 ch.13 n.155; the quote also appears in S61, so two outlets ✓/⚑). **It belongs to the morning Mojácar swim, without Fraga — don't place it after the afternoon Palomares swim.** The 54 °F figure is Moran's only (NYT gives no temperature)
- **Swim 2, Quitapellejos / Camp Wilson beach, Palomares, afternoon.** Duke and Fraga swam together, with two big US landing craft as the backdrop. EFE president Carlos Sentís swam too, while the journalists watched from shore (S82 ⚑ T3).
- **Upstaging anecdote ⚠ (D26).** Aide Tim Towell: "He wants to swim alone… He'll be dipped if he's going to share this with the American ambassador. This is his thing." Towell then raced into the divers' tent: "The American ambassador needs a bathing suit… We gotta go swimming instantly, it's an emergency!" Duke wore a borrowed wet trunk Towell called a "little damp jock strap". "Fraga's been had, so what's he to do?… And in they go together." (S40 from a 2007 interview, S45 p.204, Red Moody, and footage. S43 gives a similar 1995 account.) Fraga denied it "without much vigor". **Label it "according to the ambassador's aide".**

The Mojácar parador is ~13 km south of Palomares (coordinates in §11), while sources say 8 to 15 km ⚠. **The plutonium was in the soil, not the sea** (S82; Que.es S114). Bomb 4 was intact: "The casing was intact and no release of radioactivity into the coastal waters has occurred" (official recovery statement, quoted in S40). ### 8e. Long tail, 1966–69

- **Claims:** TIME (S64, 24 Jan 1969): **644 claims** filed, **528 settled for about $700,000** against $7,839,519 claimed, 98 rejected, 7 dropped, 11 outstanding. The US offered a $150,000 desalination plant. Half of the 2,000 residents had left. Tomato crops failed six times running; villagers blamed radioactivity, and TIME notes drought and brackish water. A villager was paid $66 a month for daily Geiger readings. ⚠ Other figures: $555,456.45 to 500+ claimants by 26 Sep 1966 (S40). 522 claims and $600,000 by 1969 (other). ~$250 per person (S80).
- Ramirez on fear: "How do you value anxiety?" (S40 ⚑)
- Villagers were asked to sign waivers accepting compensation as final (S57 via S68 ⚑).
- State history: "The population of the village declined rapidly as the soil became increasingly alkaline… tomatoes." (S02 p.11 ✓). Spanish officers asked that claims stay open 10–20 years because effects "might not be fully revealed for many years" (S02 p.13 ✓).
- **Duchess of Medina Sidonia** ("the Red Duchess", Luisa Isabel Álvarez de Toledo): she led a villagers' march toward Madrid and the US Embassy; police stopped it ~200 yd out of Palomares. The Tribunal de Orden Público gave her a 1-year sentence and a 10,000-peseta fine; she was jailed in Mar 1969 and served ~8 months. "My offense lay in the fact that I had openly sided with people who did not enjoy the privileges of the nobility." (S02 p.11 ✓ "initially spearheaded by"; S83, S100, S115 ⚑ ⚠ D29)
- **Hall–Otero Agreement, 25 Feb 1966** (founded Project Indalo): research on "el comportamiento fisiológico y ecológico del óxido de plutonio en una zona rural previamente contaminada y que haya sido descontaminada" (the physiological and ecological behaviour of plutonium oxide in a rural area that was contaminated and then decontaminated) (S10, S14 ✓). Critics call it a living laboratory.
- **1967:** Duke said the accident showed Spaniards "the American military presence in Spain was not without serious risks to Spain". He pushed the desalination plant as a goodwill gift (S03 d207 ✓).
- **Hollywood:** on 14 Apr 1966 the DoD turned down Samuel Bronston's request to film Palomares. The letter is known only in Spanish translation, so paraphrase it (S43 ⚑).
- **1976:** "the Embassy braced for major demonstrations… None took place." (S02 p.13 ✓)
- **Declassified lines worth quoting (S02 ✓):**
  - "Both the United States and Spanish Governments were eager to eliminate the memories of Palomares in order to assure the continued growth of this key element [tourism] in Spain's development." (p.14)
  - The 1985 foreword calls overseas deployments a "fact of life" and warns of "atrophying of expertise".
  - Lesson: "the United States should seek to provide the press with all available information as quickly as possible." (p.3)
  - At Thule, John Leddy "pointedly cited the bad precedents created by press censorship at Palomares." (p.18)

### 8f. Broken Arrow

- "Broken Arrow is the code term used in notifications of nuclear accidents." The commander's notifications "set the 'Broken Arrow' response system in motion." (S01 p.17 ✓)
- Later DoD definitions (DoDD 5230.16) and the related terms Nucflash, Bent Spear and Empty Quiver: ⚑ via S100. Cite the directive if shown on screen.
- "Airborne Alert was finally terminated in 1968 due to the implementation of ballistic missiles, rising costs, and –not least of all– nuclear accidents, most notably Thule." (S92 ⚑)

### 8g. Deep-submergence lessons

- After the Thresher loss (10 Apr 1963, 129 dead) came the Deep Submergence Systems Project. ⚠ It was created in 1964 (S40) or June 1965 (Navy, S100).
- At Palomares it was "not ready". Trieste was "in bits and pieces in San Diego". Craven said the DSSP had "almost nothing". Mooney: "a ragtag pickup team." (S40 ⚑)
- **Irony:** Under Secretary Baldwin told a DSSP conference on 11–12 Jan 1966, days before the crash, that it "is here—today—to serve both the Navy and the national interest." (S40 ⚑)
- **Legacy:** DSRV-1 *Mystic* launched 24 Jan 1970 (⚑). Los Alamos developed insensitive high explosive after Palomares (S40 ⚑). Craven to WaPo: "Minisubs may some day be as common under the sea as planes streaking over it." (S40 ⚑)

## 10. The legacy today (status ⏱ as of 21 Oct 2026)

### 10a. Rediscovery, fences, residual plutonium

- **1980s:** irrigated farming and tourism boomed. Reservoirs of 151,600 m³ (1986) and 347,000 m³ (1988) were dug at the head of the bomb-2 valley (8.5 ha), and terraces were cut. The bomb-3 site (0.5 ha) was levelled, and land was reclassified as buildable (S10, S16 ✓).
- **1998–2002:** CIEMAT found contamination "superior a la estimada inicialmente" ("higher than first estimated") (S11 ✓).
- **Dec 2001 CSN criteria:** total restriction at ≥5 mSv/yr (Am-241 >5 Bq/g); partial restriction at ≥1 mSv/yr (1–5 Bq/g); top 15 cm (S11 ✓).
- **17 Dec 2004:** PIEM-VR approved and declared of public utility, which allows expropriation (S11 ✓).
- **Expropriation and fences (corrected):** in **1998–2002** CIEMAT "realizó diferentes gestiones encaminadas a la expropiación forzosa de 10 ha" (took steps toward the compulsory expropriation of 10 ha). On 17 Dec 2004 the Council of Ministers approved expropriation and use restrictions; characterisation work began in 2006 (S119). A further temporary-occupation proposal was "informada favorablemente por el CSN el **2 de julio de 2007**" (a favourable CSN report, not a CSN decision), and the **Council of Ministers on 28 Sep 2007** extended it to "an additional 30 hectares" (S119), for **40 ha in total**. CIEMAT "delimitó con una valla todos ellos… y colocó unos carteles informativos" ("fenced all of them off and put up information signs") (S11 ✓). It is **wrong** to say CIEMAT occupied 10 ha in 2006. NYT says "In 2004, Spain quietly fenced off" (D15). **Say "fenced off in the mid-2000s".** Whether the fence still stands in Oct 2026 rests on T5 regional press only (S71, S73, S74) ⏱⚑.
- **Zone 6 fence**, completed **27 Jun 2011** after the Euratom Art. 35 mission of 20–21 Apr 2010: 1,500 m long, galvanised posts ~4 m apart and 2 m high set in cement, 2-m woven-wire game fencing, and two double gates each 2 × 5 m (S11 ✓).
- **3D survey, Nov 2006–Mar 2009,** cost shared with DOE: **660 ha** walked on a 25×25 m GPS grid, and the 40 ha on a 1×1 m grid. **321 boreholes** to 5–6 m, 734 samples. Pu/Am ratio ~4. IAEA peer review in 2009 was positive (S10, S12 ✓).

### 10b. Monitoring of villagers (Project Indalo)

- **As of the 2013 CSN report**, **1,077 people** had been examined, with **5,029 medical and internal-dosimetry exams** (24-h urine), about 150 a year since 1966. These are cumulative to ~2012–13, not current; the report doesn't use the name "Project Indalo" (that name comes from the 1966 Hall–Otero agreement, S10, S14). More than 9,500 farm samples and more than 12,500 analyses since 1967. Ingestion dose for 1995–2010: **0.006 mSv/yr** against a 1 mSv limit (S11 ✓).
- CSN: "no han mostrado la existencia de morbilidad inducida por la contaminación y no hay ninguna evidencia de patologías causadas por el accidente" ("they have shown no illness induced by the contamination and there is no evidence of disease caused by the accident") (S11 ✓). One stated aim of the programme was to "mitigate the concern and anxiety of the population through the psychological effect of medical examination" (S12 ✓).
- **Funding:**
  - The US paid ~15% for decades (S61).
  - 1997 annex: 25%, up to $300,000 (S14 ✓).
  - 2007 annex: $383k (2006), $750k (2007), $850k (2008) (S14 ✓).
  - **US funding ended in 2009** (S10 ✓).
- **Health questions (attribute):**
  - Early-1970s field team: "a lone graduate student".
  - Two child leukaemia deaths were never investigated. A 1976 request for "medical surveillance of the population" was not acted on (S61).
  - DNA notes "one local boy died six years later of 'cancer of the blood'" (S63).
  - A 2005 mortality study found higher cancer rates, possibly due to random error.
  - Yolanda Benito (CIEMAT): "there is nothing that allows us to draw a relationship…" (S61 ⚑).

### 11.1 Bomb (B28 / Mk 28)

- **Build at ~3.7 m long × 0.51–0.56 m diameter, ~1,050 kg** (±0.3 m by variant). S06 ✓ gives FI 145 in, 22 in, 2,350 lb and RI 132 in, 2,265 lb. Gibson via S104 gives RI 12 ft 3 in, FI 12 ft 1.25 in. Moran says "twelve feet long and twenty inches around".
- **Surface:** unpainted dull aluminium with stencils and white rectangular tape patches. Rounded "frangible false nose", four tail fins, circular tail plate holding the chute pack (✓ visual, S111 "Palomares H-Bomb Incident.jpg"; S40).
- **Bomb #4 as recovered:** tail torn and jagged, nose dented "as if punched by a giant fist", part of the bomb rack still attached. Navy caption: "note missing tail fins and damage to frangible 'false nose'". Sat on wooden chocks on *Petrel*'s fantail. On surfacing it hung "tangled about a third of the way up" the chute (S40, S111).
- **Bomb #1:** 9-in nose gash, 3 of 4 fins gone, a chute spilled nearby (S40).
- **Carriage:** four Mk 28s in a clip-in assembly (S111 "Mark 28 Thermonuclear Bomb.jpg", PD USAF).
- **Weight:** ⚠ Moran says "more than two tons" for the Alvin scene, a figure that includes the rig. The bomb alone (FI) is 2,350 lb ≈ **1,070 kg ≈ 1.2 short tons**: say "more than a ton", not "about one ton" and not "two tons".

### 11.2 Parachute

- **64-ft solid canopy** (S06 ✓; Maydew via Moran: "the 64-foot solid-canopy parachute"; NMNSH caption: "wrapped in 64-ft parachute"), **off-white or dirty yellow nylon** (Wendorf's recollection via S40 ⚑: "Bomb chutes are more of an off white or dirty yellow. Wendorf had seen an off white chute." No T1 source for the colour). Simó: "grey and white" (S44). Tarzan saw "a large white double parachute". The B&W recovery photos show a pale canopy ✓ visual. **Not orange-and-white.**
- **Behaviour on the seabed:** "billowing up like… a Barnum and Bailey circus tent"; "danced in the water like a giant jellyfish" (5 Apr). On 15 Mar it "lay" over the bomb, and Wilson saw "a fin… underneath the parachute".

### 11.3 Seabed and the 24 March set

- **Darkness:** at 777–870 m there is **no sunlight**. The winch started ~7:30 pm (around sunset, ~19:20 by our calculation) and the bomb was lifted and lost ~9–9:20 pm, in darkness. **Alvin was not present.** Any Alvin lamp cone or daylight falloff is licence. The one real light source was **POODL's strobe** (S40).
- **Slope:** the find was on a "**70 degree slope**" (S01; the pilot's impression ⚠). The re-find was on "a gently sloping plain". The 400-ft furrow ran down the slope. Bottom: "gray and featureless", visibility ~20 ft, silt clouds hanging 15–20 min (S40).
- **Rig:** grapnel → 300-ft line → anchor → POODL (7-ft steel "giant shuttlecock", 38 ft above the anchor) → 3½-in-circumference (~1.1-in diameter) braided nylon → *Mizar*'s moon pool. The break was **below the anchor**, a frayed stump three-quarters cut clean.
- **Bathymetry** (S38, inference ⚠, label it "reconstruction"):
  - From Quitapellejos (37.2325 N, 1.7954 W), the shelf runs 0–80 m deep out to ~5 km.
  - The shelf break (200 m) is at 5–6 km on bearings 110–160°.
  - The **777–870 m** band (2,550–2,850 ft) is first reached **8.9–9.3 km out on bearings ~125–147°**, in a canyon at **37.165–37.185 N, 1.71–1.74 W**. Gridded walls are 27–29° at ~195 m sampling, so the true local slope is steeper.
  - The canyon head bites in to ~280 m only ~5 km offshore (37.21 N, 1.74 W).
  - This matches CSN's 8 km (S10), CIEMAT's ~9 km (S12) and Melson's 5½ mi (S52 via S100).
  - It is named the "Río Almanzora canyon" (S100 ⚑).
  - Grid file: `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-better-videos/79ab24ef-785f-53ed-8c76-d7f9915739fd/scratchpad/dl/palomares_bathy_grid.csv` (scratch; copy it into `projects/palomares/assets/` before the session ends).
- **Sandia IBM 7090 estimate:** "37° 13.9' N, 01° 42.3' W", about 8 km ESE of Quitapellejos, near the real site. This contradicts Moran's gloss "closer to Africa than to Spain" (S40 ⚠, possibly a transcription issue).

## 13. Do not claim

- That **Alvin was yellow**, or that Alvin's lamps lit the 24 Mar loss. Alvin was white, and it was on the surface; the lift was at night. (Do not claim "there was no light at all" either: POODL carried a strobe.)
- That **Cubmarine was "the" yellow sub** at Palomares (Deep Jeep was yellow too), or the **1982** sail-repaint year on screen (unverified).
- That the bomb's chute was **orange-and-white**.
- That the bomb went "**300 ft deeper**". Use 250 ft (DNA).
- That Simó found or showed "**the exact spot**", or that "**nobody listened**". Also do not say "the search stayed on land".
- That **the maths/Bayesian search found the bomb**, or that Craven "**invented**" it.
- That the swim was "miles from" Palomares. It was at Palomares's own beach (plus Mojácar). Only bomb 4 was miles away.

- **"70-kiloton bombs"** (S107). **"B28RI"/"B28FI"** printed on screen.
