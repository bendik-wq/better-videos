# Notre-Dame: research and fact pack

This pack is for EP10, "Notre-Dame Was 30 Minutes From Collapse", a fully animated Paper & Spring explainer (`docs/library/animation-study/STYLE-BIBLE.md`) about the fire of 15–16 April 2019. It covers the alarm that sent a guard to the wrong attic, the half-hour before the brigade was called, the spire, the second clock on the north-tower bell frame, the twenty firefighters, the vault, and the rebuild.

It was compiled **2026-11-05** from four researcher passes (timeline, structure, firefight, after), followed by two adversarial verification passes. **The script is written from this pack, never from memory.** Every spoken or on-screen number needs a row here. Source IDs (S01…) point to `SOURCES.md`, which gives full citations, URLs, tiers and read status.

**Tiers (channel rule for this episode)**
- **T1** primary record or official body: Paris prosecutor, BSPP, Élysée/government, Senate/Assemblée, Ministry of Culture/DRAC, Établissement public Rebâtir Notre-Dame, Ville de Paris, CMN, Santé publique France, Diocese of Paris (church record)
- **T3** on-record words: briefings, interviews, signed expert articles (Épaud, Heyman)
- **T5** quality press: NYT, Le Monde, AFP, AP, Reuters, Le Parisien, Le Figaro, Libération, franceinfo, Public Sénat, Belga, The Art Newspaper and similar
- **T6** Wikipedia and aggregators: **pointer only**. Nothing goes on screen on T6 alone.

Format "T3 via T5" means on-record words (e.g. the prosecutor) known through a press report.

**Markers**
- ✓ verified in a source a researcher or verifier opened.
- ⚑ secondary, single-source, snippet-only, or known only through someone else's summary. Resolve it or hedge it before lock.
- ⚠ disputed: sources conflict. Both versions are given here and in §9.
- ⏱ still moving (the judicial investigation, fundraising, final works). Re-check on VO-lock day and publish day.

**The NYT rule.** The NYT reconstruction of 16 Jul 2019 (S40) was **not** opened on nytimes.com (paywall). All of it was read in licensed syndication: NZ Herald (S41, the main copy), Yahoo (S42) and SBS (S43). Cite it on screen as "NYT, 16 Jul 2019". In this pack, "NYT (S41)" means that syndicated text. The NYT's 19 Apr 2019 piece (S47) was not opened at all; we know it through Il Post (S48).

**The Le Monde rule.** Le Monde's ~31 May 2019 investigation (S50) was not opened. We know it through JDD's summary (S51). Cite it as "Le Monde, reported by JDD".

**Blame rule (G6, non-negotiable).** The cause is **undetermined** (⏱, §6a). Never blame, name or picture as at fault the Elytis panel employee, the cathedral guard, Elytis, the scaffolding firm or the workers. Every fault in this pack is framed as **system design**: a zone label that put two attics in one string, a two-step human check, no automatic call to the brigade, a slow-burn assumption, no fire breaks in the roof.

**Access note.** Blocked or unopened in this environment: nytimes.com (paywall), lemonde.fr (S50), theguardian.com (S69), x.com (402), Europe 1 (403), artdaily.com (now 404) and Borneo Post (403) for the AFP night reconstruction (S59), hal.science (Anubis wall, for the verifier only; the researcher opened both Épaud papers), images.cnrs.fr (503).

## 2. The alarm: how a working system produced a half-hour (system design)

### 2a. What the system was ✓
- 2.1 The attic had **aspirating smoke detection**: tubes with tiny holes ran through the roof space, and at one end of each tube was "an 'aspirating' detector — a highly sensitive device that draws in air" (S41 ✓). **No source names a brand.** Say "aspirating smoke detectors", never VESDA/Xtralis.
- 2.2 More than 160 detectors and manual alarms covered the complex. ZDA-110-3-15-1 was "code for a specific smoke detector" (S41 ✓, verifier upgrade). The complex "was divided into four" zones (S41 ✓, re-opened 2026-11-06).
- 2.3 The system "took dozens of experts six years to put together" and involved "thousands of pages of diagrams, maps, spreadsheets and contracts" (S41 ✓). The NYT judged the result "a system so arcane" that when it was called upon to warn of fire and say where, it produced a nearly indecipherable message (S41 ✓).
- 2.4 **It alerted people, not the brigade.** By design a person had to confirm the fire before anyone phoned the fire brigade (S41, S44, S48 ✓). It **detected early**. The weak links were the message and the human-verification design. Do not say "the alarm failed".
- 2.5 The security post was in the **presbytery**, a small separate building, in "the pale-green walls of the tiny presbytery room" (S41 ✓; night guard "logé dans le presbytère", S03 T1 ✓).

### 2b. How it was staffed (frame as staffing design)
- 2.6 Official framework (S03 T1 ✓): the convention of 19 Dec 2014 named the Architecte des bâtiments de France (conservator) the "responsable unique de sécurité". The DRAC funded **one SSIAP-trained agent at the PC every day of the year, plus a night guard**.
- 2.7 ⚠ Two-agent vs one-agent. Le Monde: after the 2014 contract, Elytis went "rapidement" from two agents (screen + rounds) to one, backed by a cathedral employee trained in basic fire safety (S51). A deputy's written question claimed the tender "prévoyait le passage de deux agents à un seul"; the ministry's answer neither confirms nor denies (S03). NYT 19 Apr via Il Post: "almeno due persone di guardia" (S48). **Reconciled by the verifier:** they agree once both people are counted. Script: "**one person at the screen, one guard to do the checking**".
- 2.8 The panel employee was on the second leg of a **double shift** because his relief hadn't come (NYT "since 7am"; Le Monde first shift from 7:30, second due to end at 23:00) ⚠ → "**since early morning, on a double shift**". Tenure ⚠ → "**only a few days into the job**". **Never name him.**

### 2c. The message ✓

## 3. The structure: what was burning, and what held

### 3a. The "forest" (roof frames)
- 3.1 Until 2019 the choir and nave attics held their **13th-century oak frames**. The frames around the spire and both transept arms were rebuilt by Lassus and Viollet-le-Duc in the mid-19th century (Épaud, S31, S32 ✓ researcher-opened; verifier blocked).
- 3.2 Three Gothic frames were lost: the first choir frame (c.1185, timber reused), the nave (c.1215), and the choir rebuilt c.1226 on the nave's model, reusing 12th-century timbers "massivement" (S31, S32 ⚑; Épaud: the 1995 dendro dates "restent imprécises"). Label them "about".
- 3.3 About **1,000 oaks** ("autour de 1 000 chênes"). More than nine in ten trunks were only 25–30 cm thick and ≤12 m long, from young, fast-grown oaks of 60–80 years (S31 ✓; 93% in S31, 96% in S32 ⚠). « On est donc bien loin de l'image d'Epinal des énormes chênes… »
- 3.4 Each beam was a whole oak squared **with an axe while green**, heart in the centre; no saws, no years of drying (S31 ✓).
- 3.5 The nickname "forêt" comes from the **density of bracing pieces**, the newest techniques of the 1220s piled up, not from a vast felled forest (S32 ✓).
- 3.6 Burning rates: the safety plan assumed slow-burning oak (§2.15).

### 3b. Lead ✓
- 3.7 « la destruction de la toiture en plomb de 210 tonnes qui reposait sur la charpente… et des 250 tonnes de plomb recouvrant la flèche ». The lead "a en partie fondu sur l'extrados des voûtes, à l'intérieur de la cathédrale et sur certaines parties des élévations" (S04 T1 ✓). The deputy's question gives the 460 t total.

### 3c. The spire, statues and rooster
- 3.8 Viollet-le-Duc's spire was oak covered in lead. Work began 1859 and took 18 months, with carpenter Bellu and plumber Durand (S23 ✓). Ville de Paris: "culmine à 93 mètres", 500 t oak + 250 t lead, inaugurated 15 Aug 1859; with the rooster the summit reaches 96 m (S19 ✓). Say "1859" only (the exact date is ⚠).
- 3.9 The **16 copper statues** (12 apostles + 4 evangelist symbols) were lifted off on **Thursday 11 April 2019**, four days before the fire, by Socra with an 80 m crane, for restoration near Périgueux (S07 ✓; S09 confirms "déposées quelques jours avant le sinistre"). They were saved by **scheduled restoration timing, not by fire foresight**. Each is ~150 kg and ~3.40 m, modelled by Adolphe Geoffroy-Dechaume and installed 1861. St Thomas has Viollet-le-Duc's face and looks up at the spire (S08 ✓, not re-checked).
- 3.10 The old rooster weighed 30 kg; the new one weighs 25 kg and is 90 cm (S19 ✓). Relics: a fragment of the Crown of Thorns, St Denis and St Geneviève (S25 ✓). Placement 1935 (Cardinal Verdier, S77; the Diocese gives "restored 1935") vs 1860 (WP) ⚠. Found 16 Apr (§1a).

### 3d. The stone vault (peak 10)
- 3.11 Heyman: "one function of the stone vault is to provide a fire-resistant barrier between the outer roof and the church"; "the falling timbers were largely caught by the masonry vaults"; "It seems that collapse of the vaults occurred in only three locations" (S33 T3 ✓). The three: the last bay of the north transept, one bay of the nave, and the near-square vault at the crossing, all from falling sections of the spire (S33).
- 3.12 DRAC: three heaps of rubble on the floor of the nave, the crossing and the north transept arm. « tous les effondrements de voûte ont été causés par un choc consécutif à la chute d'éléments de charpente, et non par les effets du feu » (S09 ✓). The heaps held vault stones, charred timber mostly from the spire and its tabouret, and metal from ridge crests, the spire's armature, and the forks that had held the statues (S09 ✓).
- 3.13 The vault stayed at risk afterwards (shaken adjacent areas, heat damage). On 25 Jul 2019 two blocks fell into the nave nets from the hole left by the spire top, more precisely the metal "corbeille" under the summit cross (S09 ✓).
- 3.14 Vault thickness ~12–15 cm in the choir and ~19–24 cm in the nave (S34 snippet, S114 not opened) is **UNSUPPORTED**: ⚑, off screen, or "about a hand-span" with ⚑.

### 3e. The towers and bells (the second clock)
- 3.15 Both west towers are **69 m**. The visitor climb is 424 steps, up the south tower (S20 ✓; "down the north" and the older figure 422 not on that page).
- 3.16 The north tower's **wooden belfry** holds **8 bells**, installed in 2013 for the 850th anniversary (S09 ✓). Weights run from 782 kg (Jean-Marie, S17 ✓) to just over 4 t (Gabriel, ">4.1 t", S92). The AFP range "780 kg to 4 t" is ⚑ (snippet). Names (S80 ✓): Gabriel, Anne-Geneviève, Denis, Marcel, Étienne, Benoît-Joseph, Maurice, Jean-Marie. **No per-bell kg on screen** (Maurice is 1,011 kg on WP vs 1,101 kg on Paris Zigzag ⚠).
- 3.17 The **south** tower holds the two bourdons: **Emmanuel** (1686), "13 tonnes" (13.3 t Paris.fr; "plus de 13 tonnes" CMN), 500 kg clapper; and **Marie** (2012), ~6 t (S19, S20, S23, S99 ✓). Emmanuel hangs in an oak frame that damps its vibration (S23).
- 3.18 **The danger mechanism, in on-record words.** José Vaz de Matos (Culture ministry fire expert, AP 17 Apr 2019):
    - "If the fire reached this wooden structure, the bell tower would have been lost."
    - "From the moment we lose the war of the bell towers, we lose the cathedral, because it's a chain-reaction collapse." (S60 ✓)

  "acted aggressively to protect wooden supports" is **AP's paraphrase**, not his words.
- 3.19 Gallet (15 Apr evening): "Si les bourdons s'effondrent c'est l'effondrement de cette tour" and "On n'est pas sûr de pouvoir enrayer la propagation au beffroi nord" (S61 ✓; see correction 15 on "bourdons").
- 3.20 **What actually happened to the frame** (DRAC T1 ✓): flames "ponctuellement" reached the belfry frames, attacking the south-east corner from the level of the Galerie des chimères. A few beams were charred, but not weakened enough to threaten stability. One bell's headstock bearing ("mouton") needed monitoring. The stair to the second bell level was burnt and fragile (S09). Paris.fr 2025: "nombre de poutres… calcinées" (S20 ⚠). Two of the eight bells were heat-damaged (S17, S27 ✓).
- 3.21 The south tower's NE corner at the Galerie des chimères took heavy flame. Wind drove it onto the stones: "rubéfiées", sculpture "comme rabotée"; chimeras strapped, then removed (S09 ✓).
- 3.22 The small bells in the spire were lost with it (S122 ⚑; **no number**).

### 3f. What survived ✓
- 3.23 The great organ, under its own vault and stone-slab roof between the towers, was spared by fire and water but coated in lead dust. **All the stained glass survived**, including both 13th-century transept roses, north and south (« les deux roses des croisillons nord et sud, du XIIIe siècle », S09; the west rose was protected separately, so never just "both roses"). The north transept rose's stones were heat-reddened and flaking (S09).
- 3.24 The pre-fire spire scaffold survived because « il avait été exigé qu'il ne prenne pas appui sur la charpente et sur la flèche ». It sagged where the spire fell and from the heat, and got movement sensors (S09 ✓).

### 6c. Securing (2019–2021)
- 6.19 Six-month works: gables (N, S, W) consolidated; tarps on the high vaults; nets under the nave, choir and transept vaults; a floor on the side walls above the vaults; ~80% of the floor rubble cleared by robots with the LRMH lab, archaeologists and police forensics (S09 ✓). Centring status on 14 Oct 2019: choir and north nave done, south nave due end of October, crossing "à venir" (S09).
- 6.20 28 larch centring arches, each >8 t, by Lorraine carpenters, under all 28 flying buttresses, 2 Jul 2019–28 Feb 2020 (S12 ✓). Without the roof's weight, the buttresses' thrust was pushing the walls in.
