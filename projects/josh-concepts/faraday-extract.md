## (b) Title / thumbnail / packaging rules, with cited numbers

### Thumbnail rules — Faraday’s “12 lines”

1. Eye scans a thumbnail in about **0.02 seconds**; eye moves in a Z from top-left to bottom-right; put hook top-left, proof bottom-right.
2. Use **three elements at most**: face, text, one object. Three is the most the brain reads instantly.
3. Make **one element** clearly the main focus; everything else supports it.
4. Add a **twist the title doesn’t say**; a thumbnail that only repeats the title gives no reason to click.
5. Avoid **red, white, and black** as main colors; YouTube’s page uses them so you blend in.
6. Put your **most intense color on the twist**.
7. Break your niche’s usual look on **exactly one element**; break one = memorable; break all = unrecognizable.
8. Turn it **black and white** before publishing; if you can’t tell what matters most, contrast is too weak.
9. Show the **result the viewer wants as a picture**; eye reads thumbnail before title.
10. Light your face from **one side**; look for small triangle of shadow beside nose. Phones default flat = amateur.
11. **Pose for the thumbnail on set**; frames pulled from footage are usually blurry/badly lit/mid-word.
12. Make your face show **the feeling the title promises**.

### Thumbnail/packaging additional rules

- **3 promises** in every thumbnail/title: topic, format, feeling. Deliver all three starting in first **30 seconds**.
- If a thumbnail shows the **tool**, it shows the part nobody wants; write the result in one sentence first, draw that, shrink the tool.
- For **expert viewers**, show the **method** on screen (prompt, framework, named tool); beginner page wants the **result**.
- **Face labels**: if you have a credibility label, use it in first **three words** (“real doctor,” “real lawyer”).
- **Quote test**: if a viewer would repeat a line to a friend, test it as thumbnail text. “AI is all a scam” — **8.7M views**; top comment **10,700 likes**.
- **Banner blindness**: search “how to invest for beginners”; **9 of top 24** thumbnails show **$0 to $1m** and **£100 to £1m**. When every thumbnail looks like a thumbnail, stand out with **a moment** or **a screenshot**.
- **Thumbnail text placement**: in 3,594 thumbnails, **15–17%** put words in each other corner; only **7%** bottom-right where timestamp lands; faces sit upper two-thirds, **1%** in bottom row.
- **Count/clock**: MrBeast’s last **18** long titles: **13 of 18** name a count or clock. Rewrite obstacles with a count, distance, and clock.
- **Progress/rule**: Hot Ones uses **10 wings**, same order; biggest replay peak in second half in **14 of last 16** episodes.

## (c) Playbook / diagnosis steps visible or quoted

### Faraday’s YouTube diagnosis checklist (text; “got me 4,025,251,527 views”)

1. Read impressions, CTR, and watch time together. Low impressions + high CTR = potentially small audience; high impressions + low CTR = weak packaging; high CTR + low watch time = video didn’t deliver promise.
2. Check **who** YouTube is showing the video to; compare recommended videos with biggest hits/flops.
3. Separate **Browse** and **Suggested** CTR; Browse often higher; CTR falls as YouTube shows to strangers.
4. Check the **three promises**: topic, type of video, feeling; does video deliver all three?
5. Watch first **5 seconds**; immediately prove viewers clicked right video.
6. Compare **new vs returning** viewer retention. Only new viewers leave = wrong audience; both leave at same moment = video problem.
7. Diagnose retention drops: information overload, boring sections, predictable moments, lost curiosity, promises not delivered.
8. Decide: **recut** weak opening; **repackage** good video attracting wrong viewers; **rebuild** when idea and video don’t match.
9. **Save every flop**: thumbnail, title, retention graph, what went wrong.
