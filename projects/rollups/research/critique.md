**Five weakest lines + rewrites**

1. **“I spent the last few weeks studying three of the biggest roll-ups on the planet. Companies that bought their way to tens of billions of dollars.”**  
   **Rewrite:** “Three billion-dollar roll-ups use three contradictory playbooks. Copy the wrong one and you go broke.”  
   This puts the viewer’s risk in the first line instead of after three teases.

2. **“Not exciting software. Software for golf courses. For municipal water billing. For car dealerships.”**  
   **Rewrite:** “They buy the boring software that runs golf courses, water bills, and car dealers—where customers almost never leave.”  
   Tighter, more visual, and instantly states the moat.

3. **“In 2011 he took over a small logistics company with 175 million in revenue: XPO. He bought truck brokers, then much bigger companies, plugged them into one technology platform, and grew it into a giant.”**  
   **Rewrite:** “In 2011, he bought XPO for $175M in revenue, bolted on competitors, and later split it into three public companies.”  
   Cuts corporate chronology and leads with the payoff.

4. **“And he’s doing it again right now. In 2025 his new company QXO bought Beacon Roofing Supply for about 11 billion dollars, after a hostile, five-month fight.”**  
   **Rewrite:** “Right now he’s repeating it: a hostile five-month fight to buy Beacon Roofing for $11B.”  
   Same facts, less setup, stronger drama.

5. **“[IF YOU WANT HELP PICKING YOURS, AND STRUCTURING YOUR FIRST DEAL SO A BANK WILL FUND IT: LINK BELOW.]”**  
   **Rewrite:** “If you want the exact off-market outreach script, lender package, and no-cash-down structure, grab the checklist below.”  
   Specific mechanism beats vague promise.

**Likely drop-off points**
- **0:10–0:20:** If the hook stays abstract too long; “you could go broke” arrives too late.
