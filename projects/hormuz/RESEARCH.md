# The Strait That Controls a Fifth of the World's Oil: research and fact-check

Compiled 2026-10-12. The script is written from this pack, never from memory. Every spoken number needs a row here.
Source IDs (S01…) point to `SOURCES.md`, which has full URLs, publishers, dates and tiers.

**Confidence levels**
- **High**: a primary record or official statistic (EIA, IEA, IMO, UN Treaty Collection, CENTCOM, the Naval History and Heritage Command (NHHC), ICJ), or two independent strong outlets that agree.
- **Medium**: one strong outlet, or several secondary outlets.
- **Low**: contested, a single weak source, or paraphrased. Low rows can't be used in the title, the thumbnail, the cold open or a red annotation (playbook §1 and §7).

**Markers**
- ⏱ means time-sensitive. The figure moves week to week, so re-check it on the day of the voice-over (VO) lock and again on publish day, and put an "as of" date in the caption.
- ⚑ means it must be resolved or cut before the script locks.

**Access note (affects sourcing).** Reuters, AP and the FT block this research environment, so I couldn't read them directly. The IEA's site and Wikipedia's API were also blocked or rate-limited. Where a row rests on Reuters or AP, I used an official source (EIA, IMO, CENTCOM, USNI News), a syndicated copy, or the Reuters URL cited by a secondary source. Those rows say "Reuters, not read directly". Before publishing, someone with access should open every Reuters and AP URL in `SOURCES.md` §F and confirm the wording. I read IEA figures through search-engine extracts of iea.org pages, so they need a direct check of the page too.

**The headline framing.** "A fifth of the world's oil" is safe only as: *about 20% of global petroleum liquids consumption passed through the strait (EIA, 2024)*. Of **seaborne** oil trade, the share is **more than a quarter**, not a fifth. Don't swap the two (see §10).

## 0. The 2026 situation in one paragraph (⏱ as of 2026-10-12)

The US and Israel struck Iran on **28 Feb 2026** (Operation Epic Fury), and Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was killed. Iran's Revolutionary Guard (IRGC) then warned ships away and began attacking them, and later laid mines. Traffic through the strait collapsed. Oil flows averaged **4.9 mb/d in Q2 2026**, against **21.6 mb/d in Q4 2025** (EIA). There was a brief ceasefire in April. The US blockaded Iranian ports from **13 Apr to 18 Jun**. A US–Iran memorandum was signed on **17 Jun**, but the truce broke down after attacks on ships resumed on **7–8 Jul**, and the US reimposed its blockade in mid-July. Iran runs a permit-and-fee regime (the "Persian Gulf Strait Authority", set up **5 May**) and steers ships into its own waters. Saudi Arabia's bypass pipeline was attacked on **10 Sep**, which sent the daily Brent spot price to **$131 on 15 Sep** (EIA). The week to **5 Oct** brought at least 12 ship attacks, the most of any week in the war (Reuters, not read directly; Al Jazeera). As of **11 Oct**, diplomacy is stalled, a supertanker is reported mined on an "unauthorised route", and the EIA forecasts Brent at **$105/b in Q4 2026**. The strait is **not reopened**. Never say "reopened" or "closed for good". Say "largely shut since February, with traffic on Iran's terms".

## 8. Surprising, counter-intuitive facts (for the hooks and the 3:00 / 6:00 beats)

Each one carries its row reference above. ✅ = safe for the cold open, title or red annotation (tier 1–2, or two independent sources). ⚠ = body only.

1. ✅ **The shipping lanes aren't in Iran's waters. They run through Oman's.** The IMO lanes, set in 1968, lie entirely inside Omani territorial waters at the narrowest point. Iran's 2026 power play is literally to **move the lanes north into its own sea**. (S17, S20, S21)
2. ✅ **Iran signed the Law of the Sea treaty in 1982, then wrote a note at the bottom.** Its signing declaration says transit passage through straits is a right only for countries that ratify. Iran never did, and neither did the US. Both sides of the 2026 standoff are outside the treaty that is supposed to govern the strait. (S16)
3. ✅ **In the 1980s "Tanker War", Iraq, not Iran, attacked most of the ships**, about three in five. **And the deadliest single attack on a US warship in the Gulf came from Iraq**: USS Stark, 37 killed, 1987. (S41, S42; Stark ⚑ upgrade the source)
4. ✅ **America barely uses the strait for its own oil.** Only ~0.5 mb/d, **about 7%** of US crude imports, came through Hormuz in 2024. **84%** went to Asia. Yet the US Navy has policed it for 40 years. (S01, S57)
5. ✅ **It "closed" without a wall.** The EIA, March 2026: the strait "is not physically blocked". The threat of attack and the **cancellation of insurance** stopped the tankers. War-risk premiums went from ~0.1–0.25% to 3.5–7.5% of a ship's value **per trip**. (S05, S34)
6. ✅ **The forecasters assumed a month.** The EIA's first wartime outlook assumed a one-month disruption and Brent around $70 by year-end. Seven months on, it forecasts **$105** for Q4. (S05, S04, S02) ⏱
7. ✅ **The escape route became the target.** Saudi Arabia's 1,200 km bypass pipeline carried more than 5 mb/d to the Red Sea until drones hit it on 10 Sep. The oil price peak of the war (daily Brent $131) came **then**, six months in, not in the first week. (S02)
8. ✅ **Iran's own 1993 law demands prior permission for ships carrying "dangerous or noxious substances"** through its waters. Read literally, that could mean tankers. *(Our reading, so label it.)* (S67)
9. ⚠ **The smugglers never stopped.** While supertankers sat at anchor, up to 500 speedboats a day kept crossing to Khasab with washing machines one way and pistachios and sheep the other. (S22)
10. ✅ **It's not just oil: helium.** Qatar produced an estimated **~35% of the world's helium** in 2024 (USGS), extracted alongside its LNG. Ras Laffan's damage reaches MRI scanners and chip fabs. (S58 for the share; ⚠ for "offline" claims, Low)
11. ✅ **A fifth of the world's LNG, not just oil.** One country, Qatar, shipped nearly 20% of global LNG through the strait in 2025, and one attack on 18 Mar knocked out 17% of its export capacity for "up to five years". (S04)
12. ⚠ **Who won the oil war? Possibly the US and Russia.** A New York Times analysis found the biggest revenue gains from 28 Feb to 8 May went to the US (~+$50bn) and Russia (>+$15bn). (S63 → ⚑ trace to NYT before use)
