01 · Offer one-pager

What we sell and who it's for

Buy profitable businesses with little to no money down, using the 3C model.
C1

Credibility

Build a board of credible operators and a top-tier external team (lawyers, accountants) by offering founders' equity instead of cash. Banks fund you and sellers take your offers seriously.

C2

Capital

Vendor finance, vendor rollover and commercial lending. Two rules: free cash flow covers debt service, at a minimum 1.5 DSCR. Debt over dilution, so you keep control and the exit.

C3

Closing

Sourcing by cold outreach, offers by LOI/NBIO at 3–5× EBITDA, due diligence with a quality-of-earnings focus, financing, legals, post-closing.

Two ways in

  • The Owners Academy: private community, full course, every template and script, daily deal discussion.
  • 1:1 advisory: Josh works your strategy, structures and lender presentations with you. Equity or advisory roles considered case by case.

Bonuses on joining

  • Non-binding indicative offer (NBIO) template. A client was quoted about $7,000 for the same document.
  • A 1:1 coaching session with Jy Kingston (KingVets), who built his board in under 30 days.

Who it's for

  • Willing to put in 40–100 hours a week
  • Wants a big outcome: a roll-up exit, a legacy business, or flips
  • Ideal: has cash, an existing business or property to leverage
  • Not for: four-hour-work-week lifestyle seekers

Call to action: book a call directly with Josh (no setters). Short application form first.

05 · Objections

Objection handling in Josh's own words

"I don't have any money."

"How much money do you need to buy a company? Zero."

Answer with the mechanism, not reassurance. The seller is paid out of the business's own cash flow (vendor finance), or keeps a stake that fills the bank's equity gap (rollover / 60-40). Josh was unemployed with no savings on his first deal. 2:20:30 0:51:20

"Why would a seller ever agree to that?"

"There is something called the burden of ownership."

Retiring owners in traditional industries want out. Vendor finance can pay above market value (6–7× vs the usual 3–5×), closes in 2–4 weeks instead of 6–16 with a bank, and the seller can walk away and still get paid. Never say "vendor finance" to the seller; say "getting paid over time". 0:54:35 1:35:59

"Nobody will take me seriously. I have no track record."

"If you have no track record, we can't do much about that. So what can we do? We can steal credibility."

Build a board with founders' equity, at zero cost, found through LinkedIn and cold contact. Josh did it at 25 with no network; Jy did it in under 30 days. 0:35:21 0:36:56

"I'm too analytical / I need to learn more first."

"Learning to just shut up and take action is probably one of the best skills you can learn… You probably know too much."

Jy came from IT. Then use decide-at-60%: "There is never a perfect moment." 0:47:40 2:10:37

"I'm worried about taking on debt."

Debt means no dilution, so you keep control and keep the exit. It's more readily available than investors (months for one investor vs a bank set up to lend), and it's only safe inside the rule: free cash flow covers debt service at 1.5× or better. 1:02:18 0:59:28

"I can't afford the program right now."

"If you can't afford an upfront component, then we can explore some kind of financing arrangement. We can explore a pure equity arrangement… It is important though that there is a monetary exchange… because then there's commitment on both ends."

Josh offered this live. Decide as a team whether closers may offer payment plans or equity deals, and under what criteria. 2:34:43

"I'm in the US / UK. Does this work outside Australia?"

Elliot and Cam's examples are outside Australia. Josh: "We have a network of banks and private lenders in the US. A lot of our work revolves around helping you structure and present the deals." Josh can be a director only in Australia; elsewhere, an advisor or equity holder. 2:36:34 2:35:47

"Should I find a deal first, or build the board first?"

Either works. Board-first is the macro approach (an executive summary PDF plus handshake equity). Deal-first is project-based, the way Christian did it. Josh recommends board-first. 2:37:24

"I can do this myself from your free content."

Agree; Josh says so himself. The case for the Academy is speed and the community: "The model is easy… but to go through it on a day to day basis, to keep yourself motivated… there's so much value to just being able to ask someone." Then ask: "You've been watching for months. How many offers have you sent?" 2:26:43 2:19:06

06 · Discovery call

Questions for the call, built on Josh's own filters

Josh qualifies on time, commitment, goal size, and existing assets. Ask in this order, then use the pop quiz to see how deal-ready they are.

AreaAskListen for
GoalTen years from now, what does this look like: a big exit, a legacy business, a lifestyle business, or flipping?Jackpot or legacy is the best fit. Lifestyle-only is a soft no ("maybe this isn't for you").
SizeHow big do you want to go? What number would change your life?Under ~$10M: "maybe there's an easier model". Tens of millions or more: acquisitions.
TimeRealistically, how many hours a week can you give this from next Monday?40–60 minimum; 100 ideal.
AssetsDo you have cash, an existing business, or property you could leverage?Any of the three: "you could buy your first company in the next 30 days".
Action so farWhat have you actually done so far: outreach, board conversations, offers?Months of watching with no outreach means an energy problem, not a knowledge problem.
IndustryWhich industry, and why? Is it asset-rich enough for a bank to lend against?Don't let them stall here ("don't spend too long on that").
DecisionIf this is a fit, what would stop you starting this week?Surfaces the real objection. Answer from section 05.

Pop quiz: "How deal-ready are you?"

  • Minimum DSCR Josh recommends? 1.5
  • How much money do you need to buy a company? Zero
  • Best way to find deals? Cold outreach, with cold calling best of all
  • Best negotiation tactic? Walk away; second best is a time limit ("24 hours to accept")
  • Three items before making an offer? 3 years of financials, matching tax returns, revenue breakdown by FY

Studio VSL (~11 min)

Booking page / ads
[HOOK]
When I was 25, I was unemployed, with no savings and zero experience in business.

Then I bought a healthcare clinic. Within the first year I'd pulled a hundred and fifty thousand dollars out of it, and I'd built mid six figures in equity.

I didn't put a single dollar down.

My name's Josh Li. In the next few minutes I'm going to show you exactly how I did it, how people I work with are doing it right now, and how to tell whether you can do it too.

[B-ROLL: photo with the seller of Josh's first deal]

[THE TWO WRONG WAYS]
These days there are only two ways most people try to buy a business.

One: you save for a deposit. Years of grinding to scrape together ten, twenty percent. Then the bank still says no, or you lose the deal to private equity.

Two: you raise money from investors. Six months to land one cheque, and when you finally do, you've given away the equity, the control, and most of your exit.

There's a third way. It's how I bought my first business, and it's how my friend Jason bought a five-and-a-half-million-dollar radiology business with nothing down. Seven years later, his group sold for two hundred million.

I call it the 3C model: credibility, capital and closing.

[C1: CREDIBILITY]
Credibility is simply how seriously people take you. When I started, nobody had any reason to. I was a kid.

If you have no money and no track record, you borrow credibility. You build a board of directors: people with the experience and success you don't have yet. You don't pay them. You offer them founders' equity, a slice of what you're going to build.

I found every person on my board through LinkedIn and cold outreach. None of them were friends, family or old colleagues. Today the founder of the Australian Institute of Fitness is a shareholder in my company.

One of my students, Jy, came from IT. No business background, no finance experience. He built his board in under thirty days using only LinkedIn. He now has twelve deals in his pipeline.

[B-ROLL: leadership team slide, Jy's KingVets profile]

[C2: CAPITAL]
Now the money. Vendor finance means the seller lends you the money to buy their business. You pay them over time, out of the business's own profit.

Why would a seller do that? The burden of ownership. There's a generation of owners in traditional industries ready to retire. They don't want to wait sixteen weeks for a bank. They want out, and they want to be looked after.

Here's what that looks like. Take a business making two hundred and fifty thousand a year in profit. Divide by one point five, my safety buffer. That's a hundred and sixty-six thousand a year you can comfortably pay the seller. Over ten years, that's one point six six million: well above what most buyers would offer. The seller retires and gets paid every month. You take over a business that pays for itself from day one.

That's almost exactly how I closed my first deal. Seven years, four percent interest. I negotiated nine months with no payments while we took over, and no deposit on the lease.

[B-ROLL: 1.66M speed-run notepad]

[C3: CLOSING]
Closing is a process. Find deals with cold outreach: calls, emails, LinkedIn. Get three years of financials, the tax returns, and a revenue breakdown before you offer. Offer three to five times profit in a letter of intent. Do proper due diligence. Get the contract right with top-tier lawyers.

Saul, in our community, built his board and started cold calling. Soon after, he had an NDA signed with an electrical and air-con business doing just under four million a year, whose owner wants to retire.

My friend Cam closed two deals in twenty-three days. Both a hundred percent vendor finance.

[B-ROLL: Saul's Telegram message, Cam's "just closed two deals" message]

[THE ROLL-UP]
And here's why this beats real estate. Buy ten businesses like that one at four times profit. Grow them, combine them, and sell the group. Groups trade at eight, ten, fifteen times. That's the multiple arbitrage, and it's how most of the wealthiest people on the planet made their money.

[WHO THIS IS FOR]
This is not a four-hour-work-week model. If you want a lifestyle business, this isn't for you.

It's for people who can give this real hours every week, who want to build something big, and who are ready to act before they feel ready. Decide at sixty percent and figure it out as you go. I had four deals fall over before my first one closed.

If you already have cash, a business or property, this gets a lot easier and a lot faster.

[CALL TO ACTION]
If that's you, book a call with me below. Not with a setter or a sales team. With me.

There's a short form so I can understand your situation. On the call we'll work out your strategy, your first structure, and whether the Owners Academy or one-on-one advisory makes sense for you.

I run a healthcare group full time, so I only take on people who'll actually do the work.

If that's you, I'll see you on the call.
