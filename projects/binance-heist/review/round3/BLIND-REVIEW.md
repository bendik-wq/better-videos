## 6. Fact sanity

**Statements that need attribution, or are framed beyond the source:**

1. **4–5** "A recruiter posing as a Project Director contacted…" / "There were interviews…" This starts a new beat with no attribution. The script's own rule says each step carries "Binance says" or follows one *in the same beat*, and the last attribution was line 2 in the previous beat. Add "Binance says" to line 4.
2. **25, 32, 59** "The attackers had just learned **who** works the vault" / "a map of **who** works the vault", and **26** "You can't change who works for you". The source says *roles* were exposed, and the fact map says names were not reported as leaked. "Who" makes listeners hear identities. Say "which roles run the vault" or "what the wallet team looks like". Line 32 also drops the attribution.
3. **61** "B wasn't the target. B was the door." This states attacker intent, a form of mind-reading, as fact. Line 60 frames it ("On Binance's account"), but 61 lands as the narrator's assertion. Keep it, but consider "On that account, B wasn't the target…", or make sure 60–61 render as one attributed unit.
4. **88** "Nobody broke into the vault." Lines 76–77 just said the attackers deployed a malicious script that *reached the wallet signing service*. A careful listener hears a contradiction, and Binance's critics could read it as minimising the breach. "Nobody stole the key. They got the vault to sign" keeps the punch and stays true.
5. **91 vs 98–99.** The CZ post "User funds are safe!" is set up as irony, an amusement beat, yet 98–99 then show users were made whole. That frames him as wrong in a way the script's own facts undercut, which is the "misleading framing" risk in `script-patterns.md` §4.1. Either drop the ironic framing (e.g. "That same night, Binance's founder posted: 'User funds are safe!' In one sense, he'd turn out to be right.") or keep the quote neutral.
6. **66 → 67–69.** The ladder "Half a billion. One and a half billion. Now six point three." puts this theft in the North Korea series *before* the attributions arrive. Each sentence is sourced, but the order implies a settled link, which the script's own rule forbids. Consider putting "the F.B.I. says" before the ladder's final step, or ending the ladder on "Now six point three", with the TraderTraitor attribution as a separate beat.
7. **47** "its security tools didn't see this at all" goes beyond the quoted source ("not detected… at the time"). "Didn't flag it" is safer. **48** "Nothing crashed. Nothing looked wrong." is unsourced dramatisation. That's fine as color, but it is written as fact.
8. **⏱ breaking news.** 69 "As of the fourth of November, no one has been charged", 98 "almost all… by then", and 102 "more than three billion still sat" all move. The as-of dates are present; update them on publish day.

**Numbers that could be heard as contradictory or blurred:**

- **Three "halves."** 63 "more than half a billion" (Axie), 79 "even if 'most' means just over half" (drained in 20 min) and 104 "About half the money" (still held) can be heard as one figure, as in "half went in 20 minutes and half is still sitting there". Rephrase 79 as "even if 'most' means just over three billion".
- **"fifty" vs "fifty-seven".** 70 "fifty-seven North Korea-related attempts" and 87 "fifty of those slips" come 2 min apart. Listeners may merge them. Keep "fifty" (the payoff number) and say "dozens of" for the attempts, or show 57 on screen only.
- **79** "more than two and a half million dollars every second" rests on "about twenty minutes". If the real window is 21 minutes or more, "more than" fails ($3.15B ÷ 1,260 s ≈ $2.5M). "About two and a half million" is safer.
- **"Almost all replaced" (98) vs "about half still sitting there" (104) vs "By then, the money had left" (92).** These are consistent, but only if the viewer tracks that the replacement came from Binance's own funds. Line 100 does that work; keep it.
- **Two engineers, one blur.** 4 "a software engineer" (September) and 12 "an engineer on the team that runs its wallets" (June) are different people. First-time listeners may assume it's the same engineer twice, which muddies the "no 'wallet' in the title" anomaly at 31–32. Say "a different engineer" in 12, or "In June, it was someone else."

The date arithmetic checks out:

- 29 Oct 2026 is a Thursday.
- Saturday is 31 Oct.
- Five days later is 3 Nov.
- 6.3 ÷ 1.5 = 4.2, so "more than four times" holds.
- The Ethereum upgrade (Pectra, 2025) was "last year".

## 7. Verdict: **REVISE**

The craft is high: loop ledger, but/therefore chain, specifics, attribution discipline, and a genuinely great reveal at 8:52. But a cold viewer gets no 4-level moment for the first 5½ minutes, and the ending peaks below the middle. The critic gives 95 and the emotional pass 54/100, with all gates passing. Five changes, in order of impact:

1. **Put a real 4 in the first 3 minutes.**
   - **Cold open.** Spend a fragment of the 8:52 anomaly as a third "strange fact": "And after Binance shut withdrawals, the money kept leaving anyway." That makes the hook a 4 without spoiling the mechanism.
   - **The map (2:00).** Harden it with the report's own wording on screen (R23: "This is the line. Look at…"), plus a concrete stake: these are the few people whose machines can reach the signing service.
   - **The sponsor slot.** Move it to after that peak, about 2:35, so the read sits on a 4 instead of a 2.
2. **Stop spending reveals the viewer already has.**
   - Drop "posing as" from line 4.
   - Cut or compress *the company* (34–37) to one line.
   - Replace the false-suspense questions L3 ("careless or told to?") and L5 ("would it catch this one?") with questions the viewer can't answer yet. For example: "How do you get past the exact tool that caught you in June?" The answer is zero-day malware, a real reveal at 4:07.
3. **Make the ending the biggest moment, or end sooner.**
   - Cut the recap in 107.
   - Move the Socket fact (114) into the button as the stair-step ("thirty-five poisoned tests in one campaign alone"), or cut it.
   - Land 110–112 within 60 s of a concrete consequence, so the payoff is a fact plus an interpretation, not an interpretation alone.
   - For example, end Ch.5 on what Binance changed, if it says, or on the fact that take-home tests are still standard at every exchange.
   - The aim: *the answer* reads as a 5, or the button lands within 60 s of the vault.
4. **Fix the framing risks.**
   - 88 "Nobody broke into the vault": make it "Nobody stole the key" so it no longer contradicts 77.
   - 25/32/59: change "who works the vault" to "which roles run the vault".
   - 91: stop framing CZ's tweet as ironic when 98–99 show users were made whole.
   - 4: add the missing "Binance says".
   - Order the North Korea ladder (66–67) so attribution comes before the series.
5. **Tighten the two dry stretches.**
   - Merge *the ad* and *caught* (12–17) into about 20 s, and make June's engineer clearly a different person.
   - Collapse 67–70 into two sentences ("The F.B.I. says North Korea's TraderTraitor did this one too. North Korea denies it; as of …, no one's charged.").
   - Cut 94 "That's how the money left", which sounds like an ending.
   - Annotation-only fixes: retag P6 as a callback (it's paid 30 s after the plant, and again under 3 min later), giving 6 setups; and turn *aftermath* into a "but" with a real turn.
