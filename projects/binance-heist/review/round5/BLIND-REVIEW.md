## 6. Fact sanity

**Lines that state as fact what should be attributed, or hedge less than the source:**
- **Line 1.** "About six point three billion dollars" has no source. "By analysts' count" attaches only to "biggest". Say whose figure it is ("Analysts put it at about six point three billion…").
- **Lines 2–3 vs lines 14 and 22.** The hook says the way in "was a job interview". Ch.1 then says the attackers were already on a wallet-team laptop in June, through an online ad. An attentive viewer hears a contradiction: the first way in was an ad. Either the hook says "the way *to the money*", or *the map* says plainly that June got them a map but not a way in.
- **Line 22 and line 38 vs line 52.** Lines 22 and 38 treat the June and September attackers as the same people ("the attackers were already…", "had caught these attackers"). Line 52 then makes it conditional: "*if* June was the same operation, as Binance says". Pick one framing. The safe one is "Binance links June to the same attackers" at its first mention.
- **Line 26.** "That's why catching them didn't matter" is our inference, stated as fact. Binance did contain June. Soften it to "That's why, on Binance's account, catching them wasn't enough".
- **Line 51.** "Binance's own A.I. tool, used against it" repeats the claim without attribution and drops the "mostly ordinary phishing" hedge from line 47.
- **Line 57.** "That's what it can cost" implies the engineers might lose their jobs. The only source is that Binance declined to answer.
- **Line 65.** "Half a billion. One and a half billion. Now six point three." This places Binance on the North Korea ladder unconditionally. The "If the F.B.I. is right" is four lines earlier (line 61). Put the conditional back on the ladder.
- **Line 89.** "Very likely still held by North Korea" is attributed to Elliptic, but the denial (line 60) is three minutes back and only on a caption here. Consider a two-word spoken reminder ("North Korea denies it").
- **Line 77.** "As its report confirms" at 7:33, before line 94 says the report was "published on the third of November". The chronology is fine for a retrospective narrator, but the chapter clock (T+5 days) makes the report feel like new information arriving late.

**Numbers or claims a listener could hear as contradictory:**
- **$6.3B stolen, ">three billion still sat" (88) and "replaced almost all" (84).** Without a bridge, a viewer may hear that half the money is missing, or wonder where the other three billion went. One clause ("the rest has already moved on") would settle it.
- **"Five months" (13, 22, 52) vs "three months" (38).** Both are right (June → October, June → September), but they're heard close together and attached to the same "caught once" fact. Say "caught them in June" instead of giving a second interval.
- **"This one paid off" (92) vs "sitting there, in plain view" (90).** "Paid off" sounds like cashed out, but the money is parked and watched. Use "got away with it, so far" instead.
- **Line 98 "signing takes the key" vs line 99 "the keys never left".** This is consistent if you follow it, but it's the fifth mention of keys (8, 69, 98, 99, 102), and repetition invites confusion between *taking* a key and *using* it. Make the distinction once and explicitly at line 102 ("The key never left. It was used.").
- "Fifty slips" vs "dozens of attempts" (line 66) is handled well. Keep "dozens".

## 7. Verdict: **REVISE**

Critic 93, checklist 66 with all gates passed. Ship needs ≥80 on the checklist.

The writing, loop ledger and fact discipline are strong. On a cold read the problem is one thing: **no beat reaches 4 before 7:25, and no beat reaches 5 at all.** The writer's three early peaks (the map, the target, wallet team) are well built, but each reveal is smaller than its build-up: a list of roles, a job label, the more likely of two stated options.

### Top 5 changes
1. **Build one real peak between 2:00 and 5:00** (checks #2 and #10). Two ways to do it:
   - Make *wallet team* the peak. Lead with "they used one of Binance's own A.I. tools against it" as its own staged line. Then show where A and C sit on the June role chart, next to the signing machine. End on a human beat. Make it awe or surprise 4, hedge kept in the caption.
   - Or give *the map* a payoff as big as its build-up. Say concretely what the role list let them do ("they knew whose inbox was one step from the machine"), not just "the roles".
2. **Stop pre-paying the vault reveal** (lifts *the vault signs* toward a 5). Cut or blur line 73, "The script reached the wallet signing service." Use something like "The script went looking for one machine." so lines 100–102 are the first time the viewer learns the machine signed. Also cut two of the five "no key" mentions (69 and 98 are the likeliest). To earn a 5, attach a human consequence to the reveal, for example the customers locked out at that moment while the vault signed.
3. **Fix the hook vs June contradiction** (lines 2–3, 14, 22). June's way in was an ad. Either change the hook to "the way to the money was a job interview", or add one line in *the map* such as "June got them a map. It didn't get them to the money."
4. **Compress 5:11–7:00 and attach the hedges** (#8 and the fact list):
   - Cut line 57.
   - Shrink Axie and Bybit to the ladder line, with "If the F.B.I. is right" spoken on it.
   - Merge *the token* into *the script*.
   - Stage *who* (North Korea) as a deliberate hidden-origin beat, with a pause line, rather than four flat lines.
