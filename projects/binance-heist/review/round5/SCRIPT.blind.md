# The Job Interview That Cost $6.3 Billion: narration v5

**Structure:** the countdown (`script-patterns.md` §1.4). One laptop followed through five months, with an on-screen clock (T–5 MONTHS … T–0 … T+5 DAYS) on every chapter card. Runner-up: the investigation; it lost because the "who" is already named by the FBI, so the open question is "how", which a clock tells best.

**Ending choice (kept from v4):** the button lands within 60 s of the vault reveal. The money questions (who pays, where is it) come *before* the mechanism, inside the open "how did it leave?" loop, and the L1 answer (the permission-slip rhyme) is the second half of the vault beat. The biggest peak is the last peak, and the button follows it directly. No recap anywhere before the reveal (v4's "Binance has paid. A suspect has been named…" is cut).

**Peak plan (v5):** six beats at ≥ 4, two 5s, four peak feelings. *the map* (dread 4, ~1:46: the laptop Binance caught in June belonged to the wallet team, and the roles had already left) → *the target* (surprise 4, ~2:39: predict-then-reveal; they had the vault's list and went after someone with no "wallet" in the title) → *wallet team* (awe 4, ~4:30: B was the door, opened with help from Binance's own internal A.I. tool) → *the drain* (indignation 5, ~7:26: ten million dollars every four seconds; customers locked out of their own money for two days while the code kept taking) → *still held* (dread 4, ~8:39: three billion in plain view, the nuclear programme, "who gets the next folder?") → *the vault signs* (surprise 5, ~10:06: the script reached the machine you were told to keep in mind, and that machine signed fifty slips).

**Look:** night mode (style bible). Work light on a desk for everything human; fluorescent green-black for the inside of Binance's systems; red only for annotation.

**Length:** see the critic run in LOOP.md (150 wpm, VO only). The sponsor read adds 45–75 s on top.

**Re-hooks:** `[↺]` marks one; target one every 30–40 s.

**Sponsor:** one slot right after *the floor plan* (about 2:39 VO), marked `[SPONSOR SLOT — offer TBD]`. It sits between the first peak (*the map*) and the second (*the target*), with L3 ("they had the list; who would they go after?") held open across it. The return line is a predict-then-reveal on new information, because it is where skippers land.

**Fact rules:**
- Every fact is in SOURCES.md (IDs in the fact map at the bottom).
- ⏱ = everything. This is a breaking story; re-check every number on publish day. Every status that can change carries an "as of" date in the VO.
- Binance's account is a company claim: "Binance says", "according to Binance's report", "on Binance's account", "in Binance's telling". Every sentence that states a step of the attack carries it, or follows one in the same beat.
- North Korea is an F.B.I. attribution plus analysts' assessments, denied by North Korea. No one is charged. Never "North Korea stole". The attribution and the denial are spoken *before* the earlier thefts are lined up, and the ladder is introduced as conditional ("If the F.B.I. is right").
- The engineers are private people: only Binance's own role labels, no faces, no blame. The June engineer is never said to have made a mistake. Roles, never "who": the report says roles were exposed, not names.
- Engineer B's team: the report's label is "Software Engineer B", while A and C are labelled "Wallet …". The VO only ever says what the label says ("no 'wallet' in the title"), never that B was on or off a team.
- "Nobody stole the key" vs. the way in: the VO never says "stolen credentials". The attackers *used what they had taken from the wallet engineers' computers* (the deployment pass), which is not a wallet key (S18, S22).
- "The script reached the machine, and the machine signed fifty slips" joins two attributed accounts (Binance: the script reached the signing service, S20; Fireblocks and Blockaid: the hot-wallet key signed fifty authorisations, S21). Each half is spoken with its own source.
- The "permission slip" reading of the take-home test is our interpretation and is spoken as one ("Here's how we read it"), and the button keeps it a metaphor ("that kind of slip").
- `[ON SCREEN: …]` lines are picture notes for shot-design, not narration.

**Markup:** `*word*` stressed · `> ` slower line · `|` beat · `[↺]` re-hook. Beat annotations follow `docs/research/emotional-engine.md` §7. Setups use `P1…` (plant IDs) so they don't clash with source IDs `S01…`.

**Loops (open → close):** every beat that closes a loop opens the next one, until the payoff.
- **L1:** how does one coding test turn into the largest theft in crypto history? (the number → the vault signs; restated in *the interview* and *the vault signs*)

- **L5:** Binance's security caught these attackers once. How do you get past it a second time? (the folder → not detected)
- **L6:** one laptop inside Binance. Dead end, or a way to reach someone who can touch the money? (not detected → wallet team)
- **L7:** who plans a heist like that? (wallet team → who)

**Setups (plant → pay), six:**
