## 6. Fact sanity

**Lines that state as fact what should be attributed, or that overreach:**
- **Lines 25, 33 and 56: "The attackers had just learned which roles run the vault."** This is our reading of the report's "roles … exposed", stated flatly three times. Line 56 ("A chart of which roles run the vault") turns it into an object, and the on-screen chart is a recreation. Attribute at least the first one ("On Binance's account…"), or soften it to "which roles exist on the wallet team" (line 23 is accurate).
- **Line 24: "That chart is a list of the jobs that sit next to the machine that signs for the money."** An inference presented as fact.
- **Lines 6, 49 and 101: "the largest (crypto) theft in crypto history / on record"** are unattributed repeats. Only line 1 carries "by analysts' count". §4.2 says don't drop the attribution when a claim recurs. Line 6 is fine because it is a question; lines 49 and 101 should be "on record" plus a caption, or "by analysts' count".
- **Line 39: "a company whose security had caught this attack three months before."** "This attack" equates the June and September incidents. That link is Binance's claim (line 9 attributes it) but here it is unattributed. The same goes for line 59, "Five months of patience", which asserts one continuous operation.
- **Line 45: "In September, its tools didn't flag a thing."** This is broader than the quote it summarises ("not detected by the endpoint security protection tools at the time").
- **Line 84: "For customers, the founder's post held up."** Line 91: **"Binance has paid."** Both rest entirely on Binance's own 98% claim; say "Binance says it has paid."
- **Line 91: "A suspect has been named."** That implies a legal process; no one has been charged. "The F.B.I. has named a group" is safer.
- **Line 108: "If you've ever done a take-home test, you've signed one."** Inside the metaphor it's fine. Heard cold, it sounds like a factual claim that every take-home test grants malware access. Make the metaphor explicit ("…you've signed that kind of slip").
- **Line 94: "It points to a feature of Ethereum."** The theft crossed ten blockchains (line 75). Without a qualifier, viewers will hear that all $6.3B left via Ethereum permission slips. Say "some of it" or "on Ethereum and chains like it", if the sources support that.

**Numbers or claims a viewer could hear as contradictory:**
- **Line 2, "it didn't start with genius code", vs. lines 40–43, "custom malware, never seen before" / "zero-day".** A zero-day *is* what most viewers mean by genius code. The hook's misconception flip is undercut by the script's own evidence. Reframe it as "it didn't start with a break-in" or "it didn't start with hacking Binance".
- **Line 73, "stolen credentials", vs. line 99, "Nobody stole the key."** To a lay ear, credentials and keys are the same thing. Add one clause distinguishing a login from a wallet key, or keep "stolen" out of line 73.
- **Lines 75–76, "most of it went in about twenty minutes" / "the money moved", vs. line 88, "the funds that haven't moved".** "Moved" means two different things (out of Binance vs. out of the attackers' addresses). Say "funds that haven't moved since".
- **Line 83, "replaced almost all", vs. line 87, "more than three billion still sat in addresses linked to the theft".** Line 85 bridges this, but quickly. Some viewers will ask whose $3B it is. One clause would help: "the stolen coins themselves".
- **"Six point three" vs. "more than four times Bybit" (line 66).** 6.3 ÷ 1.5 = 4.2, so this is consistent. The VO "half a billion" (66) vs. "more than half a billion" (63) is also fine.
- Fine: "three months before" (June→Sept), "five months" (June→Oct), "dozens" (line 67) vs. "fifty" (line 98).

## 7. Verdict: **REVISE**

The craft is excellent. The critic gives 90, with 100 on hook, re-hooks, rhythm and specifics, and the payoff (line 99) is genuinely strong. But a cold viewer gets **three peaks in 11:43, none before 5:24**, plus three flat plateaus at 3. The emotional pass is **66/100**. Most of the writer's 4s are reframings, and a first-time viewer rates them as 3s.

**Top 5 changes**
1. **Land a real peak before 3:00.** *The map* or *not detected* needs a reversal that beats the viewer's guess, not one that confirms it. Option: make *not detected* turn on its strangest fact (the malware replaced a real program, so it *looked* like a trusted tool, which is June's trick from the inside). Or put the report's actual "roles exposed" line on screen and make the consequence concrete ("the clean win handed them the target list"). Without this, the critic's 0:00–5:30 gap stays.
2. **Cut repetition to break the flat plateaus.** Lines 30–31 and 35 retell lines 4–5; cut them so the post-sponsor return opens on new information (the fake company and CEO). Delete the recap in line 91. Trim the 72–73 attribution run so zero hour is the fastest stretch. Fix the loop gap at *who* by opening the next question in line 62 (e.g. "…more than once. So why do the defences keep failing?").
3. **Make the 5 airtight and add a second one.** At line 98, say *how* the vault signed: the script from line 74, on "the machine you were asked to keep in mind", produced the fifty slips (attributed). That pays P3 at the peak instead of at 7:40. For a second 5, let *the drain* hold its consequence (customers locked out, unknown whether they'd be repaid) before *binance pays* releases it. Or make the nuclear stakes in *still held* the peak instead of a hedge.
4. **Remove the two self-contradictions.** Line 2 "not genius code" vs. the zero-day in lines 40–43. Line 73 "stolen credentials" vs. line 99 "Nobody stole the key".
5. **Tighten attribution and vary the peak emotions.** Attribute the "roles run the vault" leap (lines 25, 33, 56), "Binance has paid" (91), "a suspect" (91) and the repeated "largest theft" (49, 101). Make line 108 explicitly metaphorical. Give the peaks different feelings: awe at 2.5M/s, indignation at "customers couldn't, the code could", and unease at the button. Today every peak is surprise or shock.
