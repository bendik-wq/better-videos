# Fascinations: the craft, 40 documentary examples and 16 explainer examples

A fascination is a line that **proves there is a specific answer worth having and hides what it
is**. Bencivenga's Boardroom colleague Mel Martin built record-breaking envelopes from them ("What
never to eat on an airplane", "Bills it's okay to pay late"; `sources.md` S3). On YouTube the
title, the thumbnail text, the hook's last line and every chapter end are fascinations.

> **Fact warning.** The examples below are *illustrations of the craft* on well-known topics.
> Every number, date and name was written from general knowledge and must pass
> `docs/library/fact-check-playbook.md` (two independent sources, tier rules) before any of it is
> used in a real episode. Items marked `[verify]` are the ones most likely to need adjustment.
> **Claim audit (2026-11-05).** Six titles in the first draft claimed more than the facts allow
> (#2, #8, #27, #31, #34, #40). Each is rewritten and keeps a note saying what was wrong, as a
> calibration lesson for critics: a title fails G1/G2 not only for a false fact but for a false
> *implication* (a causal link, a total, a stale value).

---

## 1. Anatomy of a fascination

| Part | What it does | Example ("What never to eat on an airplane") |
|---|---|---|
| **Itch** | Touches a fear, desire or a gap in something already known | Everyone flies; everyone eats on planes |
| **Hidden payload** | There is one exact answer, and you don't have it | "What" = a specific food |
| **Believability cue** | A reason to think the answer is real, not hype | Plain, deadpan wording; Boardroom added page numbers |
| **No give-away** | The line can't be answered by guessing | You can't guess which food |

**The "yeah, sure" test** (S16 Bullet 3): read it as a cynic. If you think "yeah, sure", add a proof cue
(number, date, named source) or cut the claim down.

**Reveal level** (general bullet craft from S5, not Bencivenga-specific):
- **Blind**: teases the outcome, hides everything. Strongest curiosity, weakest believability. Use
  only when the thumbnail carries the proof.
- **Half-blind**: gives part of the answer, holds back the key. Best default for titles.
- **Give-away**: delivers a real nugget. Use in the hook to earn trust ("the cloud was invisible,
  and heavier than air"), then open a bigger loop.

## 2. Twelve formulas for documentary titles

| # | Formula | Shape | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Reveal the problem, hide the cause** | Why [Thing] [Did Impossible Thing] | Urgent problem + gap |
| F2 | **Counter-intuitive pair** | [Fact A]. [Fact B that shouldn't be true too] | Two facts that collide demand a mechanism |
| F3 | **Odd exact number + odd unit** | The [N]-[Unit] [Thing] That [Consequence] | Specificity is proof |
| F4 | **What [insider] knew** | What [Person/Group] Knew Before [Event] | Exposé; implies a record |
| F5 | **The overlooked person** | The [Role] Who [Prevented/Caused] [Huge Event] | Unique promise; human stake |
| F6 | **The mundane cause** | The [Tiny Thing] That [Huge Consequence] | Scale gap; proof via object |
| F7 | **The authority's odd act** | Why [Authority] [Did Absurd Thing] | The authority is the proof (cf. "When Doctors Have Headaches") |
| F8 | **The myth reopened** | [Belief] Is Wrong. Here's the [Record] | Existing belief + proof promised |
| F9 | **The named mechanism** | How [Process] [Verb] [Object] | Promise of understanding |
| F10 | **The warning** | The Warning [Group] Ignored | Fear of loss; implies a document |
| F11 | **Could it happen again** | The [Event] That Could Happen [Again/Tomorrow] | Moves history into the viewer's future (only if sourced) |
| F12 | **The single object** | The [Object] That [Holds the Whole Story] | Mystery object; perfect for animation |

## 3b. Sixteen animated-explainer examples (the ep10 format)

Ep10 is an animated explainer, so these cover the lanes the documentary set above doesn't:
science "what if", scale, biology, "how X works", and comedic history. Each entry adds two lines:
**MAINSPRING device** (how our paper, spring and counter-wheel look carries it, per
`docs/library/animation-study/README.md` §5) and **Shape check** (whose signature the obvious
version of this title would copy, and what we did instead; see §6). The fact warning above applies
in full: every number here is from general knowledge and marked `[verify]` until the research pack
confirms it.

Explainer hooks differ from history hooks in one way: there is often no date and place to open
on, so slot 1 ("confirm the click") is a **drawable claim** and slot 2's proof is usually a
**demonstration or computed comparison** the viewer can check, not a document.

## 5. Thumbnail text fascinations

The thumbnail text is a 1–3-word fascination that **complements** the title. Five patterns:

| Pattern | Title | Thumbnail text |
|---|---|---|
| The proof number | *The Sound Heard 4,800 Kilometres Away* | **4,800 KM** (repeats a number only when the image adds the picture of it; otherwise use another fact) |
| The negation | *80 Million Trees Fell. No One Found a Crater.* | **NO CRATER** |
| The verdict | *The Emu War* title | **THE EMUS WON** |
| The overlooked detail | *The Key That Could Have Saved the Titanic* | **TAKEN OFF** |
| The answer to a different question | *Why a Lake Killed 1,746 People in One Night* | **NO ONE WOKE** |

Never put a hype word on a thumbnail; never repeat the title verbatim; keep the house typography
(`packaging` Step 3).

## 6. Originality: make a shape ours before we use it

Formulas are free; **signatures** aren't. A shape becomes a signature when one channel uses it
as a series system (in its titles, its tag, its thumbnail grammar or its voice). The full ban list
is in `docs/library/animation-study/README.md` §6. Run this check on every title in the long list.

**The four-question shape test** (any "yes" → rewrite or drop):
1. **Series tag?** Does the title use a tag a channel repeats across its catalogue ("What If…" as
   an opener, "(Minute by Minute)", "– Size Comparison", "Can you solve the…", "(Part N)",
   "TIMELAPSE OF…", "How a [machine] Works")?
2. **Two of three?** Does the package match another channel on *two* of: topic lane, title
   formula, thumbnail grammar? (One match is normal; two reads as a copy.)
3. **Swap test:** put the channel's name after the title. Would a regular viewer believe it's
   theirs? Then it's theirs.
4. **Voice test:** would the hook read as theirs if you removed the visuals (their catchphrases,
   their transitions, their jokes)? See the verbatim ban list (§6.2 of the study).

**Making a crowded shape MAINSPRING's:**
- **Turn "what if" into "what is"**: our subject is the real, measured version (E1, E2).
- **Put the mechanism's moving part in the title**: a clock, a counter, a tape, a ledger, a spring
  (E5, E11, E16). Those are our metaphors (`animation-study` §5), so they read as ours.
- **Put the check in the title**: a number the video computes on screen, so the title is proof,
  not a tag (E4, E5).
- **The question stack must be ours too.** The hook's closing questions are a common device, but
  the triad repeated word for word is LEMMiNO's habit; keep ours to 2–3 questions that become
  chapter titles, and don't repeat them verbatim later.
- **One formula per season** (`packaging` Step 2): pick two or three of F1–F12 as MAINSPRING's
  house shapes for a season (suggested: F2 counter-intuitive pair, F3 number + odd unit, F12 the
  single object) so repetition becomes recognition of *us*.
