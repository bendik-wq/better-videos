# Bencivenga copy for documentaries and explainers

Gary Bencivenga was the newsletter industry's "control beater" for decades: the writer brought in
when a promotion had to beat the best ad already running. His method is not about clever lines. It
is about **what the copy must contain** so a skeptical reader keeps reading and believes. For us the
"sale" is a click, then a minute of watch time, then the next minute.

Read in this order:

1. `references/principles.md`: the method, each rule mapped to a YouTube asset.
2. `references/fascinations.md`: bullet craft, 40 documentary and 16 animated-explainer worked
   examples (science, scale, biology, how-it-happens, comedic history), and the originality check.
3. `references/checklist.md`: the 0–10 rubric every critic uses, how it combines with
   `packaging` and `retention-script`, and the evidence status of every threshold.
4. `references/worked-example.md`: one episode packaged end to end. **Copy its format.**
5. `references/youtube-mechanics.md`: news peg, mobile truncation, search vs browse, Test &
   Compare, Shorts, localisation.
6. `references/body-copy.md`: narration for an animated explainer's body.
7. `references/sources.md`: where each idea comes from, with evidence labels.

**Evidence labels.** [primary] = Bencivenga's own words; [student]/[secondary] = others' accounts;
[observed]/[data] = our measurements; **[house] = our adaptation, untested on this channel**.
Every time box and threshold below is [house] unless it says otherwise (`checklist.md` §10).

## The one rule

> **"Never make your claim bigger than your proof."** And join claim and proof "at the hip" in the
> headline. (Bencivenga, *Marketing Bullets* 3 and 7, `sources.md` S16 [primary].)

His evidence: Caples' test of *When Doctors Have Headaches, What Do They Do?* against *Tension
Headache?*: the doctors are the proof, and that headline won by 71% (S16 B7). On YouTube an
unbacked claim is punished twice: the viewer leaves, and Test & Compare, which picks winners on
watch-time share, picks the other title (`sources.md` Y1). So the strongest legal title is **the
biggest claim the video can prove by 0:30 and settle by the end.** Raise the proof before you
raise the claim.

## The Persuasion Equation, in YouTube terms

**Urgent Problem + Unique Promise + Unquestionable Proof + User-Friendly Proposition =
Persuasion** (his site, S1 [primary]). Each part answers one objection: no interest, no
difference, no belief, no decision.

| Part | Viewer's objection | On YouTube it's… | Lives in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urgent problem | "Why should I care?" | An itch: a gap the viewer already half-feels, or a stake that touches them | Title + thumbnail + first line |
| Unique promise | "I've seen this already." | The angle no other video has: the hidden mechanism, the record, the overlooked person | Title + 0:00–0:30 |
| Unquestionable proof | "Yeah, sure." | A primary record, a demonstration, a computed comparison, shown on screen | 0:05–1:00, then every chapter |
| User-friendly proposition | "Not now." | The contract: what you'll understand, and that it's worth the minutes | End of hook; each chapter card |

**Scoring, his way (S1):** 25 per part, and **subtract 25 for any part that isn't as strong as it
can be**: weak counts the same as missing. 100 → proceed. 75 → fix the named weak part. ≤ 50 →
back to `packaging` Step 1. Details in `checklist.md` §6; a filled example in
`worked-example.md` §1.

## Workflow 0: the peg (news first, else a famous event)

Run before Workflow A (`youtube-mechanics.md` §1). Look for news from the last 14 days with a
named event, a primary record and an animatable mechanism that we can ship while it's news; use
it only if it makes the problem *urgent*. If none qualifies, pick a famous event or object (a
name everyone knows) and a door nobody uses. Most often: an evergreen title with the timely stake
in the hook and one chapter.

## Workflow A: a title (and its thumbnail text)

1. **Find the proof first.** List the 3 most surprising *provable* facts in the research pack:
   a number, a document, a demonstration, a named person. The title is built on one of them.
2. **Name the itch.** "The viewer already wonders / fears / assumes ___."
3. **Write 25+ fascinations** with the formulas in `fascinations.md` §2 (he wrote 25 headlines
   before choosing, S16 B28). Explainers: start from §3b's lanes.
4. **"Yeah, sure" test on each** (S16 B3). Read it as a cynic. If the reaction is "yeah, sure",
   add proof *into the title* (a number, a date, a named authority) or lower the claim. Test the
   *implication* too: a causal link or a total the facts don't support fails G1
   (`fascinations.md` claim audit: #2, #8, #27, #31, #34, #40).
5. **Specificity pass.** Replace every adjective with a noun or number from the research pack.
6. **Shape test** (`fascinations.md` §6, `checklist.md` §9): no other channel's series tag or
   two-of-three match; make the shape MAINSPRING's (a clock, a counter, a ledger, a check the video
   computes).
7. **Mobile and surface pass** (`youtube-mechanics.md` §2–3): the itch and proof cue in the first
   ~30 characters; decide whether it's a search title (the typed noun early) or a browse title
   (the itch first). No puns or jokes in the title (S16 B27), even in the comedic lane.
8. **Pair with thumbnail text** (≤ 3 words, house rule): the title asks, the thumbnail shows the
   proof or the stake. Never the same words. Prefer numbers or symbols for videos that will be
   localised.
9. **Score**: `packaging` Step 2 (P) and `checklist.md` §2 (B). Keep P ≥ 7 and B ≥ 7; send the
   top 5 by P + B to `packaging`. The A/B pair changes **one** variable.

## Workflow B: the first 30 seconds (≈ 70–80 words at 150 wpm)

Bencivenga's ads confirm the headline's promise at once and lay down proof before the reader's
guard goes up. Five slots [house time boxes], which sit inside `retention-script`'s 0:00–1:00
anatomy:

| Slot | Time | Job | Source idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Confirm the click | 0:00–0:05 | The thing the title/thumbnail promised, in the first frame. History: date + place + one action. Explainer: a drawable claim | Keep the headline's promise in the first line |
| 2. The proof object | 0:05–0:12 | One record, demonstration or computed comparison on screen with a source card | Join claim and proof (S16 B3) |
| 3. The pre-emptive objection | 0:12–0:18 | Voice the viewer's doubt, then answer it with the object | Candor (S16 B3); acknowledge disbelief (S4, unverified) |
| 4. The twist / unique promise | 0:18–0:25 | The fact that makes this story different from the one they know | Unique promise; predictability kills curiosity (S16 B23) |
| 5. The contract | 0:25–0:32 | 2–3 questions the chapters will answer, or one stake | User-friendly proposition |

Template (adapt, never paste):

> [Date. Place. One concrete action.] *or* [A drawable claim, stated plainly.]
> [The proof object, named, on screen: "This is the logbook of…" / the sum, assembling]
> [Objection: "It sounds like a legend. Here's the record."]
> [Twist: "But the strange part isn't X. It's Y."]
> [Contract: "Why did ___? And what happens if ___?"]

Rules: the first line contains **a noun you can draw**; no more than 2 statistics the viewer must
remember (a single number ladder counts as one; dates don't count), each on screen with its
source; no greeting, "in this video" or hype words; the hook makes the title true by 0:30. Score
with `checklist.md` §3 (≥ 8) **and** `retention-script` §3 (≥ 20/25). Worked: `worked-example.md`
§5.

## Workflow C: chapter cliffhangers and re-hooks

A chapter end is **a fascination spoken aloud**: it shows something is coming and hides what.
Shapes (worked lines in `fascinations.md` §4, including explainer and comedic ones): the withheld
detail; the wrong assumption flagged; the clock; the person about to enter; the counter-intuitive
pair; the prediction test; the missing step; the scale jump.

Rules: pay each one fast (full marks within 90 s, at most within a chapter loop's 2–4 min;
`checklist.md` §4) [house]; log it in the `retention-script` open-loop ledger with its payoff
time; never the same shape twice in a row. The **sponsor return line** is the most-replayed line
after the hook in 9 of our autopsied videos (`video-autopsies/README.md` §B1) [observed]: write it
as the strongest fascination in the script. Worked ledger: `worked-example.md` §6.

## Workflow D: proving claims on screen (the believability toolkit)

Bencivenga's own list of proof elements is in S16 B29 (case histories, endorsements, testimonials,
reasons why, outcomes, expertise, candor) [primary]. The longer checklist below adds items from
the unattributed "toolbox" notes (S4, **provenance weak**); those are marked † and are good craft,
not his documented rule. Every claim in a title, thumbnail or hook needs proof on screen; aim for
two elements [house].

| Proof element | Animated version |
|---|---|
| **Demonstration** † | Animate the mechanism at true scale or in true time: the gas flowing downhill, the fuel sum line by line |
| **Specifics** (B14) | Odd, exact numbers with units, shown as figures; names, times, places |
| **Reason why** (B9–B10) | Say *because*: every surprising fact gets its cause in the same breath |
| **Explain the mechanism** † | A 3–5 step diagram, cause → process → effect, readable in 2 s per step (`body-copy.md` §1) |
| **Believable source** (B7, B29) | A source card: who, where, when. The most authoritative source, not the most famous |
| **The document** | A redrawn facsimile of the record with the key line highlighted; credit the original |
| **The exposé** † | The version everyone believes, then the record that contradicts it |
| **Acknowledge disbelief** † | Voice the doubt right before the proof |
| **Candor** (B3) | Say what's unknown or disputed. Admitting a limit makes the rest more believable |
| **Testimonial / witness** (B29) | A named witness on a quote card, with the date |
| **Computed comparison** | A number turned into a picture the viewer can check, with the sum on screen |
| **Our method** (B29) | "Here's how we know": the channel's visible research is a proof element in itself |

**Placement:** strongest proof inside the first 60 s; one proof beat per chapter (the winners'
lesson, `video-autopsies/README.md` §B12) [observed]; the biggest proof saved for the climax.

## Workflow E: description, chapters, end line

- **First two lines**: the promise as a specific fact + the proof source, with the searched noun
  in line 1 (`youtube-mechanics.md` §3).
- **8–12 chapter names**, at least half fascinations, not labels.
- **Sources** listed: it's proof, and it costs nothing.
- **End line**: callback to the thumbnail or a widened stake; never a recap.

Worked: `worked-example.md` §8.

## Workflow F: the body of an animated explainer

`body-copy.md`: state the outcome, then one step per line and one picture per step, each with its
*because*; open sections with **plain statement, then twist** (S10 rhythm, I = B + C in two
sentences); **one governing metaphor built from our kit** (counter wheels, springs, ledgers,
stamps), where objects act and real people appear only as their records; **levity from facts,
cuts and props**, at most one beat per ~60–90 s, never in a title, never at victims' expense.

## Workflow G: Shorts and localisation

`youtube-mechanics.md` §5–6. A Short is its own copy: claim and picture in frame 1, proof by 0:03,
gap paid inside the Short, loop line back to frame 1. Localised titles translate the fascination,
not the words, pass a native speaker's "yeah, sure" test, and recheck length.

## Adapting a direct-response method honestly

- **No false urgency, no fake scarcity.** "User-friendly proposition" means easy to start
  watching, not "watch now or else".
- **Fear of loss only when the stake is real and sourced** (S16 B13 shows why it's powerful).
- **Curiosity must be paid.** A blind title the video doesn't resolve is a lie with good grammar.
- **Hype words, exclamation marks and caps-lock adjectives are banned** (S16 B3: they add "the
  aroma of hype").
- **Never copy a creator's wording, characters, series tags or style signature.** Learn the shape;
  make it ours (`fascinations.md` §6; full ban list in `docs/library/animation-study/README.md`
  §6).

## Output format

Return the blocks of `references/worked-example.md`, in its order, filling only the ones asked
for:

0. Peg check (news / famous name / door / decision).
1. Persuasion Equation table, binary scoring, /100, with the fix for any weak part.
2. The candidates (titles, hooks or cliffhangers), each with formula, character count, P and B
   scores and gate failures.
3. The top 5 with sub-scores, the mobile front-load stub and search/browse tag, and one critic
   block (`checklist.md` §8) for the top pick.
4. The proof list: each claim, its proof element and its source or `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.
5. The hook as a timed table, with both hook scores and a fallback line for any claim still
   `[verify]`.
6. The cliffhanger ledger with payoff times.
7. Description and chapters; Shorts cut and localised titles if asked.
8. The A/B pair (one variable) and what to log for calibration (`checklist.md` §10).
